# Quantum Entanglement

## Overview
Quantum entanglement is a fundamental phenomenon in quantum physics where particles become interconnected in such a way that the state of one particle instantly influences the state of another, regardless of the distance separating them. This connection holds even when particles are far apart, challenging classical notions of locality.

## Explanation by Scientists
Quantum entanglement has been studied and explained by prominent scientists like John Preskill, the Richard P. Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics and director of the Institute for Quantum Information and Matter. Preskill emphasizes the significance of entanglement in advancing quantum technologies and its role as a key theme in 21st-century science.

## Unbreakable Correlation
Entangled particles are created using special crystals, after which they are sent to different locations. When measured, each particle is in a superposition state (e.g., spin up or down). Measuring one instantly determines the state of its distant counterpart, showcasing an unbreakable correlation between them.

## Relativity and Communication
Contrary to some interpretations suggesting faster-than-light communication ("spooky action"), experiments confirm that entanglement does not violate Einstein's relativity. There is no actual communication; instead, particles exhibit correlated states without transmitting information faster than light.

## Networks of Entanglement
Entanglement can extend beyond two particles, involving hundreds or millions, forming unified systems. Caltech scientists study these many-body entangled systems to understand fundamental physics and develop quantum technologies.

## Proof of Existence
The reality of quantum entanglement is supported by experimental evidence, as detailed in videos like "Collecting Evidence for Quantum Entanglement | Einstein's Quantum Riddle" and "Untangling Quantum Entanglement." These works validate the phenomenon through rigorous testing and theoretical frameworks.

## Conclusion
Quantum entanglement remains a cornerstone of quantum physics, challenging classical intuitions while providing foundational insights into future technologies. Its study continues to evolve, promising significant advancements in our understanding of the universe.
