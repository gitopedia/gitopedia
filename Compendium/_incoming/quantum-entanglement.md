# Quantum Entanglement

## Overview
Quantum entanglement is a fundamental phenomenon in quantum physics where particles become interconnected such that the state of one particle instantaneously influences the state of another, regardless of the distance separating them. This intriguing connection persists even when entangled particles are far apart, challenging classical notions of locality.

## History
The concept of entanglement was met with skepticism by Albert Einstein, who referred to it as "spooky action at a distance." He proposed local hidden variable theories to explain particle correlations without faster-than-light communication. However, John Bell's 1960s theoretical work provided a framework to test these ideas. Experiments conducted by John Clauser and others in the 1970s confirmed that quantum mechanics aligns with experimental results, ruling out local hidden-variable theories.

## Key Concepts
- **Unbreakable Correlation**: Entangled particles exhibit correlations in their properties, such as spin direction. Measuring one particle's property instantly determines the corresponding property of its entangled partner.
- **Many-Body Systems**: Networks of entanglement can involve numerous particles acting as a unified system, akin to a flock of birds.

## Misconceptions
A common misconception is that entanglement enables faster-than-light communication. While entangled particles are correlated, no information is transmitted faster than light, preserving the principles of relativity.

## Proof and Applications
Experiments have repeatedly confirmed the reality of quantum entanglement. This phenomenon is crucial for emerging quantum technologies, with predictions suggesting it will play a pivotal role in 21st-century science.
