# Sustainable Energy Policy and Regulation

Sustainable energy is a critical aspect of addressing climate change and ensuring access to a reliable, affordable source of power for all people worldwide. This article will provide an overview of sustainable energy policy and regulation, including their history, current status, and future prospects. 

## Overview

Energy policy refers to the government's plan or strategy in implementing policies that aim to improve the production, supply, distribution, use, and conservation of energy resources. The primary goal is to create a balance between economic growth, social development, and environmental protection by ensuring sustainable sources of energy are utilized efficiently while minimizing adverse impacts on the environment and human health.

Regulation refers to the rules or guidelines established by governments to manage or control specific activities within an economy. In this context, regulations related to renewable energies aim to ensure that these resources provide a significant share of global electricity production as part of a clean energy transition strategy. 

## History and Background

The concept of sustainable energy emerged in the late 20th century when concerns about climate change began shaping global energy policies. Governments started promoting the use of renewable sources such as solar, wind, geothermal, hydroelectric, and biogas to reduce greenhouse gas emissions while mitigating environmental degradation associated with conventional energy sources like coal, oil, and natural gas.

## Current Status

Today's sustainable energy policy landscape is characterized by government efforts to encourage the adoption of clean technologies in various sectors such as transportation, buildings, agriculture, and industry. These policies are designed through regulatory frameworks that impose or incentivize specific actions for industries, businesses, and consumers. 

For instance:
- Renewable Portfolio Standards (RPS) require utilities to generate a certain percentage of their electricity from renewable sources; typically this is around 15% in many countries.
- Feed-in tariffs offer guaranteed prices or rates for energy generated by solar, wind, hydroelectric, and other sustainable technologies. This provides incentives for investors, entrepreneurs, and researchers to invest in these clean energy sectors.
- Carbon pricing mechanisms like carbon taxes or emissions trading systems help reduce greenhouse gas emissions through market-based instruments such as emission permits that can be traded amongst industries.

## Future Prospects

The future of sustainable energy policy and regulation is expected to focus on achieving higher global renewable energy penetration, enhancing energy efficiency, and fostering a circular economy by reusing and recycling materials in the production process. 

Efforts are also being made towards further research and development of new technologies like advanced nuclear fission, fusion, hydropower, geothermal energy, and innovative storage options for intermittent renewables such as batteries or compressed air energy storage (CAES). By 2050, it is anticipated that renewable energies will provide the majority of global electricity generation.
