# Schrödinger's Cat

## Overview
Schrödinger's Cat is a thought experiment introduced by physicist Erwin Schrödinger in 1935. It illustrates quantum superposition, where a cat in a box is considered both alive and dead until observed.

## History
The experiment was devised to highlight the paradoxical nature of applying quantum mechanics to macroscopic objects. Schrödinger used it to question the Copenhagen Interpretation, which states particles lack definite properties until measured.

## Key Concepts
- **Quantum Superposition**: The cat exists in a superposition of states (alive/dead) until observed.
- **Paradox**: Challenges classical notions of reality by suggesting multiple simultaneous states.

## Interpretations
The Copenhagen Interpretation suggests particles don't have defined properties until measured. Schrödinger's Cat challenges this by implying macroscopic objects can exhibit quantum phenomena, questioning the boundary between microscopic and macroscopic worlds.

## Reactions and Implications
Originally intended to critique quantum mechanics' application to everyday objects, it has sparked discussions on observation's role in reality and influenced various fields like philosophy and science fiction.

## Legacy
The thought experiment is widely referenced in popular culture and remains a tool for explaining quantum mechanics. It continues to inspire debates on the foundations of quantum theory.
