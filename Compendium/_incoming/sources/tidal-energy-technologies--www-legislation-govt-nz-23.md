The provided text outlines the **Offshore Renewable Energy Act 2024** and its amendments to other legislation, focusing on the regulation of offshore renewable energy (ORE) infrastructure. Below is a structured summary of the key provisions and their implications:

---

### **1. Core Provisions of the Offshore Renewable Energy Act 2024**
#### **A. Marine and Resource Consents**
- **Permit Requirements**: 
  - Applications for marine or resource consents for ORE activities (e.g., wind farms, tidal energy) must be submitted by the **holder of a current feasibility or commercial permit** under this Act. 
  - The applicant must provide details of the relevant permit in the application.
- **Automatic Cancellation**: 
  - A marine/resource consent is **automatically revoked** if the permit holder loses their commercial permit (via expiry, revocation, or surrender).

#### **B. Decommissioning Plans**
- **Mandatory Decommissioning Plans**:
  - For decommissioning ORE infrastructure, applicants must submit an **accepted decommissioning plan** that aligns with environmental and safety standards.
  - The plan must:
    - Identify the infrastructure to be decommissioned.
    - Detail the decommissioning process.
    - Include any revisions to the original plan (if applicable).
- **Environmental Protection Authority (EPA) Role**:
  - The EPA assesses decommissioning plans against regulatory criteria. If approved, the plan becomes the **accepted decommissioning plan**.
  - The EPA must provide **written reasons** if a plan is rejected.
- **Public Consultation**:
  - Regulations require public consultation for decommissioning plans, unless the EPA determines that the revised plan’s environmental impact is **no different** from the original.

#### **C. Transitional Provisions**
- **Stay of Applications**: 
  - Applications for marine/resource consents made **before the Act’s commencement** are **paused** until the applicant holds a current feasibility or commercial permit under the Act.

---

### **2. Amendments to Other Legislation**
#### **A. Exclusive Economic Zone and Continental Shelf (Environmental Effects) Act 2012**
- **Inserted Sections**:
  - **Section 38**: Requires applicants for marine consents to be **current permit holders** under the Offshore Renewable Energy Act.
  - **Sections 100E–100H**: Integrate decommissioning plan requirements into the Environmental Effects Act, aligning with the 2024 Act.
- **Decommissioning Plan Submission**:
  - Owners/operators of ORE infrastructure can submit decommissioning plans to the EPA for acceptance, with criteria defined by regulations.

#### **B. Resource Management Act 1991**
- **Inserted Part 8**: 
  - Similar to the Environmental Effects Act, it requires applicants for resource consents to hold current permits under the Offshore Renewable Energy Act.
  - Applications made before the Act’s commencement are **paused** until permit requirements are met.

---

### **3. Key Implications**
- **Regulatory Integration**: 
  - The 2024 Act harmonizes ORE activities with existing environmental and resource management frameworks, ensuring compliance with decommissioning, permitting, and public consultation standards.
- **Permit Dependency**: 
  - All marine/resource consents are contingent on holding a valid commercial permit under the 2024 Act, reinforcing oversight of ORE projects.
- **Decommissioning Accountability**: 
  - Operators must submit detailed, approved decommissioning plans to mitigate environmental risks, with public input where necessary.

---

### **4. Transitional and Savings Provisions**
- **No Transitional Provisions**: 
  - The Act does not include transitional measures for existing projects, meaning pre-Act applications are **suspended** until permit requirements are met.
- **Consequential Amendments**: 
  - Amendments to the Environmental Effects Act and Resource Management Act are made to align with the 2024 Act’s provisions.

---

### **5. Summary**
The **Offshore Renewable Energy Act 2024** establishes a comprehensive regulatory framework for ORE infrastructure, emphasizing:
- **Permit-based oversight** for marine/resource consents.
- **Mandatory decommissioning plans** with EPA approval.
- **Public consultation** for decommissioning activities.
- **Integration with existing laws** to ensure environmental and resource management compliance.

This structure ensures that ORE projects are developed and decommissioned responsibly, balancing energy generation with ecological and regulatory safeguards.
