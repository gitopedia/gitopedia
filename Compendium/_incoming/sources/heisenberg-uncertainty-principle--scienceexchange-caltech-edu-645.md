# What Is the Uncertainty Principle and Why Is It Important?
- The uncertainty principle is a fundamental concept in quantum physics formulated by Werner Heisenberg in 1927.
- It states that it is impossible to simultaneously know both the position and speed of a particle with perfect accuracy.
- In everyday life, measuring both the speed and position of an object like a car or tortoise is straightforward, but this is not possible for particles such as photons or electrons due to the uncertainty principle.

# The Fundamental Law in Quantum World
- Subatomic particles exhibit wave-like properties according to quantum theory, leading to the application of the uncertainty principle.
- A common misconception is that the uncertainty principle implies inaccuracies in measurements; instead, it reflects an inherent aspect of wave-like behavior.

# Mathematical Uncertainty Principles
- The uncertainty principle applies not only to quantum physics but also to problems in pure math and classical physics, affecting any object with wave-like properties.
