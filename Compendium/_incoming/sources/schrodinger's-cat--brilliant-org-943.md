# Schrödinger's Cat Thought Experiment
- The experiment involves placing a cat inside a box with a vial of poisonous gas and a mallet that breaks the vial if a radioactive atom decays, potentially killing the cat.
- The purpose of the thought experiment is to highlight how certain interpretations of quantum mechanics lead to counterintuitive results.
- Erwin Schrödinger originally proposed the idea in 1935 as an absurd example to show that the Copenhagen Interpretation of quantum mechanics could not be true.
- According to the experiment, before the box is opened, the cat is simultaneously alive and dead due to quantum mechanical behavior.

# Quantum States and Superposition
- The two main ideas behind quantum theory are quantized states and superposition.
- A physical system can exist in a superposition of states, meaning it can be in multiple states at once until observed.
- For example, an electron can be in a state that is 50% spin up and 50% spin down, with the probabilities determined by the magnitudes of the coefficients in the superposition.
- The Schrödinger equation describes how quantum states evolve over time.

# Copenhagen Interpretation
- The Copenhagen Interpretation holds that when an observer measures a system, it collapses from a superposition of states to a single state probabilistically.
- This interpretation leads to the idea that the cat is in a superposition of alive and dead until observed.

# Many-Worlds Interpretation
- The many-worlds interpretation rejects the concept of collapse and instead suggests that every possible outcome creates a new universe.
- In the case of Schrödinger's cat, it results in two "world-branches": one where the cat is alive and one where it is dead.
- This interpretation resolves the issue of differing observations among different observers by positing an infinite number of parallel universes.

# Technical Notes
- The space of valid quantum states is represented by complex vectors with an eigenbasis given by observable states.
- The probability of observing a specific state is determined by the magnitude of the coefficient corresponding to that state in the superposition.
- Linear algebra, particularly the properties of operators on complex vector spaces, is fundamental to understanding quantum mechanics.
