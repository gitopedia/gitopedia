# What Is Entanglement and Why Is It Important?
- Entanglement is a fundamental aspect of quantum physics and future quantum technologies.
- It reveals itself at very tiny, subatomic scales.
- When two particles, such as photons or electrons, become entangled, they remain connected even when separated by vast distances.
- Entanglement is described as an emergent property, similar to how a ballet emerges from individual dancers.

# How do scientists explain quantum entanglement?
- Rana Adhikari, professor of physics at Caltech, contributed to explaining entanglement.
- Xie Chen, professor of theoretical physics at Caltech, also provided insights into entanglement.
- Manuel Endres, professor of physics and Rosenberg Scholar at Caltech, was involved in the explanation.
- John Preskill, Richard P. Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics and director of the Institute for Quantum Information and Matter, contributed to the understanding of entanglement.

# Unbreakable Correlation
- Entangled particles are generated using a special kind of crystal.
- When measuring the spin direction of one particle, its distant companion will always show a correlated state (e.g., if one is spin up, the other will be spin down).
- This correlation allows researchers to infer the state of one particle based on the measurement of the other.

# Are particles really connected across space?
- Albert Einstein questioned entanglement and proposed "local hidden variable theories" in the 1930s.
- John Bell developed theoretical work in the 1960s to test these theories.
- John Clauser (BS '64) conducted experimental work starting in the 1970s to rule out local hidden-variable theories.

# Relativity Remains Intact
- A misconception about entanglement is that particles communicate faster than light, violating Einstein's relativity.
- Experiments have shown that quantum physics does not allow for faster-than-light communication.
- Thomas Vidick, professor of computing and mathematical sciences at Caltech, explains that correlations exist without communication.

# Networks of Entanglement
- Entanglement can occur among hundreds, millions, or even more particles.
- These systems are studied to understand fundamental physics and develop quantum technologies.
- John Preskill emphasizes entanglement as a key theme in 21st-century science.

# Proving that Quantum Entanglement is Real
- Einstein's Quantum Riddle video explores evidence for quantum entanglement.
- Experiments demonstrate the reality of entanglement, confirming its existence despite initial skepticism.
