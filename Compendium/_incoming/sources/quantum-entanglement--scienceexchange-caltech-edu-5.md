# What Is Entanglement and Why Is It Important?
- Entanglement is a fundamental phenomenon in quantum physics.
- It reveals itself at very tiny, subatomic scales.
- When two particles, such as photons or electrons, become entangled, they remain connected even when separated by vast distances.
- Entanglement is described as an emergent property, similar to how a ballet emerges from individual dancers.

# How do scientists explain quantum entanglement?
- Rana Adhikari, professor of physics at Caltech, contributes to explanations of entanglement.
- Xie Chen, professor of theoretical physics at Caltech, also explains entanglement.
- Manuel Endres, professor of physics and Rosenberg Scholar, is involved in explaining the phenomenon.
- John Preskill, Richard P. Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics and director of the Institute for Quantum Information and Matter, provides insights into entanglement.

# Unbreakable Correlation
- Entangled particles are generated from a special kind of crystal.
- When measuring the spin direction of one particle, its distant companion will always show a correlated state (e.g., if one is spin up, the other is spin down).
- This correlation allows scientists to infer the state of one particle based on the measurement of the other.

# Are particles really connected across space?
- Albert Einstein questioned entanglement's non-locality and proposed local hidden variable theories.
- John Bell developed theoretical work in the 1960s to test these theories.
- Experiments by John Clauser (BS '64) and others in the 1970s demonstrated that local hidden-variable theories were incorrect.
- These experiments involved measuring entangled particles from different angles, leading to unpredictable outcomes.

# Relativity Remains Intact
- A misconception about entanglement is that it allows faster-than-light communication, violating relativity.
- Experiments confirm that no actual communication occurs between entangled particles.
- Thomas Vidick explains that correlations exist without communication, viewing entangled particles as a single object.

# Networks of Entanglement
- Entanglement can occur among hundreds, millions, or more particles.
- These systems are studied to understand fundamental physics and develop quantum technologies.
- John Preskill emphasizes entanglement's importance in 21st-century science.

- The phenomenon of entanglement is real and supported by numerous experiments.
