# What Is Entanglement and Why Is It Important?
- Entanglement is a fundamental aspect of quantum physics and future quantum technologies.
- It reveals itself at very tiny, subatomic scales.
- When two particles, such as photons or electrons, become entangled, they remain connected even when separated by vast distances.
- Entanglement arises from the connection between particles, similar to how a ballet emerges from individual dancers.

# How do scientists explain quantum entanglement?
- Featured scientists include Rana Adhikari, Xie Chen, Manuel Endres, and John Preskill.
- John Preskill is the Richard P. Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics and director of the Institute for Quantum Information and Matter.

# Unbreakable Correlation
- Researchers use a special kind of crystal to generate two entangled particles from one.
- Entangled particles are sent to different locations, often to measure their spin direction (up or down along a given axis).
- Before measurement, each particle is in a state of superposition, meaning both "spin up" and "spin down" simultaneously.
- Measuring one particle's spin reveals the correlated state of its distant entangled partner.

# Are particles really connected across space?
- Some scientists, including Albert Einstein, proposed local hidden variable theories to explain entanglement without non-local influences.
- John Bell developed theoretical work in the 1960s to test these theories.
- Experimental work by John Clauser (BS '64) and others beginning in the 1970s confirmed that local hidden-variable theories are invalid.
- Observing particles from different angles showed that the second particle's state changes based on the viewing angle, ruling out pre-determined hidden information.

# Relativity Remains Intact
- A common misconception is that entanglement allows faster-than-light communication, which contradicts Einstein's special theory of relativity.
- Experiments have shown that particles do not communicate faster than light; there is no spooky action at a distance.
- Correlation exists without communication, and particles can be thought of as one object.

# Networks of Entanglement
- Entanglement can occur among hundreds, millions, or even more particles.
- These systems act as one unified entity, similar to a flock of birds.
- Caltech scientists study many-body entangled systems for fundamental physics and quantum technologies.
- John Preskill believes entanglement will be a key theme in 21st-century science.
