# What Is Entanglement and Why Is It Important?
- Entanglement is a fundamental aspect of quantum physics and future quantum technologies.
- It reveals itself at very tiny, subatomic scales.
- When two particles, such as photons or electrons, become entangled, they remain connected even when separated by vast distances.

# How do scientists explain quantum entanglement?
- Featured scientists include Rana Adhikari, Xie Chen, Manuel Endres, and John Preskill from Caltech.
- John Preskill is the Richard P. Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics and director of the Institute for Quantum Information and Matter.

# Unbreakable Correlation
- Entangled particles are generated using a special kind of crystal.
- Researchers measure the direction of particles' spin, which can be either up or down along a given axis.
- Measuring one particle's spin state reveals the correlated state of its distant entangled partner.

# Are particles really connected across space?
- Albert Einstein questioned entanglement and proposed local hidden variable theories in the 1930s.
- John Bell developed theoretical work in the 1960s to test these theories.
- Experiments by John Clauser (BS '64) and others beginning in the 1970s ruled out local hidden-variable theories.

# Relativity Remains Intact
- A misconception is that entanglement allows faster-than-light communication, violating Einstein's relativity.
- Experiments confirm no faster-than-light communication occurs through entanglement.
- Correlation without communication exists; particles can be thought of as one object.

# Networks of Entanglement
- Entanglement can involve hundreds, millions, or more particles.
- It is believed to occur naturally in atoms, molecules, metals, and other materials.
- Caltech scientists study many-body entangled systems for fundamental physics and quantum technologies.
