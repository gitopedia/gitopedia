**Quantum Entanglement: An Encyclopedia Entry**

**Introduction**

Quantum entanglement is a fundamental phenomenon in quantum mechanics where pairs or groups of particles interact such that the quantum state of each particle cannot be described independently of the others, even when separated by large distances. This peculiar correlation was initially met with skepticism but has since become a cornerstone of modern physics and technology.

**History and Einstein's Objection**

The concept of entanglement was first introduced in the 1935 Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) paradox, which challenged the completeness of quantum mechanics. Einstein and his colleagues argued that quantum mechanics could not describe reality fully, suggesting the existence of "local hidden variables." However, John Bell's theorem later demonstrated that no local hidden variable theory could explain the predictions of quantum mechanics, leading to experimental confirmation through Bell tests.

**Detecting Entanglement**

The Peres-Horodecki criterion is a key tool for detecting entanglement in 2-qubit systems. For larger systems, determining separability becomes more complex, involving methods like the range and reduction criteria. Experimental certification often involves iterative numerical approaches or uncertainty relations to confirm entanglement.

**Creating Entanglement**

Entanglement can be created through various methods, including spontaneous parametric down-conversion for photon pairs and interactions in quantum dots. Techniques like the Hong-Ou-Mandel effect enable entanglement without direct interaction, while entanglement swapping allows linking particles that never interacted.

**Applications**

Quantum entanglement is pivotal in technologies such as quantum computing, quantum communication (e.g., Quantum Key Distribution), and metrology. It enhances precision in measurements and underpins the development of quantum networks.

**Implications in Cosmology**

Entanglement offers insights into cosmological questions, like the nature of time and spacetime emergence. The Page-Wootters mechanism suggests that cosmic evolution arises from entanglement between systems, aligning with theories of quantum gravity.

**Recent Experiments**

Recent milestones include long-distance Bell tests over 1,203 km, top quark spin entanglement at the LHC, and macroscopic object entanglement. These experiments highlight practical applications and validate theoretical predictions.

**Conclusion**

Quantum entanglement's exploration continues to expand our understanding of physics, with implications in technology, cosmology, and fundamental science. As research progresses, its potential applications promise transformative technological advancements.
