# Dark Energy and Dark Matter Overview
- All atoms and light in the universe make up less than five percent of total cosmic contents.
- Dark matter and dark energy together account for the remaining 95% of the universe's composition.
- Dark matter makes up most of the mass of galaxies and galaxy clusters, influencing their structure and organization.
- Dark energy is a mysterious force driving the accelerated expansion of the universe.

# Observing Galaxies
- The Giant Magellan Telescope (GMT) will provide new details in large galaxies and detect dwarf galaxies too faint to see with current instruments.
- Dark matter models predict many more dwarf galaxies than observed, making surveys crucial for understanding dark matter distribution.

# Theoretical Models of Dark Matter
- Researchers create theoretical models of dark matter behavior based on observational data since direct measurements are not possible.
- A study explores whether some dark matter carries an electric charge.

# Measuring Cosmic Acceleration
- The Baryon Oscillation Spectroscopic Survey (BOSS) maps the position of tens of thousands of galaxies to understand dark energy.
- BOSS provides some of the best observational data on dark energy.

# Dark Matter Properties
- Dark matter is invisible but has mass, observed through gravitational influence.
- Studies show stars and gas moving as though more mass than visible pulls them along, indicating a "halo" of dark matter around galaxies.

# Galaxy Clusters and Dark Matter
- Galaxy clusters contain hundreds or thousands of galaxies, each with their own dark matter halo.
- The Bullet Cluster provides evidence for dark matter through gravitational lensing, showing most mass is around galaxies rather than gas.

# Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) Studies
- Fluctuations in the CMB indicate dark matter constitutes about 27% of the universe's content.
- Dark energy contributes approximately 68% of the total energy content of the universe.

# Historical Discoveries
- Edwin Hubble discovered galaxies moving away, leading to the conclusion the universe is expanding.
- In 1998, type Ia supernovas revealed cosmic expansion was accelerating, implying dark energy's existence.

# Astronomical Tools and Projects
- Type Ia supernovae are used to measure cosmic expansion and detect dark energy.
- Baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) from the early universe are mapped using thousands of galaxies.
- The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) plays a key role in studying dark energy.

# Telescopes and Instruments
- The South Pole Telescope is used for cosmic microwave background measurements.
- The Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) contributes to understanding dark matter and dark energy.
- Pan-STARRS-1 Science Consortium conducts wide-field surveys to study celestial objects.

# Research Facilities and Programs
- The Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian hosts various programs studying dark matter and dark energy.
- Projects include the Physics of the Primordial Universe and Astrophysicists Reveal Largest-Ever Suite of Universe Simulations.
