# What is Quantum Entanglement?
- Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where particles of the same origin remain connected even after separating and moving far apart in time and space.
- These particles share a new, united quantum state that they maintain forever.
- If something happens to one particle, it affects all the others with which it's entangled.

# A "Spooky" Science
- Albert Einstein first pointed out the "spooky" action of quantum entanglement in 1935.
- This phenomenon appeared to conflict with Einstein’s theory of special relativity, as it suggested information could travel faster than the speed of light.
- John Stewart Bell developed a method to test quantum entanglement in the 1960s, enabling later scientists to confirm its existence.

# Classical vs. Quantum Physics
- Classical physics governs predictable interactions, such as an apple staying on a table unless moved.
- In quantum physics, particles can behave unpredictably, with possibilities for multiple outcomes existing simultaneously.
- Hypothetically, in the quantum world, an apple could end up anywhere, including Mars, and exist in multiple places at once.

# NASA's Quantum Entanglement Research
- NASA is conducting research to unravel the mysteries of quantum entanglement through various experiments:
  - **SEAQUE (Space Entanglement and Annealing Quantum Experiment)**: A project focused on advancing quantum technology.
  - **How Atoms Are Defying Gravity in NASA's Cold Atom Lab**: Research involving ultra-cold atoms to study quantum phenomena.
  - **NASA Demonstrates ‘Ultra-Cool’ Quantum Sensor for First Time in Space**: Development of advanced quantum sensors for space applications.

# Biological and Physical Sciences Division
- NASA’s Biological and Physical Sciences Division aims to advance fundamental scientific knowledge through studies in extreme space environments.
- These investigations help prepare for deeper space exploration and also benefit life on Earth by improving our understanding of physical and biological processes.
