# The inconstant cosmological constant
- Dark energy is an unknown mechanism driving the Universe’s accelerating expansion.
- The concept of a time-dependent dark energy has been explored, with evidence suggesting its energy density is currently decreasing at 99.99% confidence (3.9 sigma).
- DESI collaboration revealed this evidence a year prior to March 2024.
- In March 2024, new data from DESI, DES, ACT, and Euclid strengthened the signal of time-dependent dark energy but did not reach the 99.9999% (5 sigma) confidence level considered robust in astronomy.
- DESI’s second data release (DR2) showed a preference for evolving dark energy at 99.995% (4.2 sigma) significance, based on three years of survey data.
- DR2 doubled the low-redshift galaxy and quasar sample to ~14 million and high-redshift quasar sample to over 800,000.
- The enhanced constraining power enabled precise measurement of the sound horizon of the early Universe through baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO).
- Combining BAO measurements with cosmic microwave background (CMB) information and standard-candle supernova (SN) catalogues indicated a dark energy equation of state deviating from a cosmological constant.
- The preferred parameter space implies an earlier period of increasing energy density as the Universe expanded, challenging many dark energy models.
- Replication of the evolving dark energy evidence across multiple datasets and collaborations alleviated concerns about potential biases in the first DESI data release.
- DES analysis showed that an evolving equation of state can alleviate tensions between different cosmological probes, with a 3.2 sigma preference for evolving dark energy over ΛCDM using BAO, SNe, and Planck CMB power spectra.
- ACT found only a ‘hint’ of deviations from a cosmological constant model when combining their latest CMB data with DESI BAO and Pantheon+ SNe.
- The consensus is not universal, but increasing precision in BAO measurements suggests converging evidence for time-dependent dark energy across multiple experiments.
- If ΛCDM is convincingly ruled out, it would represent a significant shift in the field’s understanding of dark energy.
- Euclid’s first quick data release doubled the number of known strong gravitational lenses and identified low-redshift Little Red Dot candidates from just 0.4% of its final survey data.
- Vera C. Rubin Observatory is expected to achieve first light in mid-2025, and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope aims for a 2027 launch, promising further insights into dark energy evolution.
- The collaborative nature of international astronomy efforts highlights the importance of cooperation in advancing cosmological understanding.
