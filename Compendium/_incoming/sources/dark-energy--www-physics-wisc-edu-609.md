# Dark Energy Survey Collaboration Release New Analysis
- The Dark Energy Survey (DES) collaboration, including scientists from the University of Wisconsin–Madison, has released new results combining six years of data from weak lensing and galaxy clustering probes.
- This analysis represents a summary of 18 supporting papers and presents the first results from combining all four dark energy probes: baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO), type-Ia supernovae, galaxy clusters, and weak gravitational lensing.
- Keith Bechtol, a physics professor at UW–Madison and DES collaboration scientist, stated that the combined approach measures dark energy from a single dataset into a summative result.
- Over 100 people have contributed to these results over more than a decade, with the UW–Madison group playing a significant role.

# UW–Madison Contributions
- Megan Tabbutt, a former physics graduate student at UW–Madison, developed a software pipeline for characterizing stars and galaxies in the data.
- Julián Beas-González, a current graduate student, refined the pipeline and conducted the final analysis using synthetic source injection to validate measurements.
- Jason Lee, a postdoc in Bechtol's group, worked on the type Ia supernovae analysis as a graduate student at the University of Pennsylvania.

# Data Collection and Analysis
- The DES dataset includes over 75,000 individual images with nearly 700 million stars and galaxies.
- Keith Bechtol coordinated the effort to assemble, validate, and document the data products that served as the foundation for the cosmology results.
- The analysis used a method called synthetic source injection to ensure accurate measurements and calibrate photometric redshifts and magnification estimates.

# Scientific Impact
- The new constraints on dark energy models are more than twice as strong as previous DES analyses while remaining consistent with earlier results.
- These findings sharpen the understanding of the physics behind dark energy and dark matter but also highlight unresolved mysteries in cosmology.
- The work concludes a decade-long effort by the DES collaboration, setting the stage for future surveys to further explore the universe's makeup, origins, and evolution.

# Future Directions
- The analysis marks the final stages of the DES project, with new generations of scientists excited to collaborate on upcoming projects.
- The results provide a foundation for more detailed surveys that aim to better understand the universe's composition and evolution.
