# Nobel 1945: the exclusion principle
- Wolfgang Pauli (pictured left, with Niels Bohr on the right) published a paper that earned him a Nobel Prize in 1945.
- Pauli described the exclusion principle in a letter to his colleague Alfred Landé dated 24 November 1924.
- The exclusion principle states that no two electrons can coexist in a state sharing the same four quantum numbers within an atom.
- Pauli recalled being asked by a friend why he looked mournful, to which he responded: “How can one look happy when he is thinking about the anomalous Zeeman effect?”
- At the age of 24, Pauli experienced his first serious setback related to the exclusion principle and temporarily stopped working on atomic physics.
- Pauli’s work was published in a paper titled "Über den zusammenhang des abschlusses der elektronengruppen im atom mit der komplexstruktur der spektren" in Z. Physik, volume 31, pages 765–783 (1925).
- J.L. Heilbron wrote about the origins of the exclusion principle in "Hist. Stud. Phys. Sci." volume 13, pages 261–310 (1983).
- Arthur I. Miller discussed Pauli’s work in his book "137: Jung, Pauli, and the Pursuit of a Scientific Obsession" published by W. W. Norton in 2009.
- Pauli reflected on the history of the exclusion principle in an article titled "Remarks on the history of the exclusion principle" published in Science, volume 103, pages 213–215 (1946).
- A detailed analysis of Pauli’s contributions is provided in "Constructing Quantum Mechanics Volume Two" by Arthur Duncan and Martin Janssen, published by Oxford University Press in 2023.
- Davide Castelvecchi authored the article on the exclusion principle for Nature Reviews Physics, volume 7, page 531 (2025).
