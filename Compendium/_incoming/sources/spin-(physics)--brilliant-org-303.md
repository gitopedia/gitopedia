# Spin (Physics)
- Spin is one of the angular momenta carried by particles and is considered an intrinsic property, similar to electric charge and mass.
- The Earth's motion around the sun can be compared to the orbital angular momentum of a particle, while its rotation about its own axis represents the spin.
- Every particle can be classified as either a boson or fermion based on its spin.
- Bosons are characterized by integer spin due to their symmetric wave functions and can occupy the same state. Examples include charge carriers, the Higgs particle, and applications like lasers and superfluids.
- Fermions have a spin of 1/2 due to their antisymmetric wave functions, preventing them from occupying the same state. Elementary fermions include quarks and leptons (electrons, muons, taus, neutrinos), while composite fermions include protons and neutrons.
- Helium-3 has a spin of 1/2 and is classified as a fermion.
- Helium-4 has a spin of 0 and is classified as a boson. At low temperatures, it forms liquid helium, exhibiting superfluid properties.
