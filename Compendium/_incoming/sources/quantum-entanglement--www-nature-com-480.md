### **Quantum Networks and Entanglement Generation**
- **Multiplexed Entanglement Generation**: Quantum networks can generate entanglement using multi-qubit nodes, enabling efficient distribution of entangled states over noisy channels.
- **Anonymous Transmission**: Anonymous transmission in quantum networks is possible using W states, which provide a secure method for information exchange.

### **Quantum Repeaters and Photon Pairs**
- **Photon Pair Sources**: Quantum repeaters can utilize photon pair sources to enhance the efficiency of entanglement distribution across long distances.
- **Multimode Memories**: The use of multimode memories in quantum repeaters is crucial for storing and manipulating entangled states.

### **Spin-Photon Entanglement**
- **Er3+ Ion**: A single Er³⁺ ion can achieve spin-photon entanglement in the telecom band, which is significant for quantum communication applications.

### **Quantum Teleportation**
- **Long Distance Multiplexed Quantum Teleportation**: It is possible to teleport quantum states over long distances using a telecom photon and a solid-state qubit, demonstrating the feasibility of practical quantum communication systems.

These facts highlight advancements in quantum networking technologies, including entanglement generation, anonymous transmission, spin-photon entanglement, and long-distance quantum teleportation.

- **Hong-Ou-Mandel (HOM) Indistinguishability Measurement:**
  - Orthogonally polarized photons emitted from two remote 171Yb ions with a frequency difference of ≈2π × 33 MHz were incident on a polarizing beamsplitter.
  - Photon coincidences were histogrammed with respect to detection time differences (Δt = t2 − t1) using a 160 ns bin size, showing no HOM interference due to distinguishable photons.
  - By reducing the bin size below 1/Δω12 (4 ns), quantum beats (oscillations between bunching and anti-bunching) were observed at the optical frequency difference.
  - A conventional HOM dip was recovered by considering coincidences only at the trough of each oscillation, with a visibility of 96 ± 2%.

# Optical Spectral Diffusion and Coherence Preservation
- A superposition state \(|\psi \rangle =1/\sqrt{2}(|0\rangle +|e\rangle )\) is used to study optical spectral diffusion.
- The coherence of the system undergoes exponential decay with a 1/e timescale limited by the Purcell-enhanced optical lifetimes of the ions.
- The entangled state coherence is probed via the X-basis parity expectation value \(\langle \widehat{X}\widehat{X}\rangle\), which oscillates at the relative difference in driving laser frequencies \(\Delta {\widetilde{\omega }}_{12}\).

# Dynamic Rephasing Protocol
- A dynamic rephasing protocol is applied to counteract phase drift and maintain optical phase stability.
- The protocol involves adjusting the driving phase of AOM 3 to correct the optical phase difference by an angle Θ.
- The resulting Fourier transform shows that the optical phase noise is significantly reduced when the phase correction is turned on.

# Entanglement Fidelity and Rate Analysis
- The entanglement fidelity is characterized through simulations based on independently measured experimental parameters, leading to a simulated fidelity of \({\mathcal{F}}=0.729\,\pm \,0.004\).
- The dominant sources of error include undetected spontaneous emission during the dynamic rephasing protocol, noise counts, and errors in the optical gates applied to the ions.
- The predicted entanglement rate is \({\mathcal{R}}=3.48\,\pm \,0.03\) Hz, with the experiment repetition rate being 12.3 kHz.

# Entanglement Herald Protocol
- A two-photon protocol is used for entanglement heralding between two remote 171Yb ions.
- The pulse sequence involves preparing each qubit in a superposition state and optically exciting them in two rounds, separated by a qubit π pulse.
- Photon detections in both rounds at stochastic times t0 and t1 carve out \(| 00\rangle \) and \(| 11\rangle \), thereby heralding entanglement.

# Multiplexed Entanglement
- A multiplexed entanglement protocol is used to parallelize the initialization of all four ions.
- Two consecutive entanglement attempts are performed on Pair 2 (Ions 3 and 4) and then on Pair 1 (Ions 1 and 2).
- The experiment measures two-qubit populations in the XX, YY, or ZZ bases, leading to fidelities of \({{\mathcal{F}}}_{{\rm{m}}{\rm{u}}{\rm{l}}{\rm{t}}}^{(1)}=0.700\,\pm \,0.005\) and \({{\mathcal{F}}}_{{\rm{m}}{\rm{u}}{\rm{t}}}^{(2)}=0.668\,\pm \,0.006\) for Pair 1 and Pair 2, respectively.

# Nanophotonic Cavity Entanglement
- Heralded entanglement between two 171Yb ions in the same nanophotonic cavity is demonstrated.
- The experiment involves applying dynamic rephasing and phase compensation protocols to maintain coherence and stability.
- The resulting entangled state fidelity is limited by the Purcell-enhanced optical lifetimes of the ions, with a normalized histogram showing a Gaussian distribution with standard deviation 0.037 × 2π rad.

# Phase Stabilization
- A phase correction mechanism is implemented to counteract phase drift and render the optical phase constant between consecutive probe periods.
- The RMS difference between values of ΔΦ in probe periods of varying separation yields a 1/e phase correlation timescale of ≈ 282 μs.
- With the phase stabilization turned on, the Fourier transform shows reduced phase noise, leading to an entangled state fidelity limitation of \({\mathcal{F}} < 0.987\).

# Error Analysis
- The fidelity estimation involves simulating the fidelity when a given error source is removed or exclusively present.
- The dominant limitations to the experiment repetition rate arise from the qubit initialization time, requiring 33 μs per attempt.
- The entanglement success probability is characterized by contributions from photon detection efficiency, photons within the 500 ns acceptance window, and weak superposition states used in the single photon heralding protocol.

# Window Size Analysis
- The entanglement rate and fidelity are plotted against window size W, where heralding events are accepted under the condition ∣t1 − t0∣ < W/2.
- For the smallest window size W = 200 ns, a fidelity of \({\mathcal{F}}=0.84\,\pm \,0.03\) and a rate of \({\mathcal{R}}=17\) mHz are achieved.
- For the largest window size W = 1.6 μs, a fidelity of \({\mathcal{F}}=0.75\,\pm \,0.02\) and a rate of \({\mathcal{R}}=91\) mHz are obtained.

# Maximum Likelihood Tomography
- Density matrix tomography is performed with a window size W = 600 ns, leading to a fidelity of \({\mathcal{F}}=0.81\,\pm \,0.02\) and a rate of \({\mathcal{R}}=49\) mHz.
- The resulting density matrix is obtained via maximum likelihood tomography, providing insights into the entangled state's properties.

# Summary
- The experiment demonstrates successful entanglement between two remote 171Yb spin qubits in nanophotonic cavities.
- Key challenges include phase drift, noise counts, and spontaneous emission, which limit the fidelity and rate of entanglement.
- The dynamic rephasing protocol and multiplexed entanglement approach show promise for improving future experiments.

# Abstract
- The study introduces an entanglement protocol for qubits using differential a.c. Stark shifts generated by optical pulses.
- Single-qubit Z rotations are achieved through this method, avoiding global microwave driving.
- A differential phase is accumulated using the XY-8 dynamical decoupling sequence with 32 π pulses separated by 5.8 μs.

# Methods
- The protocol involves embedding a.c. Stark shifts into an XY-8 sequence to preserve qubit coherence while accumulating differential phases.
- The a.c. Stark intensity is adjusted by varying Ω², where Ω is the Rabi frequency of the optical pulses.
- Two methods verify the a.c. Stark control: independent initialization and measurement of spin populations for Ions 1 and 3, and heralded Bell states.

# Results
- Differential phase rotations (Φa.c.) are measured for varying Ω² values using spin population measurements and Bell state analysis.
- The correspondence between solid line plots and markers confirms the accuracy of a.c. Stark control.
- After entangling Ions 1 and 3, an a.c. Stark sequence is applied, shifting the parity oscillation of coherence with photon emission time (t0).
- Maximum likelihood tomography yields a density matrix fidelity of F = 0.763 ± 0.005 and a heralding rate of R = 4.5 Hz.
