The spin-statistics theorem is a fundamental result in quantum field theory that establishes a connection between the spin of a particle and its statistical properties, specifically whether it behaves as a boson or a fermion. Here are some key facts extracted from the provided content:

1. **Relativistic Derivation**:
   - The spin-statistics theorem can be derived within the framework of relativistic quantum field theory (RQFT).
   - This derivation typically involves the use of the CPT (Charge-Conjugation, Parity-Time) symmetry and the properties of representations of the Poincaré group.

2. **Non-Relativistic Derivations**:
   - There are attempts to derive the spin-statistics theorem in non-relativistic quantum mechanics.
   - These derivations often rely on geometric arguments involving the configuration space of particles and the concept of indistinguishability.
   - Notable approaches include the Berry-Robbins model, which uses the geometry of the configuration space to establish the connection between spin and statistics.

3. **Topological Field Theory Perspective**:
   - The theorem can also be understood from the perspective of extended topological field theory.
   - This involves categorification techniques, such as using 2-rings and applying Deligne's theorem on tensor categories.
   - Recent work by authors like Theo Johnson-Freyd, Lukas Müller, Luuk Stehouwer, and Cameron Krulewski explores these categorical approaches to the spin-statistics theorem.

4. **Higher Spin Statistics**:
   - There is ongoing research into extending the spin-statistics theorem to higher-dimensional settings.
   - This includes work on invertible quantum field theories and the development of a higher spin statistics theorem.

5. **Hermitian Functorial Field Theory**:
   - Some approaches use reflection structures within hermitian functorial field theory to understand spin and statistics in low dimensions.

6. **Key References**:
   - The book "CPT Invariance and the Spin-Statistics Connection" by Jonathan Bain provides a detailed exploration of non-RQFT derivations.
   - Works by Michael Berry, Jonathan Robbins, and others discuss spin-statistics without relying on relativity or field theory.
   - Recent papers by Johnson-Freyd, Müller, Stehouwer, and Krulewski explore categorical and higher-dimensional perspectives.

In summary, the spin-statistics theorem is deeply connected to both relativistic and non-relativistic quantum theories, with various approaches providing insights from geometric, topological, and categorical angles.
