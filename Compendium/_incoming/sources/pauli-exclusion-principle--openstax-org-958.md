# Multiple-Electron Atoms
- All atoms except hydrogen are multiple-electron atoms.
- The physical and chemical properties of elements are directly related to the number of electrons a neutral atom has.
- The periodic table of the elements groups elements with similar properties, starting from hydrogen (1 electron) up to calcium (20 electrons).
- The Pauli exclusion principle determines the maximum number of electrons allowed in each shell and subshell.

# Discovery of Factual Information
- **Factual Information**: 
  - All atoms except hydrogen are multiple-electron atoms.
  - The physical and chemical properties of elements are directly related to the number of electrons a neutral atom has.
  - The periodic table of the elements groups elements with similar properties, starting from hydrogen (1 electron) up to calcium (20 electrons).
  - The Pauli exclusion principle determines the maximum number of electrons allowed in each shell and subshell.

# Subshells and Electron Capacity
- **Factual Information**:
  - Each shell is composed of multiple subshells, labeled by letters such as s, p, d, f.
  - The capacity of each subshell to hold electrons is determined by the formula: \(2(2l + 1)\), where \(l\) is the azimuthal quantum number corresponding to the subshell (s=0, p=1, d=2, etc.).
  - For example:
    - s-subshells can hold a maximum of 2 electrons.
    - p-subshells can hold a maximum of 6 electrons.
    - d-subshells can hold a maximum of 10 electrons.

# Shell Filling Order
- **Factual Information**:
  - Electrons fill shells and subshells in order of increasing energy, starting from the lowest shell (n=1) upwards.
  - Within a shell, subshells are filled starting with the lowest \(l\) value (s first, then p, d, etc.).
  - Exceptions to this rule occur due to the penetration effect, where electrons in lower l values have closer probability clouds and are more tightly bound:
    - The 4s subshell fills before the 3d subshell.
    - Similarly, the 5s subshell fills before the 4d subshell.

# Periodic Table and Electron Configurations
- **Factual Information**:
  - The periodic table is organized based on electron configurations and chemical properties.
  - Groups in the periodic table are characterized by their outermost electron configuration:
    - Group 18 (noble gases): Filled outermost subshell, highly stable, low reactivity.
    - Group 17 (halogens): One vacancy in the outermost p-subshell, highly reactive.
    - Group 1 (alkali metals): Single s-electron in the outermost shell, highly reactive.

# Chemical Reactivity
- **Factual Information**:
  - Noble gases are chemically inert due to their filled outermost subshells.
  - Halogens are highly reactive as they readily accept an extra electron to achieve a filled outermost subshell.
  - Alkali metals are highly reactive as they easily lose their single outermost s-electron.

# Exceptions and Complex Cases
- **Factual Information**:
  - The filling order of shells and subshells is not always straightforward due to quantum mechanical effects.
  - Heavy elements, such as those in the lanthanide series, exhibit complex electron configurations that do not follow simple shell-filling patterns.

# Summary
- The Pauli exclusion principle plays a fundamental role in determining the electron configuration of atoms.
- Electron configurations and subshell capacities are crucial for understanding chemical properties and reactivity in the periodic table.
