# What is the Double-Slit Experiment?
- The double-slit experiment demonstrates that light and matter can act as both waves and discrete particles depending on whether they are being observed.
- It highlights the concept of wave-particle duality, a key feature of quantum mechanics.
- The experiment shows that particles can behave like waves when not observed and as particles when observed.

# Key Ideas from Quantum Mechanics
- Werner Heisenberg introduced matrix mechanics in 1925, which described subatomic particle properties using mathematical matrices.
- Edwin Schrödinger's wave theory (1926) showed particles could be described by wave equations, leading to the concept of wave-particle duality.
- The observer's role is crucial; measuring a particle as a wave or a particle determines its form.

# How Does the Double-Slit Experiment Work?
- A coherent light source, like a laser beam (e.g., blue visible light at 460nm), is aimed at a wall with two slits.
- The distance between the slits is approximately the same as the light's wavelength.
- Behind the wall, a screen detects and records the light pattern.
- When light passes through both slits, it creates an interference pattern due to wave behavior.

# Observations from the Experiment
- Single photons produce individual dots on the screen, but over time, they form an interference pattern, indicating wave-like behavior.
- Detecting which slit a photon passes through collapses its wave function into a particle, altering the pattern to resemble random points.

# Historical Context
- The double-slit experiment predates quantum mechanics by over a century.
- Thomas Young presented the experiment in 1803, demonstrating light's wave nature using interference patterns.
- This refuted Newton's corpuscular theory of light and supported the wave theory.

# Replicating the Double-Slit Experiment
- Materials needed include a box with a hole, a card with two slits (0.1mm to 0.4mm apart), tape, and eye-holes for observation.
- Sunlight or a laser pointer can be used as the light source.
- Alternately, digital tools like Photoshop can simulate the interference pattern.

# Advanced Applications
- Modern experiments use photon detectors in advanced physics labs to explore quantum mechanics.
- These setups reveal that particles behave probabilistically, governed by wave functions until measured.

# Conclusion
- The double-slit experiment is a cornerstone of quantum mechanics, illustrating the fundamental role of observation and measurement in determining particle behavior.
