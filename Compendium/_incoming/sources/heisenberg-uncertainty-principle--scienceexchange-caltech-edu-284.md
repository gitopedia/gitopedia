# What Is the Uncertainty Principle and Why Is It Important?

- The uncertainty principle makes it impossible to simultaneously know both the position and speed of a particle, such as a photon or electron, with perfect accuracy.
- Werner Heisenberg formulated the uncertainty principle in 1927.
- In quantum physics, particles exhibit wave-like properties, which means the uncertainty principle applies to all quantum objects.
- The principle describes a trade-off between two complementary properties, such as speed and position.

# Fundamental Law of Quantum World

- Subatomic particles can behave like waves, leading to inherent uncertainty in their measurements.
- A common misconception is that the uncertainty principle implies inaccuracy in measurements; however, it reflects an inherent aspect of wave-like behavior.

# Mathematical Uncertainty Principles

- The uncertainty principle applies not only to quantum physics but also to problems in pure math and classical physics.
- Objects with wave-like properties are affected by the uncertainty principle.
- Quantum objects exhibit wave-like properties by the very nature of quantum theory.
