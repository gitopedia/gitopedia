# What is Quantum Entanglement?
- Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where particles of the same origin remain connected even after separating and moving far apart in time and space.
- These entangled particles share a new, united quantum state that they maintain forever.
- If something happens to one particle, it affects all the others with which it’s entangled.

# A "Spooky" Science
- The concept of quantum entanglement was first pointed out by Albert Einstein and his colleagues in 1935.
- Einstein referred to this phenomenon as “spooky action at a distance.”
- Quantum entanglement appeared to conflict with Einstein’s theory of special relativity, which states that nothing can travel faster than the speed of light (mathematically demonstrated by E=mc²).
- The ability to instantaneously measure the quantum state of one particle by measuring its entangled partner anywhere in the universe suggests information travels faster than lightspeed.
- John Stewart Bell developed a method to test quantum entanglement in the 1960s, enabling later scientists to confirm its existence.

# Classical vs. Quantum Physics
- Classical physics governs a predictable world where objects remain stationary unless acted upon.
- In contrast, quantum physics introduces uncertainty and multiple possible outcomes.
- Hypothetically, in the quantum world, an apple could move to Mars or exist in multiple places simultaneously.
- Quantum entanglement ties particles together across vast distances.

# NASA's Quantum Entanglement Research
- NASA’s SEAQUE (Space Entanglement and Annealing Quantum Experiment) is exploring quantum entanglement in space.
- The Cold Atom Lab on the International Space Station studies how atoms can defy gravity by forming Bose-Einstein condensates in microgravity.
- NASA demonstrated an ultra-cool quantum sensor for the first time in space.
- These efforts aim to advance scientific knowledge and benefit life on Earth while enabling exploration farther into space.

# Biological and Physical Sciences Division
- NASA’s Biological and Physical Sciences Division pioneers scientific discovery and exploration by using space environments for unique investigations.
- Studying phenomena under extreme conditions advances fundamental scientific knowledge required for long-term space exploration.
- Research in this division benefits both space exploration and life on Earth.
