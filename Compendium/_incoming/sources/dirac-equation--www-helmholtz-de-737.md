# Part #02: How an equation predicted antimatter
- Werner Heisenberg and Erwin Schrödinger made significant progress in the development of quantum mechanics in 1925.  
- Physicists such as Max Born and Paul Dirac developed a mathematically coherent formalism of quantum mechanics following Heisenberg and Schrödinger's work.  
- Paul Dirac reconciled quantum mechanics with Einstein’s special theory of relativity, resulting in the famous Dirac equation.  
- The Dirac equation laid the foundation for quantum field theory, which describes the collective behavior of particles using quantum mechanics.  
- Quantum field theory explains elementary particles and their interactions via quantized fields that interact with each other.  
- Particles manifest as excitations of these fields, but human perception is limited to macroscopic phenomena, making quantum mechanical processes hidden from our senses.  
- Dirac’s equation explained an anomaly in the magnetic moment of the electron, confirming its validity.  
- The equation also offered a second solution suggesting negative energies, leading Dirac to hypothesize the existence of antiparticles with opposite charges.  
- In the 1920s, the proton was ruled out as the antiparticle due to its greater mass compared to the electron.  
- Carl David Anderson discovered positrons in nebula chamber experiments with cosmic rays, confirming Dirac’s theoretical prediction of antimatter.  
- The annihilation of particles and antiparticles results in mutual destruction and energy release.  
- The Big Bang should have created equal amounts of matter and antimatter, but our universe is dominated by matter, raising questions about its origin.  
- Quantum field theory describes the creation and annihilation of photons within quantum electrodynamics.  
- Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle allows for the temporary creation of matter and antimatter from vacuum, leading to measurable quantum fluctuations known as “Quantenzittern.”  
- Quantum field theory forms the basis for understanding elementary particles and the development of matter in the universe’s first three minutes.  
- The Helmholtz Association’s research program “Matter and the Universe” involves experts from DESY, KIT, and GSI, building on Dirac’s contributions to quantum field theory.  
- Quantum physics research is groundbreaking due to its application in quantum engineering, such as quantum computing, which actively utilizes quantum physical systems.
