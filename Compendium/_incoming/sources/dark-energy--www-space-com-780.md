- **Dark Energy**: A hypothetical form of energy that permeates all of space and has negative pressure, driving the accelerated expansion of the universe. It is often represented by the cosmological constant (λ) in Einstein's equations.

- **Accelerated Expansion**: The discovery that the universe's expansion is accelerating, contrary to earlier expectations that gravity would eventually slow it down or cause it to contract.

- **Cosmological Constant (λ)**: Introduced by Albert Einstein as a "fudge factor" to keep the universe static, but later abandoned. It was revived with the understanding of dark energy and its role in cosmic expansion.

- **Vacuum Energy**: Theoretical explanation for dark energy, suggesting it is the energy inherent in empty space. However, there is a significant discrepancy between quantum theory predictions and observed values.

- **Big Crunch vs. Big Rip**: Without dark energy, the universe might end in a Big Crunch due to gravity overcoming expansion. With dark energy, the universe could end in a Big Rip, where dark energy tears apart all matter.

- **Critical Density**: The average density required for the universe to just halt its expansion after infinite time. Dark energy helps the universe reach this density, supporting a flat geometry.

- **Flat Universe**: A universe with critical density, which is consistent with observations and cosmic inflation models.

- **Structure Formation**: Gravity dominated during the early universe, allowing formation of structures like galaxies. Dark energy's influence became significant later, accelerating expansion.

- **Observational Evidence**: Confirmed by redshift measurements of supernovae and the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB).

- **Importance of Understanding Dark Energy**: Crucial for modeling the universe's evolution, structure, and ultimate fate.
