# Quantum Mechanics
- Quantum mechanics describes the behavior of subatomic particles such as photons, electrons, and atoms.
- It explains phenomena at microscopic scales where classical physics fails to provide accurate predictions.

# Wave-Particle Duality
- Particles like electrons can exist as both waves and particles.
- The double-slit experiment demonstrates this duality, showing interference patterns when particles are treated as waves.

# Development of Quantum Mechanics
- Max Planck introduced the concept of quantized energy in 1900 to explain blackbody radiation.
- Albert Einstein expanded on this idea in 1905 by proposing that light consists of discrete energy packets called quanta.
- Louis de Broglie showed in 1924 that particles exhibit wave-like properties and waves can display particle-like characteristics.

# Schrödinger's Cat Paradox
- Erwin Schrödinger proposed a thought experiment where a cat is placed in a box with a quantum particle, illustrating the probabilistic nature of quantum mechanics.
- The paradox highlights the debate between the Copenhagen interpretation and alternative views on quantum reality.

# Quantum Entanglement
- Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen demonstrated that entangled particles can have correlated states, allowing instantaneous communication of properties regardless of distance.
- This phenomenon is a fundamental aspect of quantum mechanics and is used in quantum computing.

# Quantum Computing
- Quantum computers use qubits, which can exist in superpositions of states (0 and 1 simultaneously).
- Superposition and entanglement enable parallel processing, offering potential computational advantages over classical computers.

# Relationship with General Relativity
- Quantum mechanics and general relativity are not directly incompatible but lack a unified theory.
- Theories like string theory and loop quantum gravity aim to reconcile these frameworks but have yet to achieve widespread acceptance.

# Historical Contributions
- Niels Bohr developed the early model of atomic structure with quantized energy levels.
- Werner Heisenberg formulated matrix mechanics, while Erwin Schrödinger developed wave mechanics, both contributing to the modern understanding of quantum systems.

# References and Further Reading
- Planck's work on blackbody radiation laid the foundation for quantum theory.
- Einstein's photoelectric effect paper was pivotal in establishing the quantum nature of light.
- de Broglie's Nobel Prize-winning research confirmed the wave-particle duality of matter.
