# Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP)  

## Overview  
- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP) is a collaborative, open-access reference work created by scholars in philosophy and related disciplines.  
- It aims to provide comprehensive, up-to-date resources on philosophical topics, including historical figures, theories, and contemporary debates.  
- The SEP is maintained by the Metaphysics Research Lab at Stanford University, which is part of the Department of Philosophy.  
- It serves as a primary resource for academic research, education, and public understanding of philosophy.  
- The encyclopedia is organized into entries on specific philosophical concepts, thinkers, and movements, with contributions from experts worldwide.  

## Editorial Structure  
- **Co-Principal Editors**: Edward N. Zalta and Uri Nodelman oversee the editorial process and ensure the accuracy and quality of entries.  
- **Editorial Board**: A group of scholars and academics review and approve entries, ensuring adherence to scholarly standards.  
- **Masthead**: The editorial leadership includes the Provost, Dean of Humanities and Sciences, and Dean of Research at Stanford University, reflecting institutional support.  
- **Contributors**: The SEP relies on a global network of philosophers and related scholars who submit and revise entries.  

## Funding and Support  
- **SEP Library Fund**: Supported by the National Endowment for the Humanities (NEH) and academic libraries that join SEPIA (SEP Institutional Access).  
- **O.C. Tanner SEP Fund**: Established through a gift from the O.C. Tanner Company, providing financial backing for the project.  
- **John Perry Fund and SEP Fund**: Funded by individual donors, supporting the development and maintenance of the encyclopedia.  
- **Friends of the SEP Society Fund**: Sustained by membership dues from individuals who join the Friends of the SEP Society, offering benefits like PDF versions of entries.  
- **Founding Support**: Initial funding came from the NEH, National Science Foundation (NSF), American Philosophical Association/Pacific Division, Canadian Philosophical Association, and Philosophy Documentation Center.  
- **Grants**: Fundraising efforts were supported by a grant from the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation, which helped expand the project’s reach and resources.  

## Publication and Distribution  
- **Copyright**: The SEP is copyrighted by the Metaphysics Research Lab, Department of Philosophy, Stanford University, with the copyright year listed as 2025.  
- **ISSN**: The publication is assigned the ISSN 1095-5054, indicating its status as a serial publication.  
- **International Hosting**: The SEP is hosted on servers at multiple institutions, including the University of Sydney (Australia Library) and the Institute for Logic, Language, and Computation (ILLC) at the University of Amsterdam.  
- **Accessibility**: The encyclopedia is freely accessible online, with options for users to access PDF versions of entries through the Friends of the SEP Society.  

## Editorial Features  
- **Browse Table of Contents**: Allows users to navigate entries by subject categories, facilitating targeted research.  
- **What's New**: Highlights recently added or updated entries, keeping users informed of the latest developments in philosophical scholarship.  
- **Random Entry**: A feature that randomly selects an entry for exploration, encouraging serendipitous learning.  
- **Chronological Archives**: Provides access to historical entries, preserving the evolution of philosophical thought over time.  
- **Archives**: Includes both chronological and thematic archives, enabling users to explore entries by date or subject.  

## Academic and Institutional Context  
- **Stanford University**: The SEP is a flagship project of Stanford University, reflecting its commitment to advancing philosophical research.  
- **Interdisciplinary Collaboration**: The encyclopedia integrates contributions from philosophers, historians, linguists, and other scholars, emphasizing interdisciplinary approaches.  
- **Educational Use**: The SEP is widely used in academic courses, research, and public education, serving as a foundational resource for students and professionals.  
- **Open Access**: By making philosophical knowledge freely available, the SEP promotes democratization of education and research, aligning with open-access principles.  

## Historical Development  
- **Origins**: The SEP was founded to address the need for a comprehensive, up-to-date reference work in philosophy, filling gaps left by traditional encyclopedias.  
- **Evolution**: Over time, the SEP has expanded its scope to include emerging fields, contemporary debates, and diverse philosophical traditions.  
- **Global Reach**: The project has fostered international collaboration, incorporating perspectives from scholars in various countries and cultural contexts.
