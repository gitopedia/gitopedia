# What is quantum entanglement?
- Quantum entanglement occurs when two subatomic particles become linked such that their properties remain connected, regardless of the distance between them.
- A change in one particle's state seems to instantaneously influence the other, even if separated by billions of light-years.

# Historical context and key figures
- Albert Einstein referred to entanglement as "spooky action at a distance," expressing skepticism about its implications for faster-than-light communication.
- John Bell formulated Bell's Theorem in 1964, which showed that quantum mechanics could not be explained by hidden local variables if entanglement was real.

# How does quantum entanglement work?
- Entanglement involves particles being in a superposition of states across two or more locations.
- An example is photons emitted from a source, where each photon's polarization is correlated with its twin, even when separated.

# Polarization and measurement
- Measuring the polarization of one photon determines the same for its entangled pair, despite the randomness of individual outcomes.
- This correlation ensures matching results between paired photons, regardless of the distance or timing of measurements.

# Faster-than-light communication concerns
- While measurements of entangled particles appear correlated instantly, no actual information is transmitted faster than light speed.
- The apparent "influence" is non-local and does not enable communication or control over distant systems.

# Proofs and experimental verification
- Experiments since 2015, including those led by Krister Shalm at NIST, have confirmed the validity of Bell's Theorem, supporting the reality of quantum entanglement.
- These studies ruled out hidden variable theories, confirming that entanglement defies classical physics.

# Applications of quantum entanglement
- **Quantum cryptography**: Entanglement enables secure communication through Quantum Key Distribution (QKD), where eavesdropping disrupts entanglement.
- **Quantum computing**: Qubits rely on entanglement and superposition to perform complex calculations efficiently, such as simulating molecular interactions.
- **Deep-space communications**: NASA's LADEE mission tested laser-based systems inspired by quantum principles for faster and more secure data transmission in space.

# Quantum teleportation
- Entanglement is used to transfer the state of a particle to another distant particle without physical movement, demonstrated experimentally.
- This process could revolutionize future quantum networks and communication technologies.

# Technological implications
- Entanglement underpins emerging technologies like quantum computing, secure communications, and advanced materials science through quantum simulation.
- NASA's research on self-healing quantum communication systems highlights the potential for robust space-based applications.

# Philosophical and theoretical significance
- The phenomenon challenges classical notions of locality and realism, prompting reevaluation of fundamental physics principles.
- Entanglement's non-local correlations suggest a deeper understanding of quantum reality is still evolving.
