# Pauli Exclusion Principle
- The Pauli Exclusion Principle states that no two electrons can have the same four electronic quantum numbers in an atom or molecule.
- In an orbital, a maximum of only two electrons can be present, and these must have opposing spins (one spin-up (+1/2) and one spin-down (-1/2)).
- Electrons in the same orbital share the same first three quantum numbers (e.g., n=1, l=0, ml=0 for the 1s subshell).
- If an orbital contains only one electron, its configuration is written as 1s1 (corresponding to hydrogen), and if fully occupied, it is written as 1s2 (corresponding to helium).
- The 1s and 2s subshells can hold a maximum of two electrons each, with the requirement that their spins are opposite to avoid violating the Pauli Exclusion Principle.
