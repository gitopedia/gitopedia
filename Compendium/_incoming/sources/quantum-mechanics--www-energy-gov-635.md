# Quantum Mechanics
- Quantum mechanics is the field of physics that explains how extremely small objects simultaneously have characteristics of both particles and waves, known as "wave-particle duality."
- A quanta is the smallest discrete unit of a natural phenomenon in a bound state. For example, a photon is a quanta of electromagnetic radiation.
- To be "quantized" means particles in a bound state can only have discrete values for properties like energy or momentum. An electron in an atom can only occupy specific energy levels.
- Tiny quantized particles such as electrons can also be described as waves with a "wave function," a mathematical representation describing the probability of a particle's location and momentum over time.
- Quantum mechanics differs from classical mechanics, which governs the macroscopic world where properties like energy can take any value in a range.
- Blackbody radiation and the photoelectric effect were phenomena that early 20th-century physicists struggled to explain, prompting the development of quantum mechanics.
- Quantum mechanics has led to advancements in technologies such as lasers, light-emitting diodes, transistors, medical imaging, electron microscopes, and smartphones.
- The Department of Energy (DOE) Office of Science supports research in quantum computing, quantum information science, and other applications of quantum mechanics.

# DOE Office of Science: Contributions to Quantum Mechanics
- The DOE Office of Science has programs in quantum computing and quantum information science.
- Facilities like the Argonne Tandem Linac Accelerator System (ATLAS) at Argonne National Laboratory, Brookhaven National Laboratory’s Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC), and Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility’s Continuous Electron Beam Accelerator Facility study subatomic particles governed by quantum mechanics.
- Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory is the core U.S. particle physics lab studying quantized particles.
- The Facility for Rare Isotope Beams (FRIB) at Michigan State University studies exotic atomic nuclei.
- Subatomic particles like protons have angular momentum, or "spin," used in MRI imaging devices.
- Smartphones contain billions of transistors that rely on the wave nature of electrons, understood through quantum mechanics.
- Quantum computers and networks use the quantized nature of particles for information storage and transfer.
