# Spin in Physics
- Spin is a physical property of particles, not literal rotation.
- Particles with half-integer spin (e.g., 1/2, 3/2, 5/2) are fermions and must occupy unique quantum states.
- Particles with integer spin (including zero) are bosons and can occupy the same state without restriction.
- The classification of particles into fermions or bosons is based on mathematical theory supported by all observations so far.
- Photons, as bosons, enable laser beams due to their identical energy and phase alignment.
- Electrons, as fermions, exist in discrete atomic orbits due to their half-integer spin and Pauli exclusion principle.
- Mesons are bosons with integral spin and an even number of quarks, making them immune to the Pauli exclusion principle.
- Neutrons have half-integer spin similar to protons but exhibit effects suggesting internal structure despite being electrically neutral.
- The magnitude of proton and neutron spin is not a simple sum of their constituent quark spins.

# Historical Development of Spin
- Electron spin was first proposed in 1925 by Samuel Goudsmit and George Uhlenbeck.
- Earlier experiments by Pieter Zeeman (1897) and Otto Stern & Walter Gerlach (1920) laid the groundwork for understanding particle spin.
- Goudsmit and Uhlenbeck faced challenges explaining the magnetic moment until Paul Dirac's relativistic wave equation in 1928 resolved the issue.

# Quantum Mechanical Implications
- Electron spin is described as a corkscrew motion, either right-handed or left-handed.
- Electrons are affected by both electromagnetic and weak nuclear forces, with W bosons acting only on left-handed particles.
- The Higgs field causes electrons to oscillate between right-handedness and left-handedness.

# Entanglement
- Spin entanglement occurs when two particles share opposite spin states regardless of distance.
- Measuring one particle's spin instantaneously determines the other's opposite spin, a phenomenon Einstein called "spooky action at a distance."
- This effect is a fundamental aspect of quantum mechanics and has been experimentally verified, including with photons.
