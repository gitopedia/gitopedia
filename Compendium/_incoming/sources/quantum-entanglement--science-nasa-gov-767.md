# What Is Quantum Entanglement?
- Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where particles of the same origin remain connected even after separating and moving far apart in time and space.
- These particles maintain a new, united quantum state forever, meaning an event affecting one particle also affects its entangled counterparts.

# A "Spooky" Science
- Albert Einstein first pointed out quantum entanglement in 1935, referring to it as "spooky action at a distance."
- This phenomenon appeared to conflict with Einstein's theory of special relativity, which states nothing can travel faster than the speed of light (E=mc²).
- John Stewart Bell later developed a method to test quantum entanglement in the 1960s, enabling future scientists to confirm its existence.

# Classical vs. Quantum Physics
- Classical physics governs predictable interactions, such as an apple staying on a table unless moved.
- In contrast, quantum physics introduces uncertainty and possibilities like particles existing in multiple states simultaneously or being entangled across vast distances.

# NASA's Quantum Entanglement Research
- NASA is conducting research to unravel the mysteries of quantum mechanics, including through experiments like the SEAQUE (Space Entanglement and Annealing Quantum Experiment).
- The Cold Atom Lab enables studies on how atoms defy gravity under extreme conditions.
- NASA demonstrated an "ultra-cool" quantum sensor for the first time in space.
- The Biological and Physical Sciences Division focuses on scientific discovery to advance knowledge for space exploration and life on Earth.
