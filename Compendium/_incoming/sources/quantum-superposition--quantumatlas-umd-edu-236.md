# Quantum Superposition
- A fundamental feature of quantum physics.
- Overlapping waves combine to form a superposition, resulting in a new wave pattern.
- In quantum systems, waves represent the probability of quantum properties like position or energy.
- Electrons do not have definite positions but exist as clouds of possibilities called orbitals.
- The shape of these waves changes based on the electron's energy level.
- Superposition involves combining two quantum waves representing different energy levels, altering the electron's probable location and physical properties.
- In superposition, an electron can appear to have multiple energies or locations simultaneously.
- Viewing electrons as waves makes superposition easier to understand compared to particles.
- A standard experiment involves firing individual electrons at a barrier with two slits.
- If electrons were particles, they would cluster behind each slit; instead, they create an interference pattern, indicating wave-like behavior.
- Each electron enters a superposition of "went through the left slit" and "went through the right slit," leading to interference patterns on the detector.
