**Extracted Facts:**

1. **Entanglement and Quantum Mechanics:**
   - Entanglement is a fundamental phenomenon in quantum mechanics where particles become interconnected, such that the state of one particle instantly influences the state of another, regardless of distance.
   - The concept was initially considered paradoxical but is now a cornerstone of quantum information science.

2. **Bell Tests and Locality:**
   - Bell tests are experiments designed to test the validity of quantum mechanics against local hidden variable theories.
   - Recent "loophole-free" Bell tests, such as those conducted by Yin et al. in 2017, have demonstrated violations of Bell inequalities over distances up to 1,203 km, confirming non-local correlations.

3. **Entanglement in High-Energy Physics:**
   - In 2023, the ATLAS detector at the LHC measured entanglement in top quark pairs with a significance level exceeding 5σ.
   - This experiment utilized quantum tomography to observe spin polarization and correlation, confirming entanglement using criteria like concurrence and the Peres–Horodecki criterion.

4. **Macroscopic Entanglement:**
   - Researchers achieved quantum entanglement between the motion of a millimeter-sized mechanical oscillator and a distant spin system in 2020.
   - Subsequent work extended this to entangle two mechanical oscillators, marking significant progress in macroscopic quantum systems.

5. **Entanglement in Proton Structure:**
   - Physicists at Brookhaven National Laboratory demonstrated that quarks and gluons within protons exhibit maximal entanglement, challenging traditional views of proton structure.
   - High-energy electron-proton collisions revealed this interdependence, reshaping understanding of subatomic particles.

6. **Applications and Implications:**
   - Entanglement is crucial for quantum computing, cryptography, and communication, offering potential advancements in technology beyond classical physics.
   - The study of entanglement extends into areas like quantum gravity and the nature of spacetime, with theories suggesting that spacetime emerges from entangled quantum degrees of freedom.

7. **Theoretical Developments:**
   - Work by Mark Van Raamsdonk suggests that spacetime arises as an emergent phenomenon due to entanglement in boundary systems, linking quantum mechanics and general relativity.
   - The AdS/CFT correspondence provides a framework for understanding such connections, though challenges remain in integrating these theories.

8. **Time and Quantum Gravity:**
   - The "problem of time" in quantum gravity explores how a timeless universe can appear dynamic to internal observers due to entanglement between systems and clocks.
   - This research intersects with efforts to reconcile quantum mechanics and general relativity, though it remains an open question.

9. **Experimental Challenges and Advances:**
   - Techniques like spontaneous parametric down-conversion are commonly used to generate entangled photons, while methods like the Hong–Ou–Mandel effect offer alternative approaches.
   - Testing for entanglement involves various criteria, including the Peres–Horodecki criterion, which is sufficient for certain systems but NP-hard in general.

10. **Future Directions:**
    - Advances in creating and testing entanglement across diverse systems, from subatomic particles to macroscopic objects, promise to deepen understanding of quantum mechanics and its applications.
    - Collaborative efforts between quantum information science and high-energy physics are expected to yield further insights into fundamental phenomena.
