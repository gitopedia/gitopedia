# Wave Function

## Overview
A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one point to another without transferring matter. Waves are fundamental to various phenomena and technologies, including sound, light, radio waves, and more. Understanding waves involves examining their properties and behaviors.

## Key Properties

### Wavelength (λ)
The wavelength of a wave is the distance between two consecutive peaks or crests of the wave. It varies significantly among different types of waves, from hundreds of kilometers in tsunamis to less than a billionth of a meter in gamma rays.

### Frequency (f)
Frequency refers to the number of wavelengths that pass a fixed point per second, measured in hertz (Hz). Higher frequency corresponds to shorter wavelengths and is seen in electromagnetic waves like X-rays.

### Amplitude
Amplitude is the maximum displacement of particles from their equilibrium position. For water waves, it is the distance from the wave crest to the normal water level.

### Speed (v)
Wave speed varies depending on the type of wave. Electromagnetic waves travel at a constant speed (approximately 300 million meters per second in a vacuum), while mechanical waves like sound have variable speeds based on medium and formation.

### Period (T)
The period is the time taken for one complete oscillation. It is inversely proportional to frequency, meaning longer periods correspond to lower frequencies.

## Types of Waves

### Mechanical Waves
These include water waves and sound waves, formed by particle vibrations. Water waves have varying speeds depending on their formation, while sound waves travel at a constant speed in a given medium (e.g., 340 m/s in air).

### Electromagnetic Waves
Formed by oscillating electric and magnetic fields, these waves include light, radio waves, microwaves, and X-rays. They all travel at the same speed in a vacuum.

## Wave Behavior

### Reflection
The bouncing back of waves when they encounter a surface.

### Refraction
The change in direction and speed as waves pass from one medium to another.

### Diffraction
The bending of waves around obstacles or spreading after passing through small openings.

### Interference
The combination of two or more waves, resulting in new wave patterns.

## Applications
Waves are integral to various technologies, including communication (radio, television), medicine (X-rays), and everyday devices like cell phones. Understanding wave behavior enables advancements in these fields.
