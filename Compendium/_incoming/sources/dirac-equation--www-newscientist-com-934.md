# How Dirac Predicted Antimatter
- Paul Dirac (1902-1984) won the Nobel Prize in physics in 1933, in part for his work predicting the existence of antimatter.
- His research was the first time something never before seen in nature was "predicted" based on theoretical rather than experimental evidence.
- Dirac's discovery was guided by human imagination and complex mathematical calculations.
- He combined quantum mechanics with Einstein’s special relativity to develop his theory.
- When asked about his equation, Dirac replied: “I found it beautiful.”
- The Dirac equation explains how particles both very small and very fast, such as electrons near the speed of light, behave.
- His equation also works for a particle that behaves like an electron with positive charge.
- Initially, Dirac did not appreciate the significance of this finding and ignored it out of what he called “pure cowardice.”
- Eventually, he realized his equation predicted the existence of antiparticles.
- Dirac asserted that every particle has a mirror-image antiparticle with nearly identical properties except for an opposite electric charge.
- He speculated that there may even be a mirror universe of antimatter.

# The Strange World of Paul Dirac
- Dirac was a peculiar man, with many stories of his eccentricities.
- When he first visited Niels Bohr’s institute in Copenhagen, his utterances consisted almost entirely of three phrases: “Yes”, “No” and “I don’t mind.”
- Later, he became more flexible. For example, when astrophysicist Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar expounded his views, Dirac repeatedly interjected with “Yes,” but explained that it did not mean agreement.
- In 1929, Dirac sailed from America to Japan with Werner Heisenberg. During the trip, Heisenberg spent the evenings dancing while Dirac looked on, puzzled.
- When Dirac asked Heisenberg why he danced, Heisenberg replied: “Well, when there are nice girls it is a pleasure to dance.”
- After thinking for five minutes, Dirac said: “But how do you know beforehand that the girls are nice?”
