The deuterium-tritium (D-T) fusion reaction is a key process in nuclear fusion, producing helium-4 (⁴He), a free neutron, and releasing significant energy. Here's a structured summary of the reaction as described in part "a":

1. **Reactants and Products**:  
   - **Deuterium (D)**: One proton and one neutron.  
   - **Tritium (T)**: One proton and two neutrons.  
   - **Products**: Helium-4 (⁴He, two protons and two neutrons) and a free neutron.  

2. **Energy Release**:  
   - The fusion reaction releases **17.6 MeV** of energy, primarily in the form of **kinetic energy** of the products.  
   - **Helium-4** carries **3.5 MeV**, and the **free neutron** carries **14.1 MeV**.  

3. **Mass-Energy Conversion**:  
   - The total mass of the products (⁴He and neutron) is slightly less than the combined mass of the initial D and T nuclei.  
   - This **mass defect** is converted into energy via Einstein's equation **E = mc²**, where the lost mass accounts for the released energy.  

4. **Significance**:  
   - The D-T reaction is highly favorable for fusion energy due to its **high energy yield** and **relatively low ignition temperature** compared to other fusion reactions.  
   - It is a primary target for experimental fusion reactors, such as tokamaks and inertial confinement systems, due to its efficiency and simplicity.  

This reaction exemplifies how nuclear fusion can convert mass into energy, offering a potential pathway for sustainable, low-carbon power generation.
