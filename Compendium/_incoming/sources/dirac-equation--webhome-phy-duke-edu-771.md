# The Dirac Equation and the Positron
- Paul Dirac suggested that one should allow oneself to be led in the direction which the mathematics suggests when developing theoretical models for particle physics. 
- Before Dirac invented his equation in 1928, the energy of a particle was modeled by the Schrödinger equation, which was only valid for particles at nonrelativistic conditions.
- Dirac had issues with the Schrödinger equation because it did not ensure that [rho] (the charge density) would be positive, which could have violated the transformation theory proposed in 1902 by Rutherford and Soddy.
- Dirac introduced four coefficients as a variation of the Schrödinger equation. These coefficients are anti-commuting and were found to be four by four matrices.
- The final product of Dirac's work was an equation that correctly predicted the spin and magnetic moment of an electron but also produced unusual results, such as an electron having positive or negative kinetic energy.
- Negative kinetic energy states suggest the existence of a "sea" of negative energy electrons, referred to as the Dirac sea. If a member of this sea is given enough energy, it can rise into a positive energy state, creating a hole that appears positively charged.
- Initially, it was assumed that this hole would be a proton due to the lack of evidence for another positively charged particle with the same mass as an electron at the time.
- Carl Anderson's experiments with cosmic rays in 1932 led to the discovery of positrons. He observed particles bending upward in a cloud chamber, indicating positive charge, and found that these particles had the same mass as an electron.
- Anderson called these new positively charged particles "positrons," which are now recognized as anti-electrons.
- The annihilation of a positron and an electron releases energy in the form of radiation, explained by Dirac's interpretation of the Dirac sea.
- Dirac was awarded a Nobel Prize in 1933 along with Schrödinger for his contributions to quantum mechanics, particularly his theoretical prediction of the positron.
