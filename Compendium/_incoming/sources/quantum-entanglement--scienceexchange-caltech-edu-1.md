# What Is Entanglement and Why Is It Important?
- Entanglement is a fundamental aspect of quantum physics and future quantum technologies.
- It reveals itself at very tiny, subatomic scales.
- When two particles, such as photons or electrons, become entangled, they remain connected even when separated by vast distances.
- Entanglement arises from the connection between particles, similar to how a ballet emerges from individual dancers.

# How do scientists explain quantum entanglement?
- Rana Adhikari, professor of physics at Caltech, contributes to explaining entanglement.
- Xie Chen, professor of theoretical physics at Caltech, also provides insights into entanglement.
- Manuel Endres, professor of physics and Rosenberg Scholar at Caltech, is involved in the study of entanglement.
- John Preskill, Richard P. Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics and director of the Institute for Quantum Information and Matter, is a key figure in quantum information science.

# Unbreakable Correlation
- Entangled particles are generated from a special kind of crystal.
- When measuring the spin direction of entangled particles, they exhibit correlation: if one particle's spin is up, the other will be down, or both will be up or down depending on the experiment design.
- This phenomenon is akin to observing one dancer in a pirouette and knowing the other must also be performing a pirouette.

# Are particles really connected across space?
- Albert Einstein questioned whether entangled particles are truly connected across space and proposed local hidden variable theories.
- John Stewart Bell's theoretical work in the 1960s provided a foundation for understanding entanglement.
- Experimental work by Caltech alumnus John Clauser (BS '64) and others beginning in the 1970s confirmed the validity of entanglement through observations involving different measurement angles.

# Relativity Remains Intact
- A common misconception is that entanglement allows faster-than-light communication, which contradicts Einstein's special theory of relativity.
- Experiments have shown that particles do not communicate faster than light; instead, their correlation arises from connections between them.
- Thomas Vidick, professor of computing and mathematical sciences at Caltech, explains that there can be correlation without communication.

# Networks of Entanglement
- Entanglement can occur among hundreds, millions, or even more particles.
- These systems are thought to exist throughout nature, including in living species and materials like metals.
- Many-body entangled systems act as a unified object, similar to a flock of birds moving together without direct contact.
