# What Is Entanglement and Why Is It Important?
- Entanglement is a fundamental phenomenon in quantum physics.
- It reveals itself at very tiny, subatomic scales.
- When two particles, such as photons or electrons, become entangled, they remain connected even when separated by vast distances.
- Entanglement is described as an emergent property, similar to how a ballet emerges from individual dancers.

- Rana Adhikari, professor of physics at Caltech, explains entanglement.
- Xie Chen, professor of theoretical physics at Caltech, provides insights into the phenomenon.
- Manuel Endres, professor of physics and Rosenberg Scholar at Caltech, discusses entanglement.
- John Preskill, Richard P. Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics and director of the Institute for Quantum Information and Matter, explains the significance of entanglement.

# Unbreakable Correlation
- Entangled particles are generated from a special kind of crystal.
- When measuring the spin direction of one particle, the other will always show a correlated state (e.g., if one is spin up, the other will be spin down).
- This correlation allows scientists to infer the state of one particle based on the measurement of its entangled partner.

# Are particles really connected across space?
- Albert Einstein questioned the "spooky action at a distance" aspect of entanglement.
- Local hidden variable theories were proposed to explain entanglement without non-local influences.
- John Bell's theoretical work in the 1960s provided the foundation for experiments to test these theories.
- Experiments by John Clauser (BS '64) and others beginning in the 1970s ruled out local hidden-variable theories.

# Relativity Remains Intact
- Entanglement does not allow faster-than-light communication, preserving Einstein's theory of relativity.
- The phenomenon of entanglement is real and passes experimental tests.
- Correlation between particles exists without direct communication.

# Networks of Entanglement
- Entanglement can occur among hundreds, millions, or even more particles.
- These systems are studied to understand fundamental physics and develop quantum technologies.
- Many-body entangled systems act as a single unified object, similar to a flock of birds.

# Proving that Quantum Entanglement is Real
- Experiments have confirmed the reality of quantum entanglement beyond any doubt.
- The phenomenon is thought to occur throughout nature, among atoms, molecules, and materials.
