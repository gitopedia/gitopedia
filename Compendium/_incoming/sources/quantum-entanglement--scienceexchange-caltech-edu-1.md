# What Is Entanglement and Why Is It Important?
- Entanglement is a fundamental phenomenon in quantum physics.
- It reveals itself at very tiny, subatomic scales.
- When two particles, such as photons or electrons, become entangled, they remain connected even when separated by vast distances.
- Entanglement is described as an emergent property, similar to how a ballet emerges from individual dancers.

# How do scientists explain quantum entanglement?
- Rana Adhikari, Xie Chen, Manuel Endres, and John Preskill are professors at Caltech who have contributed to explaining entanglement.
- John Preskill is the Richard P. Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics and director of the Institute for Quantum Information and Matter.

# Unbreakable Correlation
- Entangled particles are generated using a special kind of crystal.
- When measuring the spin direction of one particle, the other will always show a correlated state (e.g., if one is spin up, the other will be spin down).
- This correlation allows scientists to infer the state of one particle based on the measurement of its entangled partner.

# Are particles really connected across space?
- Albert Einstein and others proposed "local hidden variable theories" as an explanation for entanglement.
- John Bell's theoretical work in the 1960s provided a framework to test these theories.
- Experiments by John Clauser (BS '64) and others beginning in the 1970s ruled out local hidden-variable theories.
- The experiments involved measuring particles from different angles, showing that the outcomes were not predetermined.

# Relativity Remains Intact
- A common misconception is that entanglement allows faster-than-light communication, which contradicts Einstein's relativity.
- Experiments have confirmed that no actual communication occurs between entangled particles.
- Thomas Vidick explains that correlations exist without communication, and entangled particles can be thought of as a single object.

# Networks of Entanglement
- Entanglement can occur among hundreds, millions, or even more particles.
- These systems are studied to understand fundamental physics and develop quantum technologies.
- John Preskill emphasizes the importance of entanglement in 21st-century science.
