# Introduction
- Electrons exhibit a paradoxical nature, acting as both particles and waves.
- This duality was discovered in the early 20th century and has been a subject of scientific debate and experimentation.

# Part 1: The Classical Case for Particles (The Early Days)
- **Discovery of Electrons**: In 1897, British physicist J.J. Thomson identified electrons as subatomic particles using cathode ray tubes.
- **Oil Drop Experiment**: Robert Millikan's experiment in the early 20th century measured the exact charge of a single electron (1.6×10⁻¹⁹ coulombs).
- **Conclusion by 1910**: Electrons were definitively established as particles with mass and charge.

# Part 2: The Plot Twist (Matter Waves)
- **De Broglie's Proposal**: In 1924, Louis de Broglie proposed that matter could exhibit wave-like properties through his formula λ=h/p.
- **Davisson-Germer Experiment**: Clinton Davisson and Lester Germer observed electron diffraction patterns in 1927, confirming the wave nature of electrons.

# Part 3: Deep Dive — The Double-Slit Paradox (Grades 10-12)
- **Quantum Reality**: Electrons fired through a double-slit setup create an interference pattern, indicating wave-like behavior.
- **Measurement Problem**: Observing electrons as particles disrupts their wave behavior, highlighting the uncertainty in quantum mechanics.

# Part 4: The Math of Uncertainty
- **Schrödinger's Wave Function**: Introduced in 1925, it describes electrons as probability clouds rather than fixed points.
- **Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle**: States that precise simultaneous knowledge of a particle's position and momentum is impossible.

# Part 5: Why Do We Care? (Real World Magic)
- **Electron Microscopes**: Utilize the wave nature of electrons to achieve higher resolution than optical microscopes.
- **Flash Memory & SSDs**: Quantum tunneling allows data storage by manipulating electron movement through barriers.
