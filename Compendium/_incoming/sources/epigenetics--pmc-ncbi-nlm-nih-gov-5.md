- Collection date 2025 Jun.

- Keywords: epigenetics

- Epigenetics emerged as the dynamic successor to classical genetics, moving beyond the static sequence of the double helix that carries the code of life.
- The field studies how chemical and molecular changes affect gene expression levels without altering the underlying sequence of A, T, C, and G nucleotides [1].
- For example, adding and removing methyl groups on DNA may turn genes “off” and “on.” Acetyl groups on histones – the proteins that package and wrap DNA – can cause regions of the genome to become open, loose, and accessible, increasing gene expression.
- While other histone modifications pack and coil DNA tightly, suppressing gene expression.
- In its early days, the field sought to understand how a single fertilized egg, with one genetic code, becomes a complex multicellular organism.
- Today, the field continues to discover how genes are differentially read, suppressed, and expressed across cell types.
- It elegantly shows how the same genome, present in each cell of our bodies, is selectively harnessed to give rise to distinct cells with unique structures, functions, and behaviors.

- A surrealist depiction of epigenetics. The genome architecture, which influences gene regulatory states and expression patterns, responds to cues from some conductor – such as an environmental exposure, stressor, or developmental state. Illustration by Farah Nasser.

- In the June 2025 issue on Epigenetics, we present 13 manuscripts and one interview that encompass both fundamental and translational perspectives in the growing field of epigenetics.
- We hope this collection will deepen your understanding of how epigenetics is the foundation for optimal cell functioning across the tree of life, while also guiding future strategies for disease detection, prevention, and treatment.

- A selection of manuscripts engages with epigenetics through the lens of disease, seeking out improved clinical outcomes.
- Mc Auley and Morgan consider the applications of DNA methylation and epigenetic clocks as potential biomarkers and diagnostic tools for age-related diseases.
- Understanding how epigenetic mechanisms influence the efficacy of bone healing and may optimize fracture repair therapies is the focus of Iyer’s review.
- Holvoet dives deeper into the effect of microRNAs on T-cell differentiation within the context of cardio-metabolic and autoimmune diseases.

- While others also tackle epigenetics and disease, their focus shifts to precision medicine and the clinical applications of biomarkers.
- Ahmad and co-authors explore the strengths and current challenges in employing nanotechnology for DNA methylation modulator delivery in precision oncology.
- Contributing to the emerging area of pharmacoepigenetics, Servian et al. review novel and promising epigenetic biomarkers to predict response to inflammatory bowel disease treatments.
- In a bioinformatics analysis, Wang et al. analyze MEF-2 gene variants as a clinically valuable biomarker for the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of non-small cell lung cancer.
- Acharya and Zannas examine the association between epigenetic signatures of chronic stress and breast cancer as a potential diagnostic biomarker.
- To analyze the genome for methylation patterns associated with certain dietary intakes, Asif et al. meticulously review epigenome-wide association studies (EWASs).

- Another facet of the field seeks to understand how external stimuli and environmental exposures influence the epigenetic makeup of cells.
- Several groups study this intersection.
- Moving past gene-level transcriptomics, Núñez-Ríos and colleagues analyze differential isoform usage in postmortem brain samples of individuals with exposure to adverse childhood experiences.
- Mozhui et al. use DNA methylation biomarkers to investigate the effects of gender-affirming hormone therapy on aging.

- Transgenerational epigenetics is another growing area within the field that examines how epigenetic changes in an organism’s lifetime may be passed onto future offspring.
- The deputy editors interviewed Dr.
- Bluma Lesch, providing a glimpse behind the scenes into ongoing research projects that investigate epigenetics across generations and through evolutionary time.
- Cheong continues along the same thread of transgenerational epigenetics by reviewing plant-specific mechanisms of epigenetic inheritance in the popular model organism, Arabidopsis thaliana.
- Although well established in plants, a growing body of research is now providing increasing evidence for transgenerational epigenetic effects in mammals.
- Watkins and co-authors take a longitudinal lens to examine an association between smoking in ancestors and obesity in following generations in a UK-based cohort.

- Finally, Guevara et al. optimize a protocol to isolate nuclei from non-human primate postmortem brain tissue. Such methods can be applied to precious frozen samples and coupled with single-cell sequencing, to research cell type-specific epigenetic alterations.

- Epigenetics has undergone an extraordinary evolution over the last half-century and we are confident that the field will continue to advance rapidly.
- The development of high-throughput technologies, at single-cell and single-nuclei resolution, has the potential to reveal epigenetic landscapes that allow for unique cell types and cell states to emerge from the same genome.
- The ability to tailor therapeutics for complex diseases based on a patient’s epigenetic biomarkers holds promise for the future of precision medicine.
- Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance, though seemingly controversial in mammals, is a topic of active discussion in the field, and may explain how mammals’ interactions with their environments have long-lasting, heritable effects across generations.
- We hope that the June 2025 issue leaves you with some answers and even more questions, just as these manuscripts have for us.
- May it invite you to contribute to the future of the field of epigenetics.
