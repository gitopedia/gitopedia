# Double-Slit Experiment
- The double-slit experiment demonstrates wave-particle duality, showing that particles like photons can exhibit both wave-like and particle-like behavior.
- Thomas Young first described the experiment in 1802, involving two slits and a screen to observe interference patterns.
- When no detectors are present, single photons produce an interference pattern over time, suggesting they behave as waves.
- Introducing detectors to measure which slit a photon passes through forces it to behave like a particle, destroying the interference pattern.
- The experiment highlights the role of Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle, where measuring position (slit identity) increases uncertainty in momentum (direction), affecting the observed pattern.

# Heisenberg’s Uncertainty Principle
- The principle states that precise simultaneous measurement of both position and momentum is impossible.
- In the double-slit experiment, reducing uncertainty about slit identity (position) increases uncertainty in momentum, blurring the interference pattern into a diffraction envelope.
- This trade-off between position and momentum uncertainties underpins the observed behavior in quantum systems.

# Wheeler’s Delayed-Choice Experiment
- The experiment involves making a measurement decision after a photon has passed through the slits but before it reaches the screen or detectors.
- If the screen is left in place, an interference pattern (wave-like behavior) is observed. If detectors are revealed, a diffraction envelope (particle-like behavior) is seen.
- This demonstrates that the choice of measurement affects how the photon behaved, even after it has passed the slits.

# Temporal Reach and Interpretation
- The delayed-choice experiment suggests that decisions made now can influence past events, potentially reaching back billions of years if the slit-screen distance is extremely large.
- However, interpretations of the experiment vary among physicists, with some questioning whether it literally edits history or reflects alternative physical frameworks.

# Conclusion
- The double-slit experiment and its extensions reveal fundamental aspects of quantum mechanics, including wave-particle duality and the implications of measurement in quantum systems.
