**Extracted Facts:**

1. **Entanglement Generation Circuit:**
   - The circuit involving a Hadamard gate (H) and a controlled-NOT (c-NOT) gate generates entanglement.
   - Input state: \( |0\rangle|0\rangle \)
   - After H gate: \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle + |1\rangle)|0\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|0\rangle|0\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1\rangle|0\rangle \)
   - After c-NOT gate: \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|0\rangle|0\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1\rangle|1\rangle \)
   - Resulting entangled output: \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle) \)

2. **Bell States:**
   - The Bell states are generated by the above circuit and form an orthonormal basis in the Hilbert space of two qubits.
   - Standard notation:
     - \( |\Phi^+\rangle = |\psi_{00}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle) \)
     - \( |\Psi^+\rangle = |\psi_{01}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|01\rangle + |10\rangle) \)
     - \( |\Phi^-\rangle = |\psi_{10}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle - |11\rangle) \)
     - \( |\Psi^-\rangle = |\psi_{11}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|01\rangle - |10\rangle) \)

3. **Maximally Entangled States:**
   - Bell states are maximally entangled, meaning their reduced density matrices are maximally mixed.
   - This property implies that measurements on these states yield completely random outcomes.

4. **Measurement in Bell Basis:**
   - To measure a state in the Bell basis, one can reverse the circuit (run it from right to left) and then perform standard qubit-by-qubit measurement in the computational basis.

5. **Amplitude Calculation:**
   - For any two-qubit state \( |\psi\rangle \), the amplitude \( \langle\psi_{ij}|\psi\rangle \) can be written as \( \langle ij|U^\dagger|\psi\rangle \), where \( U^\dagger \) is such that \( |\psi_{ij}\rangle = U|ij\rangle \).

6. **Historical Note:**
   - John Stewart Bell (1928–1990) was a Northern Irish physicist whose work is referenced in the context of entanglement and quantum mechanics.

These facts highlight the fundamental concepts of entanglement, Bell states, and their properties, as well as the practical aspects of generating and measuring these states.
