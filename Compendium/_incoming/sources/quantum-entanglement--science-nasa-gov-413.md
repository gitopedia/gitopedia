# What is Quantum Entanglement?
- Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where particles of the same origin remain connected even after separating and moving far apart in time and space.
- These entangled particles maintain a shared quantum state, meaning that an event affecting one particle will instantaneously influence its entangled counterpart regardless of distance.

# A "Spooky" Science
- Albert Einstein first described quantum entanglement as "spooky action at a distance" in 1935.
- This phenomenon appeared to conflict with Einstein's theory of special relativity, which states that nothing can travel faster than the speed of light (E=mc²).
- John Stewart Bell later developed a method to test quantum entanglement, enabling scientists to confirm its existence decades after it was first proposed.

# Classical vs. Quantum Physics
- Classical physics governs predictable interactions, such as an apple remaining on a table unless moved by external forces.
- In the quantum world, particles can exhibit non-intuitive behaviors like superposition (existing in multiple states simultaneously) and entanglement.
- Entangled particles are inextricably linked, meaning their states are correlated regardless of distance.

# Quantum Entanglement's Role in Modern Science
- NASA is exploring quantum entanglement through experiments such as the SEAQUE (Space Entanglement and Annealing Quantum Experiment).
- The Cold Atom Lab enables researchers to study atoms defying gravity under extreme conditions.
- NASA demonstrated an "ultra-cool" quantum sensor for the first time in space, advancing our understanding of quantum phenomena.

# Benefits of Studying Quantum Entanglement
- Research into quantum entanglement could lead to breakthroughs in quantum computing and communication.
- Understanding these phenomena is essential for advancing space exploration and improving life on Earth.
- The Biological and Physical Sciences Division at NASA pioneers discoveries by studying extreme environments, enhancing our knowledge of the universe and its applications.
