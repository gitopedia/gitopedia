Here is a list of extracted facts from the provided text:

1. **Decoherence**:
   - Decoherence is a process where quantum systems interact with their environment, leading to the loss of coherence and the emergence of classical behavior.
   - It explains how quantum systems transition from quantum to classical states due to interactions with the environment.

2. **Key Concepts**:
   - **Pointer States**: These are stable states in measurement devices that correspond to classical outcomes.
   - **Superselection Rules**: These rules prevent certain quantum states from being observed, effectively restricting the observable outcomes to specific states.
   - **Einselection**: A process where the environment selects specific pointer states, leading to the appearance of classical behavior.

3. **Theories and Interpretations**:
   - **Everettian Quantum Mechanics (Many Worlds)**: Suggests that all possible quantum outcomes occur in parallel universes.
   - **Bohmian Mechanics**: A hidden variable theory that provides a deterministic interpretation of quantum mechanics.
   - **Modal Interpretations**: Focus on the possible states and properties of quantum systems, rather than focusing on probabilities.

4. **Historical Figures**:
   - **Wheeler and Zurek**: Pioneered the concept of decoherence and its role in the transition from quantum to classical physics.
   - **Zeh**: Contributed significantly to the understanding of decoherence and its implications for the interpretation of quantum mechanics.
   - **Wallace**: Worked on the foundations of statistical mechanics and probability in quantum theory.

5. **Applications**:
   - Decoherence is relevant in various fields, including quantum computing, where it poses challenges due to environmental interactions affecting qubits.

6. **Philosophical Implications**:
   - Decoherence challenges traditional interpretations of quantum mechanics, such as the Copenhagen interpretation, by providing a more natural explanation for the classical behavior of macroscopic systems.
   - It raises questions about the role of observation and consciousness in quantum theory.

7. **Key Publications**:
   - Works by Zeh, Zurek, and Wallace are fundamental in understanding decoherence and its implications.
   - The book "Quantum Theory and Measurement" edited by Wheeler and Zurek is a seminal work on the subject.

8. **Other Resources**:
   - The Decoherence Website maintained by Erich Joos provides extensive resources and links to related research.
   - The arXiv.org e-Print archive and the Pittsburgh Phil-Sci Archive are valuable sources for preprints and discussions on decoherence.

9. **Contributors and Discussions**:
   - Many physicists and philosophers, including Marcus Appleby, Simon Saunders, and David Wallace, have contributed to the understanding of decoherence through their research and discussions.
