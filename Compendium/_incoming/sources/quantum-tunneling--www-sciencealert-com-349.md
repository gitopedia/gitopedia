# Quantum Tunneling Experiments Earn Team The Nobel Prize in Physics
- Briton John Clarke, Frenchman Michel Devoret, and American John Martinis won the Nobel Prize in Physics for their work on quantum mechanics and its applications.
- Their research enabled advancements in digital technology, including cellphones, quantum cryptography, quantum computers, and quantum sensors.
- Quantum mechanics explains how particles behave differently at incredibly small scales. For example, a particle can pass through a wall (tunnelling) unlike a normal ball that bounces back.
- The scientists demonstrated quantum tunnelling on a macroscopic scale using superconductors in experiments during the 1980s.
- Ulf Danielsson, secretary of the Nobel physics committee, highlighted that their work brought quantum phenomena to scales humans can measure.
- Olle Eriksson, chair of the Nobel Committee for Physics, emphasized the foundational role of quantum mechanics in all digital technology.
- John Clarke is a professor at the University of California, Berkeley, aged 83.
- Michel Devoret is a professor at the University of California, Santa Barbara, and an emeritus professor at Yale University, aged 72.
- John Martinis is also a professor at the University of California, Santa Barbara, born in 1958.
- John Clarke described winning the Nobel Prize as "the surprise of my life."
- The scientists focused on the physics of their experiments without initially realizing the practical applications.
- Clarke noted that his mobile phone's functionality relies on their discoveries.
- The trio's research was conducted in the United States, which traditionally dominates Nobel science prizes due to significant investment in basic science and academic freedom.
- Eleanor Crane of King's College London pointed out that Devoret moving to the US exemplified a "brain drain."
- However, she noted that this trend is being reversed with a new administration.
- Trump's budget cuts to science programmes were criticized by Clarke as potentially disastrous for research.
- The physics prize was awarded after the medicine prize, which honoured discoveries in the human immune system.
- The Nobel Prize consists of a diploma, a gold medal, and a $1.2 million cheque, shared among winners if applicable.
- Laureates receive their prizes at formal ceremonies on December 10, the anniversary of Alfred Nobel's death in 1896.
