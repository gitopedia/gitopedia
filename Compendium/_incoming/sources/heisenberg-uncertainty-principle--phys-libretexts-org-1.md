# Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
- The Heisenberg uncertainty principle is a key principle in quantum mechanics.
- It states that the product of the uncertainty in position (Δx) and momentum (Δp) of a particle cannot be less than one-half of the reduced Planck constant: ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2.
- For a free particle, if the uncertainty in position is infinite, the uncertainty in momentum is zero, and vice versa.
- A localized particle is modeled by a wave packet, which is a linear superposition of plane-wave states with different momenta.
- The Gaussian function provides the minimum value for the uncertainty product ΔxΔp.

# Momentum and Position
- For an electron with a speed precision of 1.0×10−3 m/s, the uncertainty in momentum (Δp) is 9.1×10−34 kg·m/s.
- The corresponding position uncertainty (Δx) is approximately 5.8 cm.
- For a bowling ball with the same speed precision, the uncertainty in momentum (Δp) is 6.0×10−3 kg·m/s.
- The corresponding position uncertainty (Δx) is approximately 8.8×10−33 m.

# Ground-State Energy of a Hydrogen Atom
- The ground-state energy of a hydrogen atom can be estimated using the uncertainty principle.
- Assuming the size of the hydrogen atom is approximately 0.1 nm, the ground-state energy (E0) is calculated to be around 1 eV.
- This estimation aligns with early experimental results.

# Energy and Time Uncertainty Principle
- The energy-time uncertainty principle states that ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2.
- A quantum state with a short lifetime has a broad spectral width, while a long-lived state has a narrow spectral width.
- For an atom with a lifetime of 10−8 seconds, the uncertainty in frequency (Δf) is approximately 8.0×106 Hz.

# Spectral Line Width
- The width of a spectral line corresponds to the uncertainty in energy and time.
- For a sodium atom emitting a photon with wavelength 589.0 nm and energy 2.105 eV, the uncertainty in energy is approximately 4.1×10−8 eV.
- The corresponding spectral line width is about 1.1×10−5 nm.

# Emitted Radiation Monochromaticity
- The emitted radiation can be considered monochromatic if the frequency spread (Δf/f) is less than 1.1×10−6 percent.
- For the sodium atom transition, the frequency spread is within this range, confirming the monochromatic nature of the emitted radiation.

# Technical Notes
- The uncertainty principle applies to all quantum systems, regardless of the precision of measurement devices.
- The Gaussian function provides the minimum value for the uncertainty product in both position and momentum.
- The energy-time uncertainty principle does not directly relate to the position-momentum uncertainty principle but shares a similar conceptual foundation.
