# Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
- The Heisenberg uncertainty principle is a key principle in quantum mechanics.
- It states that if the uncertainty in position (Δx) is small, the uncertainty in momentum (Δp) must be large, and vice versa.
- The principle also applies to other quantities such as energy and time.

# Momentum and Position Uncertainty Principle
- For a free particle moving along the x-direction, the wavefunction is uniform and independent of time.
- A localized particle is modeled by a linear superposition of plane-wave states called a wave packet.
- The product of the uncertainty in position and momentum cannot be less than one-half of the reduced Planck constant: ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2.
- The minimum uncertainty occurs for a Gaussian function, where the product of uncertainties equals ℏ/2.

# Example Calculations
- For an electron with speed precision of 1.0×10−3 m/s:
  - Δp = (9.1×10−31 kg)(1.0×10−3 m/s) = 9.1×10−34 kg·m/s.
  - Δx = ℏ/(2Δp) ≈ 5.8 cm.
- For a bowling ball with mass 6.0 kg:
  - Δp = (6.0 kg)(1.0×10−3 m/s) = 6.0×10−3 kg·m/s.
  - Δx = ℏ/(2Δp) ≈ 8.8×10−33 m.

# Ground-State Energy of a Hydrogen Atom
- The size of a hydrogen atom is approximately 0.1 nm.
- Using the uncertainty principle, the ground-state energy E₀ can be estimated as:
  - E₀ = (ℏ²)/(8mL²).
  - Substituting values: E₀ ≈ 1 eV.

# Energy-Time Uncertainty Principle
- The energy-time uncertainty principle states that ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2.
- A quantum state with a short lifetime has an uncertain energy, leading to a broad spectral line.
- Example: An atom in an excited state for Δt = 10−8 s:
  - ΔE ≈ ℏ/(2Δt).
  - For f = 7.1×10¹⁴ Hz, Δf ≈ 8.0×10⁶ Hz.
  - The emitted radiation is monochromatic as Δf/f ≈ 1.1×10−6.

# Sodium Atom Transition
- A sodium atom transitions from the first excited state to the ground state, emitting a photon with energy 2.105 eV and wavelength 589.0 nm.
- The lifetime of the excited state is Δt = 1.6×10−8 s.
- Uncertainty in energy: ΔE ≈ ℏ/(2Δt) ≈ 4.1×10−8 eV.
- Width of the spectral line: Δλ ≈ 1.1×10−5 nm.

# Key Equations
- Momentum-position uncertainty: ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2.
- Energy-time uncertainty: ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2.
- Ground-state energy: E₀ = (ℏ²)/(8mL²).

# Conclusion
- The Heisenberg uncertainty principle imposes fundamental limits on the precision of simultaneous measurements in quantum mechanics.
- These limits are not due to experimental imprecision but arise from the wave-like nature of matter and energy.
