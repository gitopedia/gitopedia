# Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
- The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle is a key principle in quantum mechanics.
- It states that if the uncertainty in position (Δx) is small, the uncertainty in momentum (Δp) must be large, and vice versa.
- Versions of the uncertainty principle also exist for other quantities such as energy and time.

# Momentum and Position
- For a free particle moving along the x-direction, the wavefunction is uniform and independent of time.
- The probability density |ψk(x,t)|² = A² is uniform, indicating equal likelihood of finding the particle anywhere along the x-axis.
- A localized particle is modeled by a linear superposition of free-particle states called a wave packet.
- The uncertainty principle limits what we can know about a particle from simultaneous measurements of position and momentum.
- The product of the uncertainties in position and momentum is given by ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2, where ℏ is the reduced Planck constant.

# Example Calculations
- For an electron with speed precision Δu = 1.0×10⁻³ m/s:
  - Δp = mΔu = (9.1×10⁻³¹ kg)(1.0×10⁻³ m/s) = 9.1×10⁻³⁴ kg·m/s
  - Δx = ℏ/(2Δp) ≈ 5.8 cm
- For a bowling ball with mass 6.0 kg:
  - Δp = (6.0 kg)(1.0×10⁻³ m/s) = 6.0×10⁻³ kg·m/s
  - Δx = ℏ/(2Δp) ≈ 8.8×10⁻³³ m

# Ground-State Energy of a Hydrogen Atom
- The size of a hydrogen atom is approximately 0.1 nm.
- Using the uncertainty principle, the ground-state energy E₀ can be estimated as:
  - E₀ = (ℏ²)/(8mL²)
  - Substituting values: E₀ ≈ 1 eV

# Energy and Time Uncertainty Principle
- The energy-time uncertainty principle is given by ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2.
- A quantum state with a short lifetime has a broad spectral width, while a long-lived state has a narrow spectral width.

# Example Calculation for Spectral Line Width
- For an atom with a lifetime Δt = 10⁻⁸ s and average frequency f = 7.1×10¹⁴ Hz:
  - ΔE ≈ ℏ/(2Δt) ≈ 8.0×10⁶ Hz
  - Δf/f ≈ 1.1×10⁻⁸, indicating the emitted radiation is monochromatic.

# Sodium Atom Transition
- A sodium atom transitions from the first excited state to the ground state, emitting a photon with energy 2.105 eV and wavelength 589.0 nm.
- The lifetime of the excited state is Δt = 1.6×10⁻⁸ s.
- The uncertainty in energy ΔE ≈ 4.1×10⁻⁸ eV.
- The width of the corresponding spectral line is approximately 1.1×10⁻⁵ nm.

# Conclusion
- The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle applies to both momentum-position and energy-time relations in quantum mechanics.
- It has significant implications for understanding the limitations of simultaneous measurements in quantum systems.
