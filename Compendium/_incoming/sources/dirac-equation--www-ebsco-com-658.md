- **Dirac Equation**: Developed by Paul Dirac in 1928, it combines quantum mechanics with special relativity and successfully describes particles like electrons, including their spin and prediction of antimatter.
- **Antimatter**: Predicted by the Dirac equation, antiparticles (e.g., positrons) are "mirror images" of matter particles, annihilating each other upon collision to release energy.
- **Hydrogen Atom**: The simplest atom, consisting of a proton and electron, was used in early quantum mechanics to test theories like the Lamb shift.
- **Planck's Constant (h)**: A fundamental constant in quantum mechanics, equal to \(6.626 \times 10^{-34}\) joule-seconds.
- **Relativistic Effects on Chemistry**: Elements with high atomic numbers (e.g., gold and mercury) exhibit chemical behavior influenced by relativistic effects due to their electrons' speeds approaching light speed.
- **Quantum Mechanics Revolution**: Began in the early 20th century with contributions from Max Planck, Niels Bohr, Werner Heisenberg, Erwin Schrödinger, and Paul Dirac.
- **Special Relativity**: Developed by Albert Einstein, it integrates space and time, leading to the equivalence of matter and energy.
- **Wavefunction**: A mathematical description of a quantum system's state, derived from wave equations like the Schrödinger equation.
- **Spinor**: A multi-component wavefunction describing particles with spin, such as electrons.
- **Quantum Field Theory**: Evolved from Dirac's work, it unifies quantum mechanics and special relativity, forming the basis of the Standard Model of particle physics.
