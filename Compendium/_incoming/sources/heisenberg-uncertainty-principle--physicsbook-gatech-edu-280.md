# Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
- The Heisenberg uncertainty principle describes uncertainty relationships between a particle's properties, such as position and momentum, as well as energy and time.
- Uncertainty principles apply to both classical and modern physics. For example, waves have uncertainty between their frequency and position.
- Quantum particles exhibit wave-particle duality, meaning they have an associated wavelength that relates to their position and momentum.
- The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle is derived from quantum mechanics, involving state vectors and operators in abstract vector spaces.
- A quantum system is represented by a "state vector," where each component corresponds to the probability of a measurement outcome.
- Measuring one property (e.g., position) affects the uncertainty of another property (e.g., momentum) due to the commutator relationship between operators.
- The minimum uncertainty between position and momentum is given by ΔpΔx ≥ ℏ/2, where ℏ = h/(2π).
- The principle can also be extended to energy and time: ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2.
- A computational model example calculates the minimum uncertainty of a particle's momentum when confined to a box 0.1 nm wide: Δp ≈ 5.27 × 10−25 kg m/s.
- For a muon traveling at 0.2c, the position uncertainty is calculated using relativistic momentum and the given momentum uncertainty: Δx ≥ 4.585 × 10−14 meters.
- A photon's wavelength uncertainty (Δλ = 2 nm) leads to an uncertainty in time of emission: Δt ≈ 3.728 × 10−14 seconds.
- The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle explains quantum tunneling, allowing particles to pass through potential barriers they would not classically overcome.
- Microelectronics are limited in size due to electron tunneling effects influenced by the uncertainty principle.
- In stellar fusion, tunneling allows hydrogen nuclei to fuse at lower energies than classical predictions, enabling stars like the Sun to produce energy.
- The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle was first demonstrated through single-slit diffraction experiments with electrons, showing the relationship between position and momentum uncertainties.
