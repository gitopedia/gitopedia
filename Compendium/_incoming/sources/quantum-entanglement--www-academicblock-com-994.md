### Extracted Facts on Quantum Entanglement and the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) Paradox

#### **Key Concepts**
- **Quantum Entanglement**: A phenomenon where pairs or groups of particles are generated in such a way that the quantum state of each particle cannot be described independently of the others, even when the particles are separated by large distances.
- **Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) Paradox**: Introduced in 1935 by Albert Einstein, Boris Podolsky, and Nathan Rosen. The paradox questions the completeness of quantum mechanics by suggesting that quantum states might have "hidden variables" that could explain entanglement without resorting to non-local influences.

#### **Non-Locality**
- Quantum entanglement implies non-local correlations between particles, meaning measurements on one particle can instantaneously affect another, regardless of distance. This violates classical notions of locality.

#### **Bell’s Theorem and Inequalities**
- **John Bell’s Theorem (1964)**: Provided a mathematical framework to test quantum mechanics against local hidden variable theories.
- **Bell’s Inequality**: Sets limits on the correlations that can arise from local realistic theories. Violations of Bell’s inequality in experiments confirm the non-local nature of entanglement.

#### **Quantum Teleportation**
- A process where the state of one particle is transferred to another distant particle without physically moving it. Relies on quantum entanglement and is fundamental to quantum information theory.

#### **Entanglement Swapping**
- Transfer of entanglement between particles that have never directly interacted, achieved through a series of measurements. Crucial for quantum communication protocols and networks.

#### **Decoherence**
- Fragility of entanglement due to interactions with the environment, leading to loss of quantum correlations. Minimizing decoherence is essential for practical quantum technologies.

#### **Applications of Quantum Entanglement**
1. **Quantum Cryptography**: Enables secure communication through Quantum Key Distribution (QKD), where eavesdropping disrupts entanglement.
2. **Quantum Computing**: Utilizes entangled qubits to perform computations exponentially faster than classical computers for certain tasks.
3. **Quantum Teleportation**: Transfers quantum states over long distances, enabling new methods of secure communication and quantum networking.
4. **Quantum Sensors**: Develops highly sensitive sensors for metrology, navigation, and imaging, with applications in medical diagnostics and environmental monitoring.
5. **Quantum Key Distribution Networks**: Scalable networks for secure communication across multiple parties, leveraging entanglement for confidentiality and integrity.

#### **Fundamental Physics**
- Experiments testing Bell’s inequalities have confirmed the non-local correlations predicted by quantum mechanics, ruling out certain classical theories and deepening our understanding of reality's fundamental principles.

#### **Biological Implications**
- Some researchers propose that quantum entanglement may play a role in biological processes like photosynthesis and bird navigation, though this remains controversial.

#### **Entanglement in Multiple Degrees of Freedom**
- Entanglement is not limited to spin or polarization; it can occur in momentum, position, and orbital angular momentum. Explored for advanced quantum information processing.
