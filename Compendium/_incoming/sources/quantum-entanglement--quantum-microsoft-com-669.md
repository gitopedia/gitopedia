# Entanglement is a fundamental concept of quantum mechanics that describes a non-classical correlation, or shared quantum state, between two or more quantum systems (or quantum particles) even if they are separated by a large distance. This phenomenon is also known as quantum non-locality, and it is one of the key features of quantum mechanics that distinguishes it from classical mechanics.

- Entanglement describes a non-classical correlation between two or more quantum systems.
- Quantum systems are described by a wavefunction containing information about possible measurement outcomes.
- When entangled, their wavefunction cannot be expressed as a product of individual wavefunctions for each system.
- Instead, they are described by a single wavefunction capturing the correlation between them.

# In quantum computing, entanglement is used to enable quantum parallelism, which allows quantum computers to perform multiple calculations simultaneously.

- Entanglement enables quantum parallelism in quantum computing.
- Quantum computers manipulate many qubits in a single operation, unlike classical systems that process each qubit individually.

# For example, consider two qubits initially prepared in an entangled state. If a measurement is made on one of the qubits and it is found to be in the state |0⟩, the state of the other qubit immediately collapses to |0⟩ as well. Similarly, if the first qubit is measured to be in the state |1⟩, the second qubit collapses to |1⟩.

- Measuring one entangled qubit affects the state of the other.
- If one qubit is measured as |0⟩, the other collapses to |0⟩.
- If one qubit is measured as |1⟩, the other collapses to |1⟩.

# Entanglement enables quantum computers to implement various protocols and algorithms that are not possible with classical systems.

- Entanglement allows for implementing quantum-specific protocols and algorithms.
- Examples include quantum teleportation and quantum error correction.

# For example, it is used in quantum teleportation, which allows for the transfer of quantum states between two distant systems.

- Quantum teleportation transfers quantum states between distant systems using entanglement.

# Entanglement is also a key resource for quantum error correction, which is necessary to protect quantum information from decoherence and other errors.

- Entanglement supports quantum error correction.
- It helps detect and correct errors in quantum information processing.

# By creating and manipulating entangled states, quantum computers can detect and correct errors in a way that is not possible for classical computers.

- Manipulating entangled states enables advanced error detection and correction in quantum computing.

# Learn how quantum interference is used in quantum computation to amplify certain outcomes and suppress others.

- Quantum interference amplifies desired outcomes and suppresses undesired ones in quantum computations.

- Single qubit gates are foundational components of complex quantum circuits.
- They perform basic operations on individual qubits.

- Multi-qubit gates entangle qubits and manipulate their states.
- Entanglement is a key function of these gates in quantum computing.
