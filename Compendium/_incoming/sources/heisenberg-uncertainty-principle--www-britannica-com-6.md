# Quantum Mechanics

## Overview
Quantum mechanics is a branch of physics that studies the behavior of matter and energy at atomic and subatomic scales. Unlike classical physics, which describes the macroscopic world, quantum mechanics deals with phenomena such as wave-particle duality and quantization, where energy is exchanged in discrete amounts called quanta.

## Historical Development
The foundation of quantum mechanics was laid during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Initially, physicists believed light consisted of particles, but experiments like those by Thomas Young and Augustin-Jean Fresnel demonstrated wave-like properties through interference and diffraction. However, classical physics failed to explain blackbody radiation, leading Max Planck to propose that energy is emitted in discrete quanta in 1900. This discovery marked the beginning of quantum theory.

## Key Concepts
- **Quantization**: Energy is exchanged in discrete packets or quanta.
- **Wave-Particle Duality**: Particles like electrons exhibit both wave-like and particle-like properties.
- **Measurement Paradoxes**: Quantum mechanics introduces uncertainties when measuring systems, as measurements disturb the system being observed.

## Applications and Impact
Quantum mechanics has revolutionized our understanding of the atomic world and underpins many modern technologies. Its principles have been applied successfully across various fields, though specific applications are not detailed in this context.

## Interpretations and Paradoxes
A central paradox in quantum mechanics is the disturbance caused by measurement, which remains unresolved. This issue challenges the foundation of the theory and continues to attract scientific inquiry.
