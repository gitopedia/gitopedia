# Quantum Superposition
- Quantum superposition is a phenomenon where a quantum particle can exist in multiple states simultaneously until it is observed.
- In classical physics, objects are either in one state or another (e.g., a light switch is on or off), but in quantum mechanics, particles like electrons, photons, and atoms can be in multiple states at once.
- The concept of superposition was introduced by Erwin Schrödinger through his thought experiment involving a cat in a box, which illustrates how a particle can exist in a superposition of states until observed.

# What is Quantum Superposition?
- A quantum particle described by a wave equation can exist in multiple states simultaneously, with the probabilities of its properties determined by this equation.
- Schrödinger’s Cat is a thought experiment where a cat in a box is considered to be both alive and dead until the box is opened and observed.

# Experimental Observations
- The double-slit experiment demonstrates quantum superposition: photons fired through two slits create an interference pattern, indicating that each photon passes through both slits simultaneously.
- When measuring which slit a particle goes through, the superposition collapses, and the particle appears to have passed through one slit.
- Ions, larger molecules, and chlorophyll in plants have been observed in superposed states.

# Importance in Quantum Computing
- Qubits, such as electrons or photons, can exist in a superposition of both 0 and 1, allowing quantum computers to process multiple states simultaneously.
- Three qubits can represent all eight possible states at once, significantly increasing computational power compared to classical computers.
- This capability could revolutionize fields like pharmaceuticals, climate modeling, and manufacturing by solving complex problems much faster than traditional computers.

# World Quantum Day
- World Quantum Day is an annual event held on April 14 to promote public understanding of quantum science.
- The date 4/14 corresponds to the first three digits of Planck’s constant (4.14 x 10^-15), a key number in quantum physics.

# Related Developments

- A "physics shortcut" enables laptops to solve quantum problems previously reserved for supercomputers and AI.
- Quantum computers inspired by Schrödinger’s Cat could potentially become error-proof and more powerful.
