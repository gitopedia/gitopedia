# Schrödinger's Cat Thought Experiment
- The experiment involves placing a cat inside a box with a vial of poisonous gas.
- A mallet is set up to break the vial if a radioactive atom decays, leading to the cat's death.
- The quantum mechanical behavior determines whether the cat lives or dies.
- Before the box is opened, the cat is simultaneously alive and dead according to quantum mechanics.

# Quantum States and Superposition
- Physical systems can be in discrete states (e.g., spin up or spin down for an electron).
- Superposition allows particles to exist in a combination of states until observed.
- The Schrödinger equation describes how quantum states evolve over time.
- Observing a system causes it to collapse into a single state according to the Copenhagen Interpretation.

# Many-Worlds Interpretation
- The thought experiment highlights issues with the Copenhagen Interpretation regarding observer perspectives.
- The Many-Worlds Interpretation rejects collapse and instead proposes multiple parallel worlds (world-branches).
- Each world corresponds to a possible outcome of the experiment (cat alive or dead).
- Opening the box creates a superposition of two worlds, each with its own version of the observer.
