# Introduction to Superposition
- Quantum superposition is the framework for understanding all quantum phenomena.
- The concept of superposition may seem confusing due to its unintuitive nature in everyday life.
- Experiments conclusively show that the universe operates according to the law of quantum superposition at the smallest distances.
- The term “superposition” has different contexts in classical and quantum physics.

# Classical Superposition
- In classical physics, superposition describes when two physical quantities are added together to create a third quantity.
- An example is waves passing through each other, resulting in constructive or destructive interference (e.g., noise-canceling headphones).
- Another application involves calculating the total electric force on a charge by summing forces from individual charges using vector addition.

# Quantum Superposition
- Quantum superposition applies to systems like nuclei, electrons, photons, and atoms.
- Energy levels in quantum systems are quantized, meaning they can only take specific values (e.g., E = 0, 1, 2, ...).
- A coin analogy illustrates superposition: a tossed coin has a 50% chance of landing heads or tails, representing a superposition state until measured.
- Schrödinger’s cat thought experiment highlights that a quantum system can be in a superposition state (alive and dead) until measured.

# Properties of Quantum Superposition
1. A particle exists as a combination of different states simultaneously.
2. Each possible state has a probability of being observed, but measurement collapses the superposition into a definite state.

# Applications and Examples
- The concept of superposition is explored in quantum tic-tac-toe and other activities.
- Bell’s inequality experiments demonstrate that particles can be in two locations at the same time.
- The dress controversy (blue/black vs. white/gold) is used to discuss whether perception differences imply a quantum superposition.
