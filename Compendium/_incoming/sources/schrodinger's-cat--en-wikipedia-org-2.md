### Extracted Facts:

1. **Origin of the Thought Experiment**:
   - Schrödinger's cat was introduced by Erwin Schrödinger in 1935 as a thought experiment to illustrate the paradoxical nature of quantum superposition.
   - The experiment involves a cat placed in a box with a radioactive atom, a Geiger counter, and a vial of poison. If the atom decays, the Geiger counter triggers the release of poison, leading to the cat's death.

2. **Quantum Superposition**:
   - The cat is described as being simultaneously alive and dead until the box is opened and the state is observed.
   - This thought experiment highlights the paradox in quantum mechanics where particles can exist in multiple states until measured.

3. **Interpretations of Quantum Mechanics**:
   - **Copenhagen Interpretation**: Suggests that the cat is neither alive nor dead before observation, with the wavefunction representing possible outcomes.
   - **Many-Worlds Interpretation**: Proposes that every possible outcome branches into separate parallel universes, so the cat is both alive and dead in different realities.
   - **Objective Collapse Theories**: Suggest that superpositions collapse spontaneously when a certain threshold is reached, meaning the cat would settle into one state before observation.

4. **Applications and Tests**:
   - While the experiment itself has not been conducted with actual cats, similar principles have been tested with smaller objects like photons, beryllium ions, and piezoelectric resonators.
   - These experiments aim to push the boundaries of quantum superposition to larger scales.

5. **Popular Culture**:
   - The concept of Schrödinger's cat has become widely popularized in media, including science fiction novels, films, poetry, and music.
   - Notable works include Ursula K. Le Guin's short story "Schrödinger's Cat" and various science fiction stories that use the idea as a plot device or metaphor.

6. **Historical Context**:

7. **Quantum Computing**:
