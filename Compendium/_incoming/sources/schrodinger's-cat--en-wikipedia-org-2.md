### Extracted Facts:

1. **Origin of the Thought Experiment**:
   - Schrödinger's cat is a thought experiment introduced by physicist Erwin Schrödinger in 1935 to illustrate the paradoxical nature of quantum superposition.

2. **Objective**:
   - The experiment aims to highlight the apparent contradiction between the quantum description of physical reality and everyday experience, emphasizing the collapse of the wavefunction upon measurement.

3. **Description of the Experiment**:
   - A cat is placed in a box along with a radioactive atom, a Geiger counter, and a vial of poison.
   - If the atom decays, it triggers the release of the poison, killing the cat.
   - Until the box is opened, quantum mechanics describes the cat as being simultaneously alive and dead (a superposition of states).

4. **Interpretations**:
   - **Copenhagen Interpretation**: The cat is in a superposition until observed; observation causes the wavefunction to collapse into one state.
   - **Many-Worlds Interpretation**: Both states persist, with the observer existing in separate universes depending on the outcome.
   - **Ensemble Interpretation**: Focuses on statistical ensembles rather than individual outcomes, rendering the paradox moot.
   - **Relational Interpretation**: Observers' perspectives determine the state, with no absolute reality until observation.
   - **Objective Collapse Theories**: Superpositions collapse spontaneously based on physical thresholds.

5. **Applications and Tests**:
   - While the original experiment is theoretical, similar principles have been tested with smaller systems like photons, beryllium ions, and piezoelectric resonators.
   - These experiments aim to push the boundaries of quantum superposition towards macroscopic objects.

6. **Popular Culture**:
   - The concept gained prominence in popular culture through science fiction works, including novels, films, and television shows.
   - Notable mentions include Ursula K. Le Guin's short story "Schrödinger's Cat" and various science-fiction titles referencing the thought experiment.

7. **Historical Context**:
   - The thought experiment did not become widely known until the 1970s, with its popularity in popular culture increasing thereafter.

8. **Quantum Computing**:
   - In quantum computing, "cat states" refer to specific superposition states used as qubit configurations, such as GHZ states.

9. **Potential Future Developments**:
   - Proposals suggest experiments involving larger objects like viruses or bacteria, potentially leading to macroscopic superpositions.
