**Extracted Facts:**

1. **Schrödinger's Cat Thought Experiment:**
   - A theoretical experiment proposed by physicist Erwin Schrödinger to illustrate the paradoxical nature of quantum superposition.
   - Involves a cat in a box with a radioactive atom, where the cat is considered simultaneously alive and dead until observed.

2. **Quantum Superposition:**
   - The principle that a quantum system can exist in multiple states at once until measured.
   - Applied to macroscopic objects like the cat to highlight the apparent paradoxes in quantum mechanics.

3. **Interpretations of Quantum Mechanics:**
   - **Copenhagen Interpretation:** Suggests that the cat is either alive or dead, with the superposition being a mathematical construct without physical meaning.
   - **Many-Worlds Interpretation:** Proposes that both states (alive and dead) exist in separate universes, preventing interaction due to decoherence.
   - **Ensemble Interpretation:** Considers superpositions as statistical ensembles rather than individual states, making the paradox trivial.
   - **Relational Interpretation:** Focuses on relative information between observers, allowing different accounts of the same event.
   - **Transactional Interpretation:** Involves advanced and retarded waves forming standing waves, with no actual superposition experienced by the cat.
   - **Objective Collapse Theories:** Suggest spontaneous collapse of superpositions based on physical thresholds.

4. **Applications and Tests:**
   - While a literal experiment is theoretical, similar principles have been tested with smaller objects like photons, beryllium ions, and piezoelectric resonators.
   - "Cat states" refer to superposition states in quantum systems, often short-lived and requiring specific conditions.

5. **Popular Culture:**
   - The concept became widely known in the 1970s and gained popularity through science fiction works, including novels, films, and other media.
   - Ursula K. Le Guin's work is credited with introducing it to popular culture.

6. **Historical Context:**
   - Schrödinger introduced the thought experiment in 1935 to challenge the Copenhagen interpretation and emphasize the need for a more complete theory of quantum mechanics.

7. **Cultural Impact:**
   - The cat has become a prominent symbol in various media, often used humorously or metaphorically to explore themes of uncertainty and parallel realities.
