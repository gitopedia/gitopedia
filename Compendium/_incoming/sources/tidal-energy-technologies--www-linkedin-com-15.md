### **Tidal and Wave Energy Overview**
- **Tidal Energy**: Generated by the gravitational pull of the moon and sun, causing predictable tidal movements. It is a reliable renewable energy source with minimal environmental impact.
- **Wave Energy**: Harnessed from the kinetic energy of ocean waves. It is more variable than tidal energy but offers potential for decentralized power generation.

---

### **Key Examples and Initiatives**
1. **United Kingdom**:
   - **MeyGen Project**: Scotland's largest tidal energy array, producing 150 MW of power.
   - **Wave Energy**: Projects like the **CETO system** (Carnegie Clean Energy) use submerged buoys to convert wave motion into electricity.
   - **Policy Support**: The UK government has invested in marine energy research and infrastructure to integrate tidal/wave energy into the national grid.

2. **Global Developments**:
   - **France**: The **Sihem** tidal plant in the Bay of Biscay (Brittany) is a notable example.
   - **Canada**: Projects like **Ocean Renewable Power Company** (ORPC) are exploring tidal and wave energy in coastal regions.
   - **Australia**: The **CETO system** is also being tested in South Australia.

---

### **Challenges and Considerations**
- **Technical Barriers**: High costs, durability issues in harsh marine environments, and the need for advanced materials.
- **Environmental Impact**: Potential disruption to marine ecosystems, though generally less than fossil fuels.
- **Intermittency**: Wave energy is less predictable than tidal energy, requiring grid integration solutions.
- **Economic Viability**: Current projects are often small-scale or experimental, with limited commercial scalability.

---

### **Future Prospects**
- **Hybrid Systems**: Combining tidal and wave energy with other renewables (e.g., wind, solar) to stabilize power output.
- **Innovation**: Advances in materials science and AI-driven energy management could reduce costs and improve efficiency.
- **Policy and Funding**: Continued government support and private investment are critical for scaling up marine energy projects.
