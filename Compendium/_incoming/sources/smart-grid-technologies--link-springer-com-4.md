- 8 Future trends and strategic implementations

- The smart grid is on the threshold of revolutionary change, fueled by the growing need for sustainable, resilient and efficient energy systems.
- Emerging trends reflect a deep-seated movement towards increased intelligence, autonomy and resilience, radically transforming the energy environment.
- Foremost among these trends is ubiquitous digitalization, harnessing cutting edge technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML), and the internet of things (IoT) for real time tracking, predictive maintenance and maximum energy efficiency throughout the grid spectrum [152].
- Digitization is critical to integrate growing renewable energy sources and to facilitate dynamic demand response functionality.
- Simultaneously, decarburization will speed the integration of heterogeneous distributed energy resources including solar, wind, and battery storage, making the grid manage increasingly intermittent and decentralized power streams.
- This trend towards cleaner energy naturally encourages decentralization, which breeds the spread of microgrids and energy communities able to operate in isolated or combined mode with the central grid, increasing sophisticated communication infrastructures and transactive energy systems allowing peer to peer trading and active engagement in grid management [153].

- Strategic deployments are quickly transforming to achieve these future developments, advancing beyond conceptual designs to real worlds deployments.
- Sophisticated AI/ML powered analytics will become an essential tool for advanced load forecasting, anomaly detection and self-healing grid capabilities, enabling the grid to forecast and act on disturbances with minimal intervention from humans [152].
- Recent ML based approaches for fault detection, load forecasting and adaptive control in DERs are extremely essential, as the power grids with integrated DERs requires advanced techniques to ensure reliability, stability and efficiency.
- Machine learning has emerged as a powerful tool to enhance fault detection, load forecasting and also adaptive control in DER systems.

- Fault Detection in DERs: The fault detection is crucial for maintaining operational integrity and to prevent cascading failures in DERs.
- Since the traditional rule based approaches being insufficient due to complexity and variability of modern DERs, recent ML based techniques, such as hybrid deep learning models combining convolutional neural networks (CNN) and long short-term memory networks (LSTM), are capable to extract both the spatial and also the temporal features to detect faults with high accuracy [154].
- The unsupervised learning methods, including auto encoders, also are employed for anomaly detection in energy storage systems and microgrids, enabling fault identification without requiring labeled data [155].
- These methods significantly improve early detection capabilities, minimizing downtime and maintenance costs.
