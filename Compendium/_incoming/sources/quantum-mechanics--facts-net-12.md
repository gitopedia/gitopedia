# Quantum Mechanics

## Overview
Quantum Mechanics is a foundational theory in physics that describes the behavior of particles at the atomic and subatomic levels. Unlike classical physics, which treats particles as solid objects, Quantum Mechanics views them as wave functions, providing probabilities of where particles might be found. This model explains phenomena that classical physics cannot, such as electron behavior in atoms. Developed in the early 20th century by scientists like Schrödinger and Heisenberg, it revolutionized our understanding of the microscopic world and is crucial for technologies like semiconductors, lasers, and quantum computing.

## Historical Development
The development of Quantum Mechanics involved contributions from several key figures:
- **Max Planck** introduced the quantum theory in 1900, suggesting energy is quantized.
- **Albert Einstein** further developed this with his theory of photons.
- **Niels Bohr** proposed the Bohr model of the atom in 1915.
- **Erwin Schrödinger** formulated the wave equation describing quantum states.
- **Werner Heisenberg** introduced the uncertainty principle and matrix mechanics.

## Key Concepts
- **Wave-Particle Duality**: Particles exhibit both wave-like and particle-like properties.
- **Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle**: Certain pairs of physical quantities cannot be simultaneously measured precisely.
- **Schrödinger Equation**: Describes how quantum states evolve over time.
- **Quantum Superposition**: A system can exist in multiple states until measured.
- **Entanglement**: Particles can be linked, with the state of one affecting another regardless of distance.

## Applications
Quantum Mechanics has led to technologies such as:
- **Semiconductors**: Underpin modern electronics like transistors.
- **Lasers**: Used in communication, medicine, and technology.
- **MRI Machines**: Utilize quantum principles for medical imaging.
- **Quantum Computing**: Promises solving complex problems beyond classical computers.

## Misconceptions
Common misunderstandings include:
- **Determinism vs. Probability**: Quantum Mechanics is probabilistic, not deterministic.
- **Observer Effect**: Observation refers to any interaction, not necessarily a conscious observer.
- **Entanglement Communication**: Entanglement does not allow faster-than-light communication but shows correlations.
