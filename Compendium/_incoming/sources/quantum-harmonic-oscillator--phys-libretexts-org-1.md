# The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator
- A simple harmonic oscillator is a particle or system that undergoes harmonic motion about an equilibrium position, such as an object with mass vibrating on a spring. 
- In classical mechanics, the particle moves in response to a linear restoring force given by \( F_x = -kx \), where \( x \) is the displacement of the particle from its equilibrium position.
- The total energy \( E \) of an oscillator is the sum of its kinetic energy \( K = \frac{1}{2}mu^2 \) and the elastic potential energy of the force \( U(x) = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 \).
- The allowed energies for a quantum harmonic oscillator are given by \( E_n = (n + \frac{1}{2})\hbar\omega \), where \( n \) is the principal quantum number.
- The wavefunctions corresponding to these energies are \( \psi_n(x) = N_n e^{-\beta^2 x^2 / 2} H_n(\beta x) \), where \( \beta = m\omega/\hbar \), \( N_n \) is the normalization constant, and \( H_n(y) \) is a Hermite polynomial.
- The energy spacing between adjacent levels is \( \Delta E = \hbar\omega \).
- The vibrational frequency of hydrogen chloride (HCl) is \( f = 8.88 \times 10^{13} \, Hz \), and the force constant \( k \) for the HCl molecule is approximately \( 520 \, N/m \).
- The energy of the emitted photon when the HCl molecule transitions between adjacent vibrational energy levels is \( \Delta E = h f = 0.368 \, eV \).

# Differences Between Classical and Quantum Harmonic Oscillators
- The ground state energy of a quantum oscillator is \( E_0 = \frac{\hbar\omega}{2} \), unlike the classical case where the lowest energy is zero.
- A quantum particle can be found with nonzero probability outside the classically forbidden region, such as in the region beyond the turning points.
- The probability density distribution for a quantum oscillator in the ground state peaks at the equilibrium position \( x = 0 \), whereas for a classical oscillator, the probability is highest near the turning points.

# Applications and Examples
- The vibrational frequency of hydrogen iodide (HI) is \( f = 6.69 \times 10^{13} \, Hz \).
- The force constant \( k \) for the molecular bond in HI can be calculated using the relation \( k = m\omega^2 \), where \( m \) is the reduced mass of the system.
- The energy of the emitted photon when HI transitions between adjacent vibrational energy levels is also given by \( \Delta E = h f \).

# Symmetry and Expectation Values
- Using symmetry considerations, the expectation value of the position \( \langle x \rangle \) for a particle in the ground state of a harmonic oscillator is zero due to the even nature of the wavefunction.
