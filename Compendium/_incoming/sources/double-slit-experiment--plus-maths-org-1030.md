The double-slit experiment demonstrates that particles such as electrons exhibit wave-like properties when not observed, forming an interference pattern. However, when detected (observed), they behave as particles, collapsing the wave function. This highlights the role of observation in quantum mechanics, where "observation" refers to measurement by a detector, not necessarily human observers. The experiment shows that the act of measurement affects the system being studied, leading to the collapse of the superposition state into a definite particle state.
