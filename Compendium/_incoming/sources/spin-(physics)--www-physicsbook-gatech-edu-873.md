# Spin (Physics)
- Spin is an intrinsic property carried by all elementary particles, composite particles, and atomic nuclei.
- It is a form of angular momentum, with the other form being orbital angular momentum.
- Spin has never been observed directly but has been inferred from experimental data, such as the Stern-Gerlach Experiment.
- The concept of spin was proposed by Ralph Kronig, George Uhlenbeck, and Samuel Goudsmit, later expanded upon by Wolfgang Pauli.
- The quantum spin number (s) is determined by s = n/2, where n is any non-negative number.
- Spin is a vector quantity with both magnitude and direction.
- All elementary particles have the same magnitude of spin angular momentum, given by the spin quantum number (Ms).
- The quantum spin number is represented as a unitless value due to being expressed as a multiple of the Planck Constant.
- The actual unit of spin is the Joule-second.
- Measuring the direction of spin is challenging because measuring spin around the x-axis destroys information about the y and z-axes.
- All possible spin directions are described by the expression "2s+1."
- For a spin-1/2 particle, there are 2 possible directions: -1/2 ("spin-down") and 1/2 ("spin-up").
- For a spin-3/2 particle, there are 4 possible directions: -3/2, -1/2, 1/2, and 3/2.
