# Double-Slit Experiment
- The double-slit experiment demonstrates the wave-particle duality of electrons, photons, and other fundamental particles.
- When streams of particles pass through two narrow slits, they produce an interference pattern on a detector screen instead of clustering based on individual slits.
- This phenomenon occurs even when particles are fired one at a time, showing that they exhibit wave behavior by interfering with themselves.

# Historical Context
- The original double-slit experiment was performed using light (photons) around the turn of the 19th century by Thomas Young and is often called Young's double-slit experiment.
- The idea of using particles other than photons in the experiment came later, after the proposals by de Broglie and the development of quantum mechanics.

# Importance of the Experiment
- The double-slit experiment highlights the wave-particle duality, which is fundamental to quantum mechanics.
- It shows that particles can behave as waves and vice versa, depending on the properties being observed.

# Superposition and Interference
- Waves describe oscillating values of a physical quantity and obey the wave equation. They are decomposed into sine and cosine functions.
- Superposition is a principle where two or more waves combine to form a resultant wave. This is a fundamental concept in quantum mechanics.
- Constructive interference occurs when waves add together to form a larger amplitude, while destructive interference results in smaller amplitude.

# Mathematical Details
- The de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving with velocity \(1.0 \times 10^7 \text{ m/s}\) is calculated as \(7.3 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m}\).
- Usain Bolt's de Broglie wavelength, given his top speed and mass, is approximately \( -5 \) orders of magnitude in units of the Bohr radius.

# Experimental Setup
- In Young's double-slit experiment, photons of wavelength \(\lambda\) are fired at a barrier with two slits separated by distance \(d\).
- The screen is placed at a distance \(D\) from the slits, where \(D \gg d\), and the vertical displacement \(y\) from the midpoint corresponds to the interference pattern.

# Mathematical Modeling
- The condition for constructive interference is given by \(\Delta L = n\lambda\), where \(\Delta L\) is the path difference and \(n\) is an integer.
- This leads to the formula for maxima: \(y = \frac{n\lambda D}{d}\).

# Wave-Particle Duality
- The experiment shows that particles like electrons exhibit wave behavior by interfering with themselves, even when fired one at a time.

# Conclusion
- The double-slit experiment is crucial in understanding quantum mechanics and the wave-particle duality of fundamental particles.
