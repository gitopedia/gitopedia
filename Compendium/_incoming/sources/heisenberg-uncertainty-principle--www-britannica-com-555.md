# Quantum Mechanics

## Overview

Quantum mechanics is a fundamental theory in physics that describes the behavior of matter and energy at the smallest scales, such as atoms and subatomic particles. It provides a mathematical framework to understand and predict phenomena that classical physics cannot explain.

## Historical Basis

The development of quantum mechanics was driven by the need to explain the wave-particle duality observed in light and matter. Key historical milestones include:

- **Classical Physics Limitations**: By the late 19th century, classical physics struggled to explain phenomena like blackbody radiation and the photoelectric effect.
  
- **Planck's Quantum Hypothesis (1900)**: Max Planck proposed that energy is emitted in discrete quanta, E = hν, introducing Planck's constant (h). This resolved discrepancies in the observed energy distribution of blackbody radiation.

- **Wave-Particle Duality**: Early 20th-century experiments confirmed both wave-like and particle-like properties of light and matter, challenging classical physics' dual views.

## Key Concepts

### Wave-Particle Duality
Particles such as electrons exhibit both wave-like (e.g., diffraction) and particle-like behaviors. This duality is central to quantum mechanics.

### Schrödinger's Equation
Developed by Erwin Schrödinger, this equation describes how the quantum state of a physical system evolves over time. It is crucial for predicting atomic and subatomic phenomena.

### Uncertainty Principle
Formulated by Werner Heisenberg, it states that certain pairs of physical properties (e.g., position and momentum) cannot be simultaneously measured with arbitrary precision.

### Quantum Superposition
A system can exist in multiple states until measured, as described by the superposition principle. This is famously illustrated by Schrödinger's cat thought experiment.

## Notable Physicists

- **Max Planck**: Introduced the quantum hypothesis.
- **Erwin Schrödinger**: Developed wave mechanics with his eponymous equation.
- **Werner Heisenberg**: Formulated the uncertainty principle and contributed to matrix mechanics.

## Applications

Quantum mechanics underpins modern technologies, including:
- Semiconductors
- Lasers
- Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)

Despite its success, quantum mechanics raises foundational questions about measurement and reality, ongoing areas of research.
