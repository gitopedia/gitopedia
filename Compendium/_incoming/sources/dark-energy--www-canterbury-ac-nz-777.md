# Dark Energy Doesn't Exist According to New NZ Study
- Kiwi researchers are challenging the concept of dark energy, suggesting it doesn't exist.
- The study uses improved analysis of supernovae light curves to show the universe expands in a more varied and "lumpier" way.
- Theoretical Physics Professor David Wiltshire from UC leads the research with Dr Ryan Ridden-Harper and PhD students Zachary Lane, Antonia Seifert, and Marco Galoppo.
- The findings support the "timescape" model of cosmic expansion, which does not require dark energy.
- According to the timescape model, clocks in galaxies run slower than those in large cosmic voids due to gravity slowing time.
- This difference allows more space expansion, making it seem like the universe is accelerating without needing dark energy.
- Professor Wiltshire states that dark energy is a misidentification of variations in the kinetic energy of expansion.
- The research provides compelling evidence and may resolve key questions about the expanding cosmos within five years.
- The new analysis was published in the journal Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society Letters.

# Supernovae and Cosmic Expansion
- Supernovae have been crucial in understanding how the universe expands.
- The study uses supernova light curves to analyze cosmic expansion variations.
- These observations suggest that the universe's expansion is not uniform but rather "lumpier."
- This variability challenges the need for dark energy as an explanation.

# Timescape Model of Cosmic Expansion
- The timescape model explains cosmic expansion without dark energy.
- It accounts for differences in how time passes in different regions of the universe due to gravity.
- Clocks in galaxies run slower than those in large cosmic voids, leading to more space expansion.
- This model allows for faster apparent expansion as voids dominate the universe.

# Research Team and Contributions
- Professor David Wiltshire leads the research at UC's School of Physical & Chemical Sciences.
- The team includes Dr Ryan Ridden-Harper, Zachary Lane, Antonia Seifert, and Marco Galoppo.
- They collaborated with the supernova collaboration at the Space Telescope Science Institute in the United States.

# Implications for Cosmology
- The study challenges the dominant theory of cosmic acceleration driven by dark energy.
- It provides a new framework to understand the universe's expansion without relying on dark energy.
- This could settle one of the biggest mysteries in cosmology within the next decade with new data.

# Publication Details
- The research was published in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society Letters.
- The findings are significant for reinterpreting cosmic observations and understanding fundamental physics.
