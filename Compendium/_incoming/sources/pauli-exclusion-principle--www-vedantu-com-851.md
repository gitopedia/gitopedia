**Extracted Facts on the Pauli Exclusion Principle**

1. **Definition**: The Pauli Exclusion Principle, formulated by Wolfgang Pauli, states that no two electrons in a single atom can have the exact same set of four quantum numbers (n, l, m_l, and m_s). This means each electron must occupy a unique quantum state.

2. **Quantum Numbers**:
   - **Principal Quantum Number (n)**: Represents the main energy level or shell.
   - **Azimuthal Quantum Number (l)**: Describes the shape of the orbital (s, p, d, f).
   - **Magnetic Quantum Number (m_l)**: Specifies the orientation of the orbital in space.
   - **Spin Quantum Number (m_s)**: Indicates the intrinsic angular momentum or 'spin' of the electron, which can be either +1/2 (spin up) or -1/2 (spin down).

3. **Example**: In a neutral Helium atom, two electrons occupy the 1s orbital. Both share the same n=1, l=0, and m_l=0 quantum numbers. To comply with the principle, their spins are opposite: one is +1/2 and the other is -1/2.

4. **Application in Chemistry**: The principle helps determine electron configurations, which influence an element's chemical properties and periodic table placement.

5. **Relation to Other Rules**:
   - **Aufbau Principle**: Electrons fill orbitals starting from the lowest energy level.
   - **Hund's Rule**: Electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly before pairing.

6. **Scope**: Applies only to fermions (particles with half-integer spin, like electrons) and not to bosons (particles with integer spin).

7. **Importance in Matter Stability**: Prevents electrons from all occupying the same low-energy state, giving atoms volume and structure. This principle is crucial for electron degeneracy pressure, supporting stars against collapse.
