# Quantum Science and Engineering
- Researchers seek to understand and leverage quantum mechanics for real-world devices.
- The first quantum revolution in the 20th century enabled technologies like lasers, transistors, MRI, and semiconductors.
- The second quantum revolution is currently underway, focusing on realizing new quantum technologies.

# Quantum@MIT Initiative
- Paola Cappellaro PhD ’06 leads Quantum@MIT to bridge fundamental discovery and impactful technology.
- The initiative aims to consolidate educational offerings and broaden appeal to students.
- MIT's leadership in quantum research is supported by partners like the MIT Lincoln Laboratory.

# Quantum Sensing
- Quantum sensing uses highly accurate sensors for advancements in navigation, bioimaging, and battery materials.
- Guoqing Wang PhD ’23 explores diamond spin defects for quantum sensing and simulation.
- Wolfgang Ketterle won the 2001 Nobel Prize for creating a Bose-Einstein condensate, foundational for quantum simulation.

# Quantum Information Processing
- "Quantum advantage" refers to quantum computers outperforming classical ones.
- Aram Harrow ’01, PhD ’05 explores quantum algorithms and communication complexity.
- MIT's Center for Quantum Engineering (CQE) bridges computer science, math, natural sciences, and engineering.

# Superconducting Quantum Computers
- Pablo Jarillo Herrero discovered the "magic angle" in graphene for superconductivity.
- Research aims to design practical, room temperature superconductors for quantum computing.

# New Energy Applications
- Sahil Pontula ’23 uses nonlinear and quantum optics for energy applications like enhanced batteries.
- Quantum optics and nanophotonics could revolutionize quantum information and sensing platforms.

# Developing More Effective Drugs
- Haoyang “Oscar” Wu integrates quantum chemistry and deep learning for drug discovery.
- Research accelerates small-molecule screening in developing new drugs.

# A Quantum-Ready Workforce
- MIT offers courses like "Quantum Computing Fundamentals" through MIT xPRO.
- Quantum@MIT aims to create more interdisciplinary quantum programming opportunities.

# Steering Quantum Policy
- MIT faculty influence national policy on quantum technologies.
- Maria Zuber, Presidential Advisor for Science and Technology Policy, contributes to legislation and regulations.
