# Electrons and Atomic Structure
- Electrons act as diffuse clouds in orbitals rather than following circular paths like planets.
- Orbitals can take on various shapes such as spherical shells or barbell/torus patterns.

# Orbital vs. Intrinsic Angular Momentum
- Electrons have two angular momenta: orbital (from their cloud shape) and intrinsic spin.
- Spin is referred to as intrinsic angular momentum, analogous to a rotating planet but applicable to point masses.

# Quantum Numbers for Electrons in Atoms
- Four quantum numbers describe an electron's state: principal (n), azimuthal (l), magnetic (ml), and spin (s).
- Principal quantum number (n) indicates the shell (e.g., 1, 2, 3...).
- Azimuthal quantum number (l) describes subshells within a shell (0 to n-1).
- Magnetic quantum number (ml) gives angular momentum orientation (-l to +l).
- Spin quantum number (s) is always ½ for electrons.
- Possible spin orientations are -½ and +½.

# The Standard Model of Particle Physics
- Particles classified into fermions (half-integer spin, obey Pauli exclusion) and bosons (integer spin, do not obey exclusion).
- Fundamental fermions include electrons, neutrinos, quarks, muons, tau, and their antiparticles.
- Bosons include photon, gluon, Z0, W+, W-, Higgs, and graviton (hypothetical).
- Fermions have spin 1/2; bosons typically spin 1 except Higgs (spin 0).

# Pauli Exclusion Principle
- No two identical fermions can occupy the same quantum state.
- Quantum state is defined by n, l, ml, ms.
- Example for n=1: combinations of l=0 and l=1 with ml and ms values.
- Atoms with more than 8 electrons require higher shells (n=2+).
- Bosons do not obey this principle.

# Stern-Gerlach Experiment
- Demonstrated electrons have intrinsic angular momentum (spin).
- Electrons split into two beams in magnetic field, indicating two spin orientations ("up" and "down").
- Conducted by Otto Stern and Walter Gerlach in 1922 with silver atoms.
- Showed net magnetic moment due to spin.

# Fine Structure Splitting in the Hydrogen Atom
- Spin led to fine structure splitting of energy levels.
- Each combination of n, l, ml has two possible ms values.
- Observed as doublets in emission spectra at high resolution.
