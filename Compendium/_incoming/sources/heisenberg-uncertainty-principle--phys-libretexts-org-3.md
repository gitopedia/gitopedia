# Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
- The Heisenberg uncertainty principle is a key principle in quantum mechanics.
- It states that if the uncertainty in position (Δx) is small, the uncertainty in momentum (Δp) must be large, and vice versa.
- The product of the uncertainties in position and momentum cannot be less than one-half of the reduced Planck constant: ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2.
- This principle applies to other quantities such as energy and time, with the energy-time uncertainty relation given by ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2.

# Momentum and Position
- A free particle moving along the x-direction has a uniform probability density and definite momentum but infinite position uncertainty.
- A localized particle is modeled by a wave packet, which is a linear superposition of plane-wave states with multiple wavelengths and momenta.
- The minimum uncertainty product for a Gaussian function is used to estimate uncertainties in position and momentum.

# Example Calculations
- For an electron with speed precision Δu = 1.0×10−3 m/s:
  - Δp = (9.1×10−31 kg)(1.0×10−3 m/s) = 9.1×10−34 kg·m/s
  - Δx = ℏ/(2Δp) ≈ 5.8 cm
- For a bowling ball with mass 6.0 kg and speed precision Δu = 1.0×10−3 m/s:
  - Δp = (6.0 kg)(1.0×10−3 m/s) = 6.0×10−3 kg·m/s
  - Δx = ℏ/(2Δp) ≈ 8.8×10−33 m

# Ground-State Energy of a Hydrogen Atom
- The size of a hydrogen atom is approximately 0.1 nm.
- Using the uncertainty principle, the ground-state energy E₀ is estimated as:
  - E₀ = (ℏ²)/(8mL²) ≈ 1 eV
- This estimate aligns with experimental results for the ionization energy.

# Energy and Time Uncertainty Principle
- The energy-time uncertainty relation is given by ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2.
- A quantum state with a short lifetime (Δt) has an uncertain energy (ΔE).
- Finite lifetimes of excited states in atoms result in spectral line widths due to the distribution of emitted photon frequencies.

# Example Calculation for Spectral Line Width
- For an atom with Δt = 10−8 s and average frequency f = 7.1×1014 Hz:
  - ΔE ≈ ℏ/(2Δt) ≈ 1.6×10−5 eV
  - Δf ≈ (ΔE/h) ≈ 8.0×106 Hz
  - Δff = 8.0×106 Hz / 7.1×1014 Hz ≈ 1.1×10−8 (or 0.00011%)
- The emitted radiation can be considered monochromatic as the frequency spread is very small.

# Sodium Atom Transition
- A sodium atom transitions from the first excited state to the ground state, emitting a photon with wavelength 589.0 nm and energy 2.105 eV.
- If the lifetime of the excited state is Δt = 1.6×10−8 s:
  - ΔE ≈ ℏ/(2Δt) ≈ 4.1×10−8 eV
  - The spectral line width corresponds to this energy uncertainty.

# Conclusion
- The Heisenberg uncertainty principle imposes fundamental limits on the precision of simultaneous measurements of certain pairs of physical quantities in quantum mechanics.
- These limits are not due to experimental imprecision but arise from the wave-like nature of matter and energy.
