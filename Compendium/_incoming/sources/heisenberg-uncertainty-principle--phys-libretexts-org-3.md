# Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
- The Heisenberg uncertainty principle is a key principle in quantum mechanics.
- It states that if the uncertainty in position (Δx) is small, the uncertainty in momentum (Δp) must be large, and vice versa.
- The principle also applies to other quantities such as energy and time.

# Momentum and Position
- For a free particle moving along the x-direction, the wavefunction is uniform and independent of time.
- A localized particle is modeled by a linear superposition of free-particle states called a wave packet.
- The uncertainty in position (Δx) and momentum (Δp) obey the relation: ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2.
- For a Gaussian function, the minimum uncertainty product is achieved.

# Examples
- For an electron with speed precision of 1.0×10−3 m/s:
  - Δp = 9.1×10−34 kg·m/s
  - Δx = 5.8 cm
- For a bowling ball with the same speed precision:
  - Δp = 6.0×10−3 kg·m/s
  - Δx = 8.8×10−33 m

# Ground-State Energy of a Hydrogen Atom
- The size of a hydrogen atom is approximately 0.1 nm.
- Using the uncertainty principle, the ground-state energy is estimated to be around 1 eV.

# Energy and Time Uncertainty Principle
- The energy-time uncertainty relation is given by ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2.
- A quantum state with a short lifetime cannot have a definite energy.
- Finite lifetimes of excited states result in spectral line widths.

# Example Calculation
- For an atom with Δt = 10−8 s and average frequency f = 7.1×1014 Hz:
  - Δf ≈ 8.0×106 Hz
  - Δff ≈ 1.1×10−8 (less than 1% of the average frequency)
- The emitted radiation is considered monochromatic.

# Sodium Atom Transition
- Emitted photon wavelength: 589.0 nm (energy 2.105 eV).
- Lifetime of excited state: 1.6×10−8 s.
- Uncertainty in energy: 4.1×10−8 eV.
- Spectral line width: 1.1×10−5 nm.

# Conclusion
- The Heisenberg uncertainty principle has significant implications for quantum mechanics, particularly in understanding the limitations of simultaneous measurements of position and momentum, as well as energy and time.
