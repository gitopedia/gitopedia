# What is the uncertainty principle?
- The uncertainty principle states that we cannot simultaneously know precise values of certain pairs of properties, such as position and momentum.
- It is a fundamental limit in quantum mechanics, not due to measurement tools but the nature of reality at quantum scales.
- Particles do not have definite positions and momenta simultaneously; they exist in probability states.
- The principle challenges classical physics intuition by introducing probabilistic behavior in quantum mechanics.

# Scientific Principles
- **Complementary properties**: Pairs like position and momentum, or energy and time, are complementary. Knowing one precisely makes the other uncertain.
- **Fundamental limit**: Uncertainty is inherent to quantum mechanics; particles lack simultaneous definite values for complementary properties.
- **Mathematical relationship**: Quantified as Δx × Δp ≥ ℏ/2π, where Δx is position uncertainty, Δp is momentum uncertainty, and ℏ is Planck's constant divided by 2π.
- **Wave-particle connection**: Uncertainty links to wave-particle duality; precise position requires multiple wavelengths (uncertain momentum), while precise momentum needs extended waves (uncertain position).
- **Measurement effect**: Measuring one property affects the other, creating uncertainty in the complementary property.

# How does quantum mechanics work?
- Quantum mechanics describes particle behavior at atomic and subatomic scales.
- A quantum is the smallest unit of a physical property, foundational to understanding quantum phenomena.

# Why is light both a wave and a particle?
- Light exhibits wave-particle duality, behaving as both waves and particles depending on context.

# Neutrinos
- Neutrinos are nearly massless, chargeless particles interacting weakly with matter.

# Quarks
- Quarks are fundamental particles composing protons and neutrons, with six types identified.

# Subatomic particles
- Subatomic particles include protons, neutrons, electrons, quarks, and neutrinos.

# Atoms
- An atom is the smallest unit retaining an element's properties, consisting of a nucleus and orbiting electrons.

# Antimatter
- Antimatter comprises antiparticles with opposite charges to their matter counterparts.
