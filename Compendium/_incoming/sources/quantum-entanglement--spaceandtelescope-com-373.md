# NASA’s SEAQUE Instrument Arrives at ISS
- NASA’s SEAQUE (Space Entanglement and Annealing Quantum Experiment) instrument arrived at the International Space Station (ISS) on November 5 aboard a SpaceX Dragon cargo spacecraft.
- The instrument aims to demonstrate quantum entanglement, a phenomenon where two subatomic particles are connected regardless of distance.

# Description of Quantum Entanglement
- Quantum entanglement involves pairs of photons or electrons being intimately connected even when separated by vast distances.
- Changing the quantum state of one particle immediately affects the other, enabling secure deep-space communication and quantum teleportation.

# Components of SEAQUE Instrument
- SEAQUE includes an entanglement source to generate entangled photons.
- The instrument has detectors to count entangled photons and their quantum states.

# Installation Details
- The SEAQUE instrument will be installed on the exterior of the ISS using a Materials International Space Station Experiment (MISSE) mounting location.

# Potential Applications
- If successful, the experiment could enable quantum-level communication in space.
- This technology might allow quantum computers to communicate regardless of their distance apart.

# Additional Technology Testing
- SEAQUE will test photon detector self-healing from radiation damage using laser annealing.

# Co-Investigator Statement
- Makan Mohageg, SEAQUE co-investigator at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory, stated that demonstrating these technologies builds the foundation for future global quantum networks connecting quantum computers hundreds or thousands of miles apart.
