# Dirac's Equation Predicts Antiparticles
- Paul Dirac formulated an equation in 1928 that combined quantum theory and special relativity to describe the behavior of an electron moving at relativistic speeds. 
- The equation was published in his paper titled "The quantum theory of the electron," which was received by the journal *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* on 2 January 1928.
- Dirac's work earned him the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1933.
- His equation posed a problem as it had two solutions: one for an electron with positive energy and another with negative energy, challenging classical physics which dictated that particle energy must always be positive.
- Dirac interpreted this to mean that every particle has a corresponding antiparticle, identical except for opposite charge. For the electron, this would be an "antielectron" with a positive electric charge.
- In his 1933 Nobel lecture, Dirac speculated on the existence of antimatter and suggested that half the stars in the universe might be composed of positrons and negative protons, making them indistinguishable from our perspective using current astronomical methods.
