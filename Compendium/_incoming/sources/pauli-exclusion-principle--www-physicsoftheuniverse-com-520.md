# Discovery of Spin
- Spin was first discovered in 1922 by Otto Stern and Walther Gerlach.
- Their experiments showed that the intrinsic angular momentum, or spin, of a particle such as an electron was quantized, meaning it could only take certain discrete values.

# Types of Particles Based on Spin
- Particles are divided into two subcategories based on spin: bosons and fermions.
- Bosons have integer spin and include photons, gluons, W and Z bosons, and hypothetical gravitons.
- Fermions have half-integer spin and include electrons, neutrinos, muons, and quarks.

# Wave Functions
- Bosons have symmetric wave functions.
- Fermions have antisymmetric wave functions.

# Implications of Spin for Quantum Theory
- The spins of particles must be carefully added together using special rules for angular momentum in quantum mechanics.
- This distinction leads to the "gregarious" nature of bosons, such as their behavior in lasers, superfluids, and superconductors.
- Fermions exhibit an "unsociable" nature due to their wave functions not flipping.

# Pauli Exclusion Principle
- Formulated by Wolfgang Pauli in 1925.
- States that no two identical fermions may occupy the same quantum state simultaneously.
- Another way of stating this is that no two fermions can have the same values of all four quantum numbers at any given time.

# Effects on Atomic Structure
- Electrons in an atom are constrained to certain discrete orbital positions or "shells."
- The innermost shell can hold 2 electrons, one with spin "up" and one with spin "down."
- Subsequent shells can hold 8, 18, and 32 electrons respectively.
- Each energy level is composed of sub-shells (s, p, d, f) with capacities of 2, 6, 10, and 14 electrons respectively.

# Role in Chemistry
- The arrangement of electrons, particularly outermost shell electrons, affects the chemical properties of elements.
- Two atoms with "closed" shells cannot form a chemical bond because electrons cannot occupy the same quantum state.

# Degeneracy Pressure and White Dwarfs
- If two identical particles are forced to have the same quantum numbers, they repel due to degeneracy pressure or Pauli repulsion.
- This force holds white dwarf stars in place.

# Fundamental Principle of Quantum Physics
- The Pauli exclusion principle applies to electrons, protons, and neutrons, explaining why matter occupies space.
- Electrons "know" the quantum numbers of existing electrons in an atom and choose appropriate orbitals without a physical force.

# Importance of the Principle
- Explains the existence of different atoms and the variety of matter in the universe.
- Fundamental to understanding atomic structure, chemical bonding, and the behavior of matter.
