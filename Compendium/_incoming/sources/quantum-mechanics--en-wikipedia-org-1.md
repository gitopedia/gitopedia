### Extracted Facts:

1. **Quantum Mechanics Overview**:
   - Quantum mechanics is a fundamental theory in physics that provides a description of the physical properties of nature at the scale of atoms and subatomic particles.
   - It was developed in the early decades of the 20th century to explain phenomena such as black-body radiation, photoelectric effect, and the behavior of electrons.

2. **Key Developments**:
   - **Max Planck**: Introduced the quantum hypothesis in 1900, proposing that energy is radiated and absorbed in discrete quanta.
   - **Albert Einstein**: Expanded on Planck's work by explaining the photoelectric effect (1905) and introducing the concept of photons.
   - **Niels Bohr**: Developed a model of the hydrogen atom that predicted spectral lines.
   - **Louis de Broglie**: Proposed that particles exhibit wave characteristics (1923).
   - **Werner Heisenberg, Max Born, and Pascual Jordan**: Developed matrix mechanics in 1925.
   - **Erwin Schrödinger**: Introduced wave mechanics in 1925.
   - **David Hilbert, Paul Dirac, and John von Neumann**: Formalized quantum mechanics by 1930, emphasizing measurement and the statistical nature of reality.

3. **Significance**:
   - Quantum mechanics explains phenomena such as superconductivity, superfluidity, and the behavior of electrons in semiconductors.
   - It underpins modern technologies including quantum chemistry, quantum electronics, quantum optics, and quantum information science.

4. **Philosophical and Theoretical Aspects**:
   - The theory challenges classical notions of determinism and locality (e.g., Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen paradox and Bell inequalities).
   - Interpretations include Copenhagen interpretation, Bohmian mechanics, many-worlds interpretation, relational quantum mechanics, QBism.

5. **Historical Context**:
   - Early wave theories of light by Robert Hooke, Christiaan Huygens, and Thomas Young (double-slit experiment).
   - Developments in atomic theory by John Dalton, Amedeo Avogadro, James Clerk Maxwell, and Ludwig Boltzmann.
   - Discovery of cathode rays by J.J. Thomson and the identification of electrons.

6. **Modern Applications**:
   - Used in understanding chemical bonding, semiconductors, lasers, and quantum computing.
   - Plays a crucial role in the periodic table of elements and modern materials science.

7. **Key Concepts**:
   - Wave-particle duality.
   - Uncertainty principle (Heisenberg).
   - Schrödinger equation.
   - Quantum superposition and entanglement.

8. **Theoretical Frameworks**:
   - Matrix mechanics vs. wave mechanics.
   - Probabilistic interpretation of quantum states.
   - Role of the observer in quantum measurements.

9. **Impact on Technology**:
   - Revolutionized electronics, optics, and information technology.
   - Enabled advancements in nanotechnology and biotechnology.

10. **Cultural and Intellectual Influence**:
    - Challenged classical physics and influenced 20th-century philosophy and science.
    - Spawned various interpretations and debates about the nature of reality.
