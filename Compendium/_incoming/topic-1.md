Section 3: Key Concepts
----------------------

* **Maori Sovereignty**: The belief in the inherent right of the Maori people to govern and determine their own destiny, based on a deep connection to the land and culture. This concept has shaped debates about land rights, cultural identity, and political participation throughout Aotearoa's history. 
* **European Colonization and Imperialism**: The arrival of British explorers, traders, and settlers in the late 18th century marked the beginning of European colonization in Aotearoa. This process involved the imposition of new social structures, economic systems, and political institutions that often conflicted with Maori sovereignty and customs.
* **Resistance and Rebellion**: Throughout history, there have been numerous instances where Maori people resisted the encroachment of colonial powers, such as the famous uprisings led by Te Kooti, Te Whiti-o-Rongomai, and Tūrangawaewae. These events highlight the importance of understanding Maori sovereignty in interpreting Aotearoa's historical context.
* **Abolitionism and Women's Rights**: The late 19th century saw significant social movements advocating for the abolition of slavery and women's rights, which had a profound impact on Aotearoa New Zealand's history. These movements were influenced by international developments but also shaped by local contexts and ideologies.
* **Post-Colonialism and Multiculturalism**: The end of World War II marked a turning point in Aotearoa's historical evolution, as the country began to grapple with its colonial past and embrace multiculturalism. This period saw increased recognition of Maori sovereignty, land claims settlements, and efforts to promote racial equality and intercultural understanding.
* **Environmental Consciousness**: The 1970s and 1980s witnessed a growing environmental consciousness in Aotearoa New Zealand, which led to the adoption of policies aimed at preserving natural resources and promoting sustainable development. This period also saw increased recognition of indigenous rights and their connection with environmental conservation efforts.
* **Globalization**: The late 20th century marked another significant turning point for Aotearoa New Zealand as globalization brought about changes in political, economic, and cultural spheres. The country became increasingly integrated into global networks while still grappling with its colonial legacy and indigenous rights.
