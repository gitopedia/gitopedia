# Wave Function

## Overview
A wave function describes the fundamental properties of waves, which are disturbances transferring energy without the transfer of matter. This article explores the key characteristics of waves, including wavelength, amplitude (height), frequency, and speed.

## Key Properties

### Wavelength
- **Definition**: The distance between consecutive peaks or troughs of a wave.
- **Examples**:
  - Tsunamis have very long wavelengths (~100 km).
  - Sound waves vary from 70 mm (high pitch) to 70 m (low pitch).

### Amplitude/Height
- **Definition**: The maximum displacement of particles from their equilibrium position.
- **Application**: For water waves, it's the distance between the wave crest and normal water level. Wave height refers to the vertical distance between crest and trough.

### Frequency
- **Definition**: The number of wavelengths passing a point per second, measured in hertz (Hz).
- **Examples**:
  - Microwaves have lower frequencies than UV radiation.
  - Longer wavelength waves have lower frequencies and longer periods.

## Wave Speed
- **Relationship**: Calculated using the equation \( v = f \times \lambda \) (speed equals frequency multiplied by wavelength).
- **Speeds**:
  - Electromagnetic waves travel at ~300 million m/s.
  - Sound travels at ~340 m/s in air, while water waves vary.

## Types of Waves
- **Examples** include water waves, sound waves, light waves, radio waves, and microwaves. Each type exhibits common properties like reflection, refraction, diffraction, and interference.

## Real-World Applications
- Understanding wave properties is crucial across various fields, from acoustics and optics to telecommunications, where frequency and wavelength determine signal behavior.

This structured approach captures the essential aspects of wave functions based on their observable properties and real-world applications.
