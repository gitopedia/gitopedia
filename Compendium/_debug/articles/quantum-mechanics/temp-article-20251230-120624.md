# Quantum Mechanics: An Encyclopedia Article

## Overview
Quantum Mechanics is a foundational theory in physics that describes the behavior of particles at atomic and subatomic levels. Unlike classical physics, it treats particles as wave functions, offering probabilities for their locations rather than fixed positions. This model explains phenomena like electron behavior in atoms, which classical physics cannot account for, and has revolutionized technology through applications such as semiconductors and lasers.

## History
The development of Quantum Mechanics involved several key figures:

- **Max Planck**: Introduced the concept of quantized energy levels.
- **Albert Einstein**: Explained the photoelectric effect using quantum theory.
- **Niels Bohr**: Proposed quantized electron orbits in the Bohr model.
- **Erwin Schrödinger**: Developed the Schrödinger equation to describe quantum states.
- **Werner Heisenberg**: Formulated the uncertainty principle and matrix mechanics.

## Key Concepts
Quantum Mechanics introduces several pivotal concepts:

- **Superposition**: Particles can exist in multiple states until observed, as illustrated by Schrödinger's cat thought experiment.
- **Entanglement**: Correlated particles influence each other instantaneously regardless of distance.
- **Quantum Tunneling**: Electrons pass through energy barriers they classically shouldn't traverse.
- **Pauli Exclusion Principle**: No two electrons can share the same quantum numbers, structuring the periodic table.

## Applications
Quantum Mechanics underpins various technologies:

- **Semiconductors**: Enable modern electronics.
- **Lasers**: Utilize quantum principles for light emission.
- **MRI Machines**: Provide detailed medical imaging.
- **Quantum Computing**: Uses superposition and entanglement for advanced computations.

## Misconceptions
Common misunderstandings include:

- **Determinism vs. Probability**: Quantum Mechanics is probabilistic, not deterministic.
- **Observer Effect**: Observation doesn't require a conscious observer but any interaction causing collapse.
- **Size Limitation**: Quantum effects apply to macroscopic systems under certain conditions.

Quantum Mechanics challenges classical notions of reality through concepts like wave-particle duality and entanglement, shaping technologies from computers to cryptography.
