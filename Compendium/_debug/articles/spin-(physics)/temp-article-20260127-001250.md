# Spin (Physics)

Spin is a fundamental concept in physics, particularly in quantum mechanics and solid-state materials science. It describes an intrinsic form of angular momentum carried by elementary particles such as electrons. In the context of quantum materials, spin plays a crucial role in phenomena like spin-orbit coupling (SOC), which has been extensively studied due to its implications for emergent properties in these systems.

## Overview

Spin refers to the rotational motion of particles at the most fundamental level. Unlike classical angular momentum, which arises from the physical rotation of macroscopic objects, quantum spin is a property inherent to elementary particles and cannot be directly observed. The concept of spin is pivotal in understanding magnetic properties and interactions in materials, especially in the realm of quantum mechanics.

## Key Concepts

- **Spin-Orbit Coupling (SOC)**: This refers to the interaction between an electron's spin and its orbital motion around the nucleus. SOC is a relativistic effect that becomes significant in heavy atoms and has profound implications for the electronic properties of materials, particularly in quantum systems like two-dimensional magnets.

- **Quantum Materials**: These are materials with unique quantum mechanical properties that cannot be explained by classical physics alone. Examples include graphene, topological insulators, and high-temperature superconductors. Quantum materials often exhibit emergent phenomena due to strong correlations between electrons.

- **Two-Dimensional Magnets**: These are magnetic systems confined to a single plane or layer, which can lead to unique electronic properties. SOC plays a critical role in determining the behavior of these systems, as it influences the interaction between spin and orbital degrees of freedom.

## Review Article

A comprehensive review on "Spin-orbit coupling in quantum materials" was published in *Rivista Nuovo Cimento*. This article explores the role of SOC in quantum materials, with a focus on emergent phenomena, theoretical modeling techniques, and experimental observations. The study highlights the importance of SOC in shaping the electronic properties of two-dimensional magnets, providing insights into the interplay between spin and orbital dynamics.

The review was authored by Silvia Picozzi, who conceived the work and wrote the manuscript. Collaborators included K. Yamauchi, D. Di Sante, D. Amoroso, S. Stavric, A. Stroppa, R. Comin, R. Bertacco, C. Rinaldi, and R. Valenti, with additional contributions from Paolo Barone.
