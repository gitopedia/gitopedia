# Quantum Harmonic Oscillator

## Overview

The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator (QHO) is a fundamental model in quantum mechanics, describing the quantized motion of a particle under a harmonic potential. It serves as a cornerstone for understanding various physical systems, including electromagnetic fields and molecular vibrations.

## Key Concepts

### Hamiltonian

The Hamiltonian for a simple harmonic oscillator is expressed as:

\[ H = \frac{p^2}{2} + \frac{x^2}{2} \]

where \( p \) is the momentum operator, and \( x \) is the position operator. The ladder operators, defined as:

\[ a = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x + i p) \]
\[ a^\dagger = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x - i p) \]

satisfy the commutation relation:

\[ [a, a^\dagger] = 1 \]

These operators are essential in expressing the Hamiltonian and other observables.

### Coherent States

Coherent states are specific quantum states of bosonic systems, including the QHO. They bridge the gap between quantum and classical mechanics by exhibiting classical-like behavior. A coherent state is defined as:

\[ | \alpha \rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2} e^{\alpha a^\dagger} | 0 \rangle \]

where \( \alpha \) is a complex parameter, and \( |0\rangle \) is the vacuum state.

### Modified Hamiltonian

The modified Hamiltonian, incorporating a displacement in the potential:

\[ H' = \frac{p^2}{2} + \frac{(x - 2\alpha_1)^2}{2} - \alpha_1^2 \]

represents a shifted harmonic oscillator. This shift corresponds to a change in the system's equilibrium position.

### Properties

Coherent states possess several unique properties:

- **Normalization**: \( \langle \alpha | \alpha \rangle = 1 \)
- **Completeness**: They form an over-complete basis.
- **Probability Distribution**: The number of particles follows a Poisson distribution:
  
  \[ P(n) = e^{-|\alpha|^2} \frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!} \]

- **Expectation Values**: The mean position and momentum are given by:

  \[ \langle x \rangle = 2 \text{Re}(\alpha) \]
  \[ \langle p \rangle = 2 \text{Im}(\alpha) \]

### Dynamical Behavior

Coherent states evolve over time as:

\[ | \psi(t) \rangle = | \alpha(t) \rangle \]

The parameter \( \alpha(t) \) evolves according to:

\[ \dot{\alpha}(t) = (-i + \gamma(t)) \alpha(t) \]

For \( \gamma = 0 \), the solution simplifies to:

\[ \alpha(t) = \alpha_0 e^{-it} \]

This leads to classical harmonic oscillator dynamics for the expectation values of position and momentum:

\[ \langle x \rangle = 2 |\alpha_0| \cos[t - \arg(\alpha_0)] \]
\[ \langle p \rangle = -2 |\alpha_0| \sin[t - \arg(\alpha_0)] \]

This behavior underscores the classical-like motion of coherent states in quantum mechanics.
