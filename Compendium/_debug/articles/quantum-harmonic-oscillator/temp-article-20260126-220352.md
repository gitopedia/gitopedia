# Quantum Harmonic Oscillator

## Overview
The quantum harmonic oscillator (QHO) is a fundamental model in quantum mechanics, illustrating quantization in systems exhibiting periodic motion. It finds applications across various fields, including molecular physics and quantum optics.

## Basics of the Classical Model
Classically, a harmonic oscillator is described by the differential equation \( m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + kx = 0 \), representing a particle subjected to a restoring force proportional to its displacement from equilibrium.

## Quantum Mechanical Description
In quantum mechanics, the QHO's Hamiltonian is given by:
\[ H = \frac{p^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2 \]
where \( p \) is momentum, \( x \) is position, and \( \omega \) is angular frequency. The Schrödinger equation \( H\psi = E\psi \) governs the system's wave functions.

## Energy Levels
The energy levels are quantized as:
\[ E_n = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega \]
where \( n \) is a non-negative integer, and the system exhibits zero-point energy even in the ground state (\( n=0 \)).

## Wave Functions
Wave functions are expressed using Hermite polynomials multiplied by Gaussian factors:
\[ \psi_n(x) = N_n H_n(\xi) e^{-\xi^2/2} \]
where \( \xi = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}} x \).

### Orthogonality
Wave functions are orthogonal:
\[ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \psi_m(x)\psi_n(x) dx = \delta_{nm} \]

## Coherent States
Coherent states, eigenstates of the annihilation operator \( a \), satisfy minimal uncertainty and rotate in the complex plane under time evolution.

## Applications
The QHO is crucial in quantum optics for modeling coherent light, in quantum field theory for particle descriptions, and in molecular physics for vibrational modes.

## Example: Simple Pendulum
A simple pendulum with small amplitude approximates a harmonic oscillator, demonstrating quantized energy levels.

## Conclusion
The quantum harmonic oscillator bridges quantum and classical physics, offering essential insights into quantum mechanics.
