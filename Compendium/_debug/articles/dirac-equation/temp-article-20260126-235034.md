# Dirac Equation

## Overview

The Dirac equation is a fundamental equation in quantum field theory that describes the behavior of fermions, such as electrons and quarks. It was formulated by physicist Paul Dirac in 1928 and revolutionized the understanding of relativistic quantum mechanics by introducing the concept of spinors. The solutions to this equation are represented by Dirac spinors, which are bispinors that transform under Lorentz transformations.

## Derivation from the Dirac Equation

The Dirac equation is given by:

\[
\left(-i \vec{\gamma} \cdot \vec{p} + \beta m \right) \psi = i \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \psi
\]

In the Dirac representation, the gamma matrices are defined as:

\[
\vec{\alpha} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{0} & \vec{\sigma} \\ \vec{\sigma} & \mathbf{0} \end{pmatrix}, \quad \beta = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{1} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & -\mathbf{1} \end{pmatrix}
\]

where \(\vec{\sigma}\) are the Pauli matrices. The solution to this equation is a plane wave:

\[
\psi = \omega e^{-ip \cdot x}, \quad \omega = \begin{pmatrix} \phi \\ \chi \end{pmatrix}
\]

Substituting this ansatz into the Dirac equation yields coupled equations for the spinors \(\phi\) and \(\chi\):

\[
(E - m)\phi = (\vec{\sigma} \cdot \vec{p})\chi, \quad (E + m)\chi = (\vec{\sigma} \cdot \vec{p})\phi
\]

## Positive-Frequency Solution

The positive-energy solution is conventionally written as:

\[
u(p) = \sqrt{\frac{E + m}{2}} \begin{pmatrix} \phi \\ \frac{\vec{\sigma} \cdot \vec{p}}{E + m} \phi \end{pmatrix} e^{-ip \cdot x}
\]

where \(E = +\sqrt{|\vec{p}|^2 + m^2}\). The normalization factor ensures orthonormality of the spinors.

## Chiral Representation

In the chiral representation, the gamma matrices are defined as:

\[
\sigma_\mu = (I_2, \vec{\sigma}), \quad \bar{\sigma}_\mu = (I_2, -\vec{\sigma})
\]

This representation is particularly useful for demonstrating chiral symmetry.

## Charge Conjugation

Charge conjugation transforms the positive-energy spinor into its negative-energy counterpart. This transformation is an involution and plays a crucial role in understanding the symmetry properties of the solutions.

## Energy Eigenstate Projection Matrices

The projection matrices \(\Lambda_+(p)\) and \(\Lambda_-(p)\) are defined as:

\[
\Lambda_+(p) = \frac{1}{2}(1 + \gamma^0 \vec{\gamma} \cdot \vec{p}), \quad \Lambda_-(p) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \gamma^0 \vec{\gamma} \cdot \vec{p})
\]

These matrices are idempotent, orthogonal, and sum to the identity matrix. Their trace is 2, making them Lorentz covariants.

## Majorana Equation

The Dirac equation can be interpreted as a complexified version of the Majorana equation, which describes massive, electrically neutral spin-\(\frac{1}{2}\) particles. Coupling to electromagnetism requires taking a real field and making it complex, effectively doubling the number of solutions.

This overview provides a concise introduction to the Dirac equation, highlighting its key concepts and their implications in quantum field theory.
