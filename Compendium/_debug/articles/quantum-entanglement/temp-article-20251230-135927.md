# Quantum Entanglement

## Overview
Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon observed in quantum mechanics where two or more particles, originating from the same source, remain interconnected regardless of the distance separating them. Once separated, these particles maintain a unified quantum state, meaning an event affecting one particle instantaneously influences its entangled counterpart(s).

## History
The concept of quantum entanglement was first highlighted by Albert Einstein in 1935, who referred to it as "spooky action at a distance." This term reflects the seemingly paradoxical nature of particles maintaining connection over vast distances. Initially viewed with skepticism due to its apparent conflict with Einstein's theory of special relativity (which posits that no information can travel faster than light), the phenomenon gained scientific validation through the work of John Stewart Bell in the 1960s. Bell developed a method to test quantum entanglement, paving the way for experimental confirmation of its existence.

## Key Concepts
Quantum mechanics fundamentally differs from classical physics in its treatment of matter and energy. While classical physics predicts deterministic outcomes, quantum physics introduces inherent uncertainty and probabilistic behavior. For example, particles can exist in multiple states simultaneously (superposition) and become entangled across vast distances. This entanglement implies that the state of one particle cannot be fully described independently of the other(s), challenging classical notions of locality and realism.

## Quantum Entanglement Research at NASA
NASA is actively engaged in quantum mechanics research, particularly focusing on quantum entanglement. Projects include:
- **SEAQUE (Space Entanglement and Annealing Quantum Experiment)**: Aiming to explore quantum annealing in space.
- **Cold Atom Lab**: Conducting experiments to study how atoms behave under extreme conditions, defying gravity.
- **Quantum Sensor Development**: NASA achieved a milestone by demonstrating an "ultra-cool" quantum sensor in space.

These efforts align with the Biological and Physical Sciences Division's mission to advance scientific knowledge for both space exploration and terrestrial applications.
