# Quantum Entanglement

## Overview
Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where particles originating from the same source remain interconnected even when separated by vast distances. These particles share a unified quantum state that persists indefinitely. Any event affecting one particle influences all others it is entangled with.

## History
The concept of quantum entanglement was first highlighted by Albert Einstein in 1935, who termed it "spooky action at a distance." He noted that it seemed to conflict with his theory of special relativity, which posits that information cannot travel faster than the speed of light. In the 1960s, John Stewart Bell developed methods to test entanglement experimentally, leading to its eventual confirmation by later scientists.

## Key Concepts
Quantum physics contrasts significantly with classical physics in how it describes particle behavior. While classical physics predicts orderly interactions, quantum physics involves inherent unpredictability and multiple possible outcomes coexisting simultaneously. For instance, a quantum object like an apple might theoretically exist on a table and Mars at the same time until observed.

## NASA Research
NASA is conducting several experiments to explore quantum entanglement:
- **SEAQUE (Space Entanglement and Annealing Quantum Experiment)**: Investigates quantum phenomena in space.
- **Cold Atom Lab Studies**: Examines how atoms behave under microgravity conditions.
- **Quantum Sensor Demonstration**: Showcases an advanced, ultra-cool sensor technology.

## Contributions
NASA's Biological and Physical Sciences Division aims to advance knowledge for deep space exploration while also benefiting life on Earth. Their research in quantum entanglement exemplifies this mission by pushing the boundaries of scientific understanding.
