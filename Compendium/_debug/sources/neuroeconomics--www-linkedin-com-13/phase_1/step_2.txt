- The Impact of History and the Unknown in Neuroeconomics

- Neuroeconomics is a relatively new field that has emerged from the convergence of economics, psychology, and neuroscience12. This interdisciplinary field aims to understand decision-making processes by integrating insights from these three disciplines12.

- Neuroeconomics has its roots in the neoclassical economic revolution of the 1930s and the birth of cognitive neuroscience during the 1990s2.
- The neoclassical economic revolution focused on the mathematical structure of consumer choice and behavior in markets2.
- On the other hand, the birth of cognitive neuroscience provided the tools and techniques to investigate the brain’s functioning when humans make decisions12.

- Impact of Knowledge and the Unknown

- The knowledge developed by various branches of modern neuroscience has significantly contributed to the emergence and rise of neuroeconomics over the past two decades1.
- These advances are grouped over three non-independent topics referred to as the “emo-rational” brain, “social” brain, and “computational” brain1.
- For each topic, it emphasizes findings considered as critical to the birth and development of neuroeconomics while highlighting some of the prominent questions about which knowledge should be improved by future research1.

- However, there are still many unknowns in the field of neuroeconomics.
- The boundaries between neuroeconomics and several recent sub-fields of cognitive neuroscience, such as affective, social, and more generally, decision neuroscience, are rather porous1.
- This suggests that there is still much to learn and discover in this field.

- In conclusion, the historical context and the current state of knowledge in neuroeconomics provide a rich tapestry for understanding decision-making processes. However, the unknowns in this field also present exciting opportunities for future research and discovery.
