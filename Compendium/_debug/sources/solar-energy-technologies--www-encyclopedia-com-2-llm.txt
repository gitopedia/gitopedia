There are two main types of solar hot water systems: active and passive. Active systems use pumps and other mechanical devices to circulate water through a collector and storage tank, while passive systems rely on the natural convection of heated water.

Active solar hot water systems typically consist of a solar collector, which is usually a flat-plate or evacuated tube collector that absorbs sunlight and heats up the water in a storage tank. The heated water can then be used for various household needs. Solar collectors come in different sizes and shapes depending on their efficiency and capacity to heat water.

Passive solar hot water systems, also known as thermosiphon systems, rely on natural convection of heated water to circulate it through the system. In a thermosiphon system, the storage tank is placed above the collector, and the two are connected by a short pipe or tube that allows the heated water to rise up and enter the top of the storage tank. The cooler water then flows back down into the bottom of the collector, where it is reheated by the sun. This process continues until all the hot water in the system has been used for household needs, at which point the thermosiphon effect stops, and the cycle starts again when the next batch of hot water is needed.

Both active and passive solar hot water systems can be combined with other renewable energy sources such as wind or geothermal to further reduce a home's dependence on fossil fuels. Additionally, some manufacturers offer hybrid solar hot water systems that combine both active and passive technologies for maximum efficiency and cost-effectiveness.
