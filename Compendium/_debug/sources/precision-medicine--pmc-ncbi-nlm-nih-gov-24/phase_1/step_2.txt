  "summary": "### **Precision and Personalized Medicine: Definitions and Models**\nPrecision and personalized medicine are often used interchangeably but reflect distinct conceptual frameworks. **Precision medicine** focuses on tailoring healthcare based on individual genetic, environmental, and lifestyle factors, often leveraging advanced technologies like genomics and data analytics (National Human Genome Research Institute, 2022). **Personalized medicine** emphasizes individualized care, integrating clinical, biological, and social data to optimize treatment and prevention strategies (National Library of Medicine, 2019). Both concepts implicitly reflect the **biomedical model of health**, which prioritizes biological mechanisms over social and environmental determinants (Yuill et al., 2010). However, critics argue that this narrow focus overlooks the **\"embodiment dynamic\"**—how social inequalities and environmental exposures shape health outcomes through biological pathways (Kelly-Irving & Delpierre, 2021). \n\n---\n\n### **Why a Broader Vision of Health is Critical**\nDeveloping personalized medicine and health based on a **broader vision** is essential to address health inequities and the complex interplay between **exogenous** (e.g., pollution, socioeconomic status) and **endogenous** (e.g., genetic predispositions, psychosocial stress) factors (Krieger, 2005). Key arguments include: \n1. **The Exposome Concept**: The **exposome**—the totality of environmental exposures across the lifespan—highlights how non-genetic factors (e.g., air pollution, trauma) interact with biology to influence disease (Wild, 2005). For example, adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) are linked to epigenetic changes and accelerated telomere shortening, increasing risks for chronic diseases (Lang et al., 2020). \n2. **Social Determinants of Health**: Structural inequalities (e.g., racism, poverty) contribute to disparities in health outcomes. Psychosocial stress from racial discrimination, for instance, is associated with accelerated epigenetic aging (Lim et al., 2022). \n3. **Integration of \"Personomics\"**: Ziegelstein (2017) proposes \"personomics\" to integrate social, behavioral, and biological data for personalized healthcare. \n\n---\n\n### **Specificities of Current Personalized Medicine and Implications**\nCurrent personalized medicine models face challenges in scalability, ethical considerations, and resource allocation. The integration of social determinants requires rethinking healthcare systems to address disparities. \n\n---\n\n### **Data Availability Statement**\nThis section addresses the availability of data and references for further study. \n\n---\n\n### **Conclusion**\nA holistic approach combining biological, social, and environmental factors is critical for advancing personalized medicine and achieving equitable healthcare.",
