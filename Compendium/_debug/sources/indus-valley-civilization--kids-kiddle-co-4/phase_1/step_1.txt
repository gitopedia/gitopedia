**Overview of the Indus Valley Civilization**  
The Indus Valley Civilization (c. 3300–1300 BCE) was one of the world’s earliest urban cultures, flourishing in present-day Pakistan and northwest India. Known for its advanced city planning, trade networks, and cultural achievements, it left a lasting legacy in South Asian history.  

---

### **Key Features**  
1. **Urban Planning**  
   - Cities like **Mohenjo-daro** and **Dholavira** featured grid layouts, multi-story buildings, and advanced drainage systems.  
   - Public baths, granaries, and standardized weights and measures indicate a highly organized society.  

2. **Trade Networks**  
   - Traded with Mesopotamia, Central Asia, and the Arabian Peninsula via bullock carts and boats.  
   - Exchanged goods like pottery, seals, and textiles, with evidence of sea trade (e.g., artifacts found in Oman).  

3. **Art and Craftsmanship**  
   - Created terracotta figurines, bronze sculptures (e.g., the *Dancing Girl*), and intricate seals with animal motifs.  
   - Produced standardized weights, dice, and toys, showcasing technical skill.  

4. **Writing System**  
   - Used the *Indus script*, a set of symbols found on seals and pottery. Despite efforts, it remains undeciphered, leaving the language a mystery.  

5. **Religion and Beliefs**  
   - Evidence suggests reverence for water (e.g., the *Great Bath*), possible deities (e.g., the *Pashupati Seal* figure), and animal symbolism.  
   - No large temples or palaces were found, hinting at decentralized religious practices.  

---

### **Cultural Contributions**  
- **Agriculture**: Pioneered multi-cropping (summer and winter crops) and rice farming.  
- **Hydraulics**: Advanced water management systems for irrigation and sanitation.  
- **Legacy**: Elements of their culture influenced later societies, including the Vedic tradition.  

---

### **Decline and Legacy**  
- Around 1900 BCE, cities were abandoned, likely due to climate shifts (droughts, weakened monsoons) and social changes.  
- Regional cultures like the *Cemetery H* tradition emerged, preserving some Harappan practices.  
- The civilization’s innovations in urban planning, trade, and art continue to inspire modern studies.
