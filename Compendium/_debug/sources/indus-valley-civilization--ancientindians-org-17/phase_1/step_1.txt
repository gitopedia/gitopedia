**Indus Valley Civilization: Comprehensive Overview**  

---

### **1. Introduction and Key Facts**  
- **Alternative Name**: Indus-Sarasvati Civilization.  
- **Time Period**: Flourished from approximately **8,000 BCE to 1,500 BCE**, with the decline attributed to climate change, environmental degradation, and the drying up of the Sarasvati River.  
- **Geographic Range**: Spanned **1.25 million square kilometers**, covering parts of modern-day **Pakistan, India, and Afghanistan**, making it larger than the combined territories of Egypt and Mesopotamia.  
- **Oldest Site**: **Bhirrana** in Haryana, India, is the earliest known Harappan site, dating to **7,570–6,200 BCE**.  

---

### **2. Major Sites and Urban Planning**  
- **Key Cities**:  
  - **Mohenjo-Daro** and **Harappa** (core cities).  
  - **Dholavira** (in Gujarat), **Lothal** (coastal), and **Rakhigarhi** (largest site in India).  
- **Urban Design**:  
  - **Grid Layout**: Cities were planned with straight streets and drainage systems.  
  - **Citadels**: Elevated areas (e.g., Mohenjo-Daro’s Great Bath) served administrative or ceremonial purposes.  
  - **Residential Zones**: Multi-story buildings with courtyards and wells.  
  - **Materials**: Use of baked bricks for construction, indicating advanced engineering.  

---

### **3. Infrastructure and Technology**  
- **Drainage System**:  
  - **Covered drains** with brick-lined channels, some with **sewage disposal** and **waste treatment**.  
  - **Water Management**: Reservoirs, wells, and sophisticated water distribution systems.  
- **Weights and Measures**: Standardized systems for trade, suggesting a centralized economy.  
- **Trade Networks**:  
  - **Maritime Trade**: Connected to Mesopotamia (via the Arabian Sea), the Red Sea, and the Persian Gulf.  
  - **Goods Exchanged**: Jade (from China), cedar wood (from Himalayas), lapis lazuli (from Afghanistan), and terracotta ware.  
  - **Lothal**: Identified as a **dockyard**, indicating maritime activity.  

---

### **4. Economy and Daily Life**  
- **Agriculture**:  
  - Cultivation of **wheat, barley, millet, and cotton**.  
  - Evidence of **rice domestication** (pushed back by 3,000 years).  
- **Craftsmanship**:  
  - Pottery, metallurgy (copper, bronze), and bead-making.  
  - **Seals**: Used for trade and identification, often depicting animals (bulls, elephants) and symbols.  
- **Social Structure**:  
  - Evidence of **class distinctions** (e.g., burial goods in Harappa’s cemetery).  
  - **Peaceful Society**: No weapons or fortifications found, suggesting a focus on trade and urban life.  

---

### **5. Religion and Beliefs**  
- **Deities**:  
  - **Mother Goddess** (Shakti), linked to the **female cosmic energy** still worshipped in Hinduism.  
  - **Shiva-Linga**: Lingam structures in Mohenjo-Daro and Harappa suggest early worship of Shiva.  
  - **Pashupati Seal**: Depicted a proto-Shiva with three horns, later evolving into the trident.  
- **Sacred Symbols**:  
  - **Swastika** (found at Mohenjo-Daro and Dholavira).  
  - **Peepal Tree** and **bull** as sacred symbols.  
- **Fire Rituals**:  
  - Fire altars at **Kalibangan** indicate early Vedic practices, challenging the theory that Vedic religion originated outside India.  

---

### **6. Funerary Practices**  
- **Methods of Disposal**:  
  1. **Complete Burial**: Graves or pits.  
  2. **Cremation**: Ashes interred or scattered.  
  3. **Partial Burial**: Bones placed in jars or directly in the ground.  
  4. **Post-Cremation Burial**: Ashes mixed with **terracotta figurines, shells, and beads** in burial pots.  
- **Evidence**:  
  - Over **126 post-cremation pots** found at Harappa, often containing animal bones and jewelry.  
  - **Human remains** in some pots suggest ash was dispersed into rivers or mixed with charcoal.  

---

### **7. Decline and Legacy**  
- **Factors Contributing to Decline**:  
  - **Climate Change**: Drying of the Sarasvati River.  
  - **Environmental Degradation**: Soil salinity and overuse of resources.  
  - **Social Disruption**: Possible shifts in trade routes or internal strife.  
- **Legacy**:  
  - **Hinduism’s Roots**: The Indus religion is considered ancestral to Hinduism, with shared symbols and practices.  
  - **Cultural Influence**: Art, architecture, and trade networks shaped South Asian civilization.  

---

### **8. Notable Discoveries and Scholars**  
- **Key Sites**:  
  - **Bhirrana** (oldest Harappan site), **Rakhigarhi** (largest), **Lothal** (dockyard), and **Dholavira** (water management).  
- **Scholars**:  
  - **John Marshall** (Mohenjo-Daro excavations), **M.S. Vats** (Harappa), **Sir Mortimer Wheeler** (Indus Civilization studies).  
- **Modern Research**:  
  - **Bates et al. (2017)**: Evidence of early rice domestication in the Indus region.  
  - **Fire Altars at Kalibangan**: Suggest early Vedic rituals.  

---

### **9. Cultural and Historical Significance**  
- **First Dockyard**: Lothal’s dockyard is the world’s earliest known.  
- **Peaceful Society**: No evidence of warfare, emphasizing trade and urban planning.  
- **Global Connections**: Trade links with Mesopotamia, Afghanistan, and China.
