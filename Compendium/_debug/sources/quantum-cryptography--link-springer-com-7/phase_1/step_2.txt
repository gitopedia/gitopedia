- Quantum cryptography applies principles of quantum physics to the design of cryptographic primitives.

- Quantum Cryptography was born in the early 1970s when Stephen Wiesner wrote “Conjugate Coding,” which unfortunately took more than 10 years to see the light of print (Wiesner 1983).
- Bennett (who knew of Wiesner’s idea) and Gilles Brassard picked up the subject and brought it to fruition in a series of papers that culminated with the demonstration of an experimental prototype that established the technological feasibility of the concept (Bennett et al. 1992a).

- Quantum cryptographic systems take advantage of Heisenberg’s uncertainty relations, according to which measuring a quantum system, in general, disturbs it and yields incomplete information about its state before the measurement.
- Eavesdropping on a quantum communication channel therefore causes an unavoidable disturbance, alerting the legitimate users.
