Solar energy is the energy received by the Earth from the Sun that is converted into useful forms of energy for various purposes. This includes solar thermal or solar photovoltaic (PV) energy. The Sun is our primary source of energy and it has been harnessed by humans since ancient times, initially in the form of sunlight through photosynthesis by plants and later as a source of heat through solar heaters.

Solar energy is considered a renewable resource because the energy from the sun is constantly available and can be harnessed efficiently without depleting natural resources or producing greenhouse gases. It has become an increasingly popular alternative to fossil fuels, contributing significantly to global energy production and consumption. The use of solar energy helps reduce dependence on non-renewable sources like coal and oil while mitigating the adverse effects of climate change.

Solar energy can be harnessed in various forms:
1. Solar thermal energy: This involves converting sunlight into heat through the use of solar collectors, which are typically made up of a series of mirrors or lenses that focus sunlight onto a small area, such as a receiver tube containing a fluid. The heated fluid is then used to generate steam for power generation in a conventional power plant or directly for heating purposes like space and water heating systems.
2. Solar photovoltaic (PV) energy: This involves converting sunlight into electricity through the use of solar panels made up of silicon-based semiconductors, such as monocrystalline, polycrystalline, and amorphous silicon. When sunlight hits these materials, it excites electrons in the semiconductor material, creating an electric current that can be used to power homes or businesses directly or fed into the grid for distribution.
3. Solar hydrogen energy: This involves using solar energy to split water molecules (H2O) into hydrogen and oxygen gases through a process called electrolysis. The produced hydrogen gas can then be stored and utilized as an alternative fuel source, either by burning it in internal combustion engines or by combining it with other fuels like natural gas for more efficient power generation.
4. Solar air heating: This involves using solar energy to heat the air, which is used for space heating, water heating, and industrial processes such as drying of materials. The process typically uses a collector that absorbs sunlight and transfers this energy to a fluid in a closed loop system, increasing its temperature before it's used for various applications.
5. Solar cooling: This involves using solar energy to cool buildings or other spaces by transferring heat from the building into the surrounding environment through the use of solar collectors. The process is typically more efficient and cost-effective than traditional air conditioning systems as it utilizes free, renewable energy sources.
6. Concentrated solar power (CSP): This involves utilizing large areas of mirrors or lenses to focus sunlight onto a small area where heat can be collected for conversion into electricity through conventional power plants that use steam turbines. CSP is considered an efficient and cost-effective way of harnessing solar energy, especially in regions with high levels of direct sunlight.
7. Solar biomass gasification: This involves using solar energy to convert organic waste materials like wood chips or agricultural residues into a combustible gas (syngas) through the process of gasification. The syngas can then be used as an alternative fuel source for power generation, heating, and other applications.
8. Solar desalination: This involves utilizing solar energy to evaporate seawater or brackish water using solar-powered distillation systems. Evaporated water is collected and treated before being utilized for various purposes such as drinking water supply in remote locations with limited access to fresh water sources.
9. Solar air conditioning: This involves using solar energy to cool buildings by transferring heat from the building into the surrounding environment through a closed loop system that utilizes solar collectors. The process can be more efficient and cost-effective than traditional air conditioning systems as it relies on free, renewable energy sources.
