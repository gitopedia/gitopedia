The **Indus Valley Civilization (IVC)**, also known as the **Harappan Civilization**, was one of the world's earliest urban cultures, flourishing around **2500 BCE to 1900 BCE** in **northwestern South Asia** (modern-day **Pakistan** and **northwest India**). Here's a structured summary of its key aspects, based on the article:

---

### **1. Key Features of the IVC**
- **Urban Planning**: Cities like **Harappa** and **Mohenjo-Daro** were meticulously planned with grid layouts, wide streets, and advanced drainage systems.
- **Architecture**: Buildings were made of standardized baked bricks, with multi-story structures, public baths, and granaries.
- **Social Organization**: Evidence suggests a well-organized society with a focus on trade, craftsmanship, and communal living.

---

### **2. Major Cities**
- **Harappa** and **Mohenjo-Daro** (Pakistan): Iconic examples of urban planning.
- **Lothal** (India): A major port city with a dockyard, indicating maritime trade.
- **Dholavira** (India): Known for its advanced water management systems.
- **Kalibangan** (India): Revealed early agricultural practices and a Harappan phase.

---

### **3. Discovery and Pioneers**
- **Sir John Marshall** (1920s): Led the initial excavations at Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro, uncovering the civilization's ruins.
- **Mortimer Wheeler** and **Gregory Possehl**: Contributed to understanding the decline and economic aspects of the IVC.

---

### **4. Economy**
- **Agriculture**: Cultivated wheat, barley, and cotton, with irrigation systems.
- **Trade**: Engaged in extensive trade with **Mesopotamia**, exporting goods like textiles, beads, and pottery.
- **Crafts**: Skilled in metallurgy (copper, bronze), pottery, and bead-making.

---

### **5. Decline Theories**
- **Environmental Factors**: Climate changes, river shifts (e.g., drying of the Indus River), and prolonged droughts.
- **Human Activity**: Deforestation and overuse of resources may have degraded the environment.
- **Invasions**: Early theories (e.g., Wheeler’s invasion hypothesis) are now disputed, with environmental factors gaining more support.

---

### **6. Technological Advancements**
- **Urban Planning**: Grid layouts, drainage systems, and water management.
- **Standardized Weights and Measures**: Suggests a centralized system of trade.
- **Metallurgy**: Use of copper, bronze, and early iron tools.
- **Craftsmanship**: Advanced bead-making, pottery, and textiles.

---

### **7. Undeciphered Script**
- The **Indus script** (around 400 symbols) remains undeciphered, with debates about whether it represents a language, religious symbols, or administrative codes.
- **Asko Parpola** argues it may be a **Dravidian language**, while others suggest it could be non-linguistic.

---

### **8. Areas for Further Research**
- **Deciphering the Indus script** to unlock insights into their culture and administration.
- **Exploring lesser-known sites** to understand regional variations and interactions with neighboring cultures.
- **Technological advancements** (e.g., satellite imaging, GIS, and radiocarbon dating) to refine timelines and uncover new evidence.

---

### **Summary of Key Questions and Answers**
- **What is the IVC?**  
  A Bronze Age civilization (2500–1900 BCE) known for its urban planning, trade, and advanced technology in the Indus River basin.

- **Where was it located?**  
  Modern-day **Pakistan** and **northwest India**, with key sites like Harappa, Mohenjo-Daro, Lothal, and Dholavira.

- **Who discovered it?**  
  **Sir John Marshall** led early excavations in the 1920s.

- **What caused its decline?**  
  Likely a combination of environmental changes (e.g., river shifts, droughts), overexploitation of resources, and societal factors.

- **What was its economy like?**  
  Diverse, including agriculture, trade with Mesopotamia, and crafts like pottery and metallurgy.

- **What technological advancements did it have?**  
  Advanced urban planning, drainage systems, standardized weights, and metallurgy.

- **What remains to be studied?**  
  Deciphering the script, exploring new sites, and understanding the civilization’s decline.

---

This civilization remains a cornerstone of ancient history, offering profound insights into early urban societies and their complexities. Continued research and technological innovation promise to reveal even more about this remarkable culture.
