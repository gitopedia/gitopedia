## Overview
Osmosis is a fundamental biological and physical process defined as the movement of a solvent, typically water, through a selectively permeable membrane into an area of higher solute concentration. This process aims to equalize solute concentrations on both sides of the membrane. Osmosis plays a critical role in maintaining balance at cellular and macroscopic levels, essential for survival across various biological systems.


## Overview

Osmosis is a process in which solvent molecules, typically water, move through a semipermeable membrane from an area of lower solute concentration to one of higher solute concentration. ## Aquaporins and Water Transport

Osmosis involves the movement of water molecules through aquaporins—specialized channel proteins that facilitate rapid water transport across cell membranes. These proteins are essential for processes such as water regulation in tissues like kidney cells, ensuring efficient fluid exchange and maintaining cellular homeostasis.

## Types of Osmotic Solutions

- **Isotonic Solution**: The solute concentration is equal on both sides of the membrane, resulting in no net water movement. Example: A cell with 75% water and 25% salt placed in a solution with the same concentrations.
- **Hypotonic Solution**: Lower solute concentration outside the cell causes water to flow inward, leading to cell swelling. Example: Skin cells pruney after swimming.
- **Hypertonic Solution**: Higher solute concentration outside the cell results in water moving outward, causing cell shrinkage. Example: Plant cells losing water in a salt-heavy environment.

### Practical Importance of Osmosis

#### For Plants
Osmosis is crucial for water uptake by plant roots and maintaining turgidity, which helps plants resist environmental stress. It also regulates stomatal function.

#### For Animals (Humans)
Osmosis maintains water balance between blood and tissues and is essential in kidney function for urine production. It ensures solute concentration balance throughout the body.
